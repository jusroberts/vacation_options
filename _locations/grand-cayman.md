## Flights

### Route & Schedule

Air Canada operates non-stop service between Toronto Pearson (YYZ) and Owen Roberts International Airport (GCM) in Grand Cayman. In late April 2026, Air Canada typically operates this route **2–3 times per week** during the tail end of the winter sun season. Frequency varies — verify the exact schedule at time of booking as some seasonal flights transition to summer schedules in late April.

**Flight duration:** Approximately **4 hours 30 minutes** (YYZ to GCM). Return is similar.

**Terminal at YYZ:** All Air Canada flights depart from **Terminal 1** at Toronto Pearson International Airport.

**Estimated round-trip cost (2 passengers, economy):**
- Economy fares on YYZ–GCM typically range from approximately **CAD $700–$1,100/person** one-way in late April, making a round trip approximately **CAD $1,400–$2,200 per person** all-in with taxes.
- For 2 passengers: budget approximately **CAD $2,800–$4,400 total** for economy round-trip flights in late April.
- Late April is the tail end of peak season in Grand Cayman; fares are higher than the Caribbean average but begin softening compared to January–March. Book 6–8 weeks in advance for the best fares.
- WestJet and Cayman Airways also serve YYZ–GCM and are worth comparing for price. Cayman Airways, the island's national carrier, occasionally offers competitive fares and good baggage allowances.

## Hotels

Grand Cayman is one of the most expensive resort destinations in the Caribbean — a function of its status as a British Overseas Territory with a high cost of living, its offshore financial sector wealth, and the premium positioning of its resort corridor along Seven Mile Beach. This is not a budget destination. What it offers in return is world-class beach quality, outstanding water clarity for diving and snorkelling, luxury resort infrastructure, and a remarkably safe, English-speaking environment.

**Expect prices broadly comparable to Turks & Caicos or the premium end of Aruba.** There is no large all-inclusive mass-market product here the way there is in Cuba or the Dominican Republic. Most hotels are room-only or European Plan with à la carte dining.

### 1. The Ritz-Carlton, Grand Cayman
- **Area:** Seven Mile Beach, central corridor
- **Category:** 5-star luxury; one of the most acclaimed Caribbean resorts
- **Price range (5 nights, 2 people):** Approximately **USD $4,500–$10,000+** depending on room category and season

Ritz Kids club.
- **Best for:** Couples for whom no-compromise luxury service, exceptional dining, and a perfect Seven Mile Beach location are the priority. The gold standard on the island.
- **Note:** Not all-inclusive. Meals and activities are additional — budget USD $200–$400/day for food and beverage.

### 2. Kimpton Seafire Resort + Spa
- **Area:** Seven Mile Beach (north end, quieter stretch)
- **Category:** 5-star boutique luxury; adults-preferred atmosphere
- **Price range (5 nights, 2 people):** Approximately **USD $4,000–$8,000** depending on room category
- **Key amenities:** Stunning design-forward property; adults-oriented atmosphere (children permitted but vibe skews couples); large pool with swim-up bar, private beach, full-service spa, multiple restaurants including Ave (contemporary Caribbean), Coccoloba restaurant, and an excellent beach bar, complimentary yoga and fitness classes, watersports, and one of the best wine programs on the island. IHG loyalty program.
- **Best for:** Design-conscious couples seeking a more boutique experience than the Ritz-Carlton with equally strong food and service; slightly lower price point for comparable quality. The Kimpton brand's casual-luxe service philosophy suits many couples better than the Ritz's formality.
- **Note:** Not all-inclusive. À la carte dining throughout.

### 3. Grand Cayman Marriott Beach Resort
- **Area:** Seven Mile Beach, central
- **Category:** 4-star full-service resort
- **Price range (5 nights, 2 people):** Approximately **USD $2,500–$5,000** depending on room type

- **Best for:** Couples who want a reliable, full-service beach resort with a central Seven Mile Beach location at a somewhat more accessible price point than the Ritz or Kimpton; good for Marriott Bonvoy members earning or redeeming points.
- **Note:** Not all-inclusive. Food and beverage are additional. The hotel's central location makes nearby restaurants easily walkable.

### 4. The Westin Grand Cayman Seven Mile Beach Resort & Spa

- **Best for:** Couples wanting a reliable large-resort experience with Westin's well-known wellness brand (Heavenly Beds, spa, fitness amenities) on Seven Mile Beach. Larger and more family-oriented than Kimpton but generally well-maintained.

### 6. Turtle Nest Inn & Condos

- **Best for:** Couples seeking seclusion, local character, and significant savings over the tourist strip; particularly suitable if you have a rental car and prefer an independent, non-resort experience. The property has genuine charm that larger resorts cannot replicate.
- **Note:** Not on a sandy beach (south coast is rocky limestone). A rental car is essentially required from this property.

> **Booking tip:** Grand Cayman resorts rarely appear in Canadian mass-market all-inclusive packages. Most bookings are made directly or through luxury travel agents. **Marriott Bonvoy, IHG Rewards, and Ritz-Carlton Rewards** members should book through the loyalty program portals for the best rate and benefit protections. Air Canada Vacations occasionally offers GCM packages and is worth checking. Book 3–5 months in advance for peak season room types at the Ritz or Kimpton.

## Dining

Grand Cayman has one of the most sophisticated restaurant scenes in the Caribbean — driven by the island's affluent population of expat financial professionals and the high-end resort clientele along Seven Mile Beach. The concentration of genuinely world-class restaurants per square kilometre rivals destinations many times larger.

**Local specialties:** Turtle stew is the traditional Caymanian dish — the Cayman Turtle Centre raises green sea turtles partly for this purpose. Conch fritters, fresh snapper, lobster (in season), and lionfish (an invasive species that local chefs have embraced as a culinary initiative) are the ingredients to seek out. Local rum cake is the quintessential souvenir edible.

**Budget note:** Dining in Grand Cayman is expensive. Expect USD $30–$70/person for a sit-down lunch, USD $50–$120/person for dinner at a mid-range restaurant, and USD $100–$200+/person at the finest establishments. Alcohol prices are high. Budget accordingly, especially if not staying at an all-inclusive property.

### Restaurants

**1. Blue by Eric Ripert**
- **Cuisine:** Contemporary seafood / fine dining
- **Price range:** ~USD $100–$200/person for dinner (tasting menu available)
- **Location:** The Ritz-Carlton, Grand Cayman
- **Known for:** Consistently rated the best restaurant in Grand Cayman and one of the finest in the entire Caribbean. Chef Eric Ripert — of the legendary Le Bernardin in New York City — designed the menu, which features impeccably prepared seafood in an elegant beachfront setting. The tasting menu with wine pairing is the full experience; it is a splurge worth making for one evening. Reservations are essential and book out weeks in advance. If you are going to spend money on one dinner in Grand Cayman, this is it.

**2. Luca**
- **Cuisine:** Italian seafood / Mediterranean
- **Price range:** ~USD $60–$120/person for dinner
- **Location:** Seven Mile Beach (Caribbean Club)
- **Known for:** One of the most consistently praised restaurants on the island. Beautiful terrace setting overlooking the beach; impeccable pasta, fresh seafood, and an extensive Italian wine list. Beloved by both local expats and visitors. The lobster risotto and fresh fish preparations are exceptional. Reservations required.

**3. Calypso Grill**
- **Cuisine:** Caribbean-inspired contemporary seafood
- **Price range:** ~USD $50–$100/person for dinner
- **Location:** Morgan's Harbour, West Bay (20 minutes from Seven Mile Beach)
- **Known for:** Housed in a converted caboose overlooking Morgan's Harbour, Calypso Grill is one of Grand Cayman's most storied restaurants. The seafood — fresh local catch, conch fritters, lionfish, Caribbean lobster — is simply prepared and excellent. The harbour setting at sunset is genuinely beautiful. A local institution favoured by expats and in-the-know visitors; less visible to tourists than the hotel-strip restaurants but consistently excellent.

**4. Ristorante Pappagallo**
- **Cuisine:** Northern Italian; upscale
- **Price range:** ~USD $60–$110/person for dinner
- **Location:** Barker's, West Bay (near the North Sound lagoon)
- **Known for:** Set dramatically over a lagoon with tropical birds roosting in the grounds at night, Pappagallo is one of the most romantic restaurant settings in Grand Cayman. The Italian menu — fresh pasta, risotto, veal, and excellent seafood — is refined and consistent. The combination of the setting and cuisine makes it a strong choice for a special evening. Reservations essential.

**5. Tukka**
- **Cuisine:** Australian-Caribbean fusion; casual fine dining
- **Price range:** ~USD $40–$80/person for dinner
- **Location:** East End (far east coast, 40 minutes from Seven Mile Beach)
- **Known for:** A destination restaurant at the quiet east end of the island, Tukka serves inventive fusion cuisine with Caymanian, Australian, and Asian influences. Alligator tail, lionfish, curried conch, and ostrich have all appeared on the menu. The setting — a breezy wooden deck overlooking the ironshore coast — is outstanding, and the drive through the island to get there is half the experience. Worth planning as a full-evening excursion.

**6. Cayman Cabana**
- **Cuisine:** Caribbean; casual beachfront
- **Price range:** ~USD $20–$50/person
- **Location:** Seven Mile Beach public beach (north end)
- **Known for:** One of the few genuinely casual, affordable options right on Seven Mile Beach. Jerk chicken, fish tacos, conch fritters, and frozen rum cocktails in a relaxed open-air setting. A good option for a casual beach lunch or early dinner without the white-tablecloth price tag.

**7. The Brasserie**
- **Cuisine:** Contemporary international; farm-to-table focused
- **Price range:** ~USD $50–$100/person for dinner
- **Location:** Cricket Square, George Town (commercial district)
- **Known for:** Uses Cayman Islands' own 7 Mile Brewery beer and locally sourced ingredients. The Brasserie is popular with the expat business community and offers a more urban, less resort-oriented dining experience. The menu changes regularly; consistently strong execution. Good for a lunch in George Town combined with sightseeing.

**8. Cayman Turtle Centre Café**
- **Cuisine:** Caymanian; casual
- **Price range:** ~USD $15–$30/person
- **Location:** Cayman Turtle Centre, West Bay
- **Known for:** The only place on the island to legally try traditional turtle stew — made from the green sea turtles raised at the centre's farm. This is an authentically local dish with deep cultural roots, though its ethical dimensions are worth considering before ordering. Also serves standard Caymanian comfort food. The centre itself is the main attraction; the café is a functional add-on rather than a destination for dining.

## Transportation

### GCM Airport to Hotel

Owen Roberts International Airport (GCM) is located in **George Town**, approximately **5–10 km from the main Seven Mile Beach resort corridor** — a 10–15 minute drive.

**Taxis:**
- Taxis at GCM operate on **government-regulated fixed fares** by zone — no meters. The rate from the airport to the Seven Mile Beach resort area is approximately **USD $25–$35 per vehicle** for a standard taxi; SUV/minivan taxis charge slightly more.
- Taxis hold 3–4 passengers. For two people, a taxi is the most convenient arrival option.
- **Important:** Always confirm the fare before departing. Carry USD cash — many Cayman taxis do not accept credit cards.

**Resort transfers:**
- Many luxury resorts (particularly the Ritz-Carlton) offer pre-arranged private airport transfers. Confirm at booking — this is often worth arranging for a seamless arrival with luggage.

**Note:** There is no public bus service to/from GCM airport. Uber and rideshare apps do not operate in the Cayman Islands.

### Getting Around Grand Cayman

**Taxis:**
The primary transport option for visitors without a rental car. Fares are set by government rate card:
- Seven Mile Beach to George Town: ~USD $15–$20
- Seven Mile Beach to West Bay: ~USD $15–$25
- Seven Mile Beach to East End: ~USD $40–$60
- Budget approximately **USD $20–$40 per taxi ride** as a baseline for longer trips. Tipping 15% is standard.
- Taxis cannot be hailed on the street generally — have your hotel arrange them, or call ahead.

**Rental Cars:**
- Car rental is available from major agencies at GCM airport and from in-town operators.
- **Critical: Grand Cayman drives on the LEFT side of the road** (British territory). North American visitors find this disorienting — allow time to acclimatize, drive slowly, and take special care at roundabouts.
- Rates: approximately **USD $50–$90/day** for a compact or midsize in late April.
- A rental car gives you access to the East End (Tukka restaurant, Rum Point, Starfish Point), Bodden Town (historic capital), and West Bay (Cayman Turtle Centre, Morgan's Harbour, Calypso Grill) independently. For couples planning multiple day excursions, a car pays for itself quickly in avoided taxi fares.
- A valid Canadian driver's licence is accepted. Minimum age 21 at most agencies; under-25 surcharges apply at some.
- Roads are generally well-maintained. The island is small enough (35 km long) to cross end-to-end in under an hour.

**Public Buses:**
- A limited public minibus network (CINICO Bus Service and independent operators) runs along the West Bay Road / Seven Mile Beach corridor and into George Town. Fares are CI$2 / ~USD $2.40 per ride.
- Useful for moving between the hotel strip and George Town but not reliable for reaching West Bay, East End, or Rum Point.

**Water Taxis and Boats:**
- Stingray City (North Sound sandbar) is accessed exclusively by boat from multiple departure points including the Hyatt Pier, Royal Palms Beach Club, and Morgan's Harbour. Tours are approximately USD $45–$65/person for a half-day excursion including snorkelling.
- Rum Point (a calm, shallow bay on the north coast) is accessible by car (~40 min from Seven Mile Beach) or by a scenic 25-minute water taxi (operated by Red Sail Sports from the Hyatt Pier area) — approximately USD $20/person round trip.

## Attractions

### 1. Seven Mile Beach
- **Hours:** Always accessible; public beaches throughout
- **Admission:** Free
- **Description:** One of the finest beaches in the Western Hemisphere and Grand Cayman's defining attraction. The beach runs approximately 7 miles (though closer to 5.5 miles of continuous sand) along the calm west coast of the island, sheltered from the Atlantic by the island itself. The sand is white and fine, the water a brilliant clear turquoise, and the bottom sandy to depths of 2–3 metres near shore. Water temperature in late April is approximately **27°C (80°F)**. The beach is public by law for its entire length; resort properties cannot restrict access to the shoreline.
- **Tip:** The northern stretch (near the Kimpton and quieter public areas) is less crowded than the central resort zone. The beach is at its most stunning from about 4 PM onward when the angle of the light turns the water vivid blue-green.

### 2. Stingray City (Grand Cayman's Most Famous Attraction)
- **Hours:** Boat tours depart daily, typically 8 AM–2 PM; morning tours preferred
- **Admission:** ~USD $45–$65/person for a half-day boat tour including snorkelling
- **Description:** A shallow sandbar in the North Sound where dozens of **southern stingrays** have congregated for over 40 years, originally drawn by fishermen cleaning their catch. The rays are large (wingspan up to 1.5 m), completely accustomed to human contact, and can be fed and handled under guide supervision. You can stand in waist-deep water and interact with rays that glide around your legs, or snorkel among them just offshore. It is genuinely extraordinary and unlike anything available at most beach destinations. One of the most visited marine attractions in the Caribbean for good reason.
- **Operators:** Red Sail Sports, Fantasea Divers, Captain Marvin's, Native Way Watersports, and many others offer regular departures. Most tour operators combine Stingray City with snorkelling at the Coral Garden reef and a stop at the Barrier Reef — a full half-day.
- **Tip:** Book in advance in late April — tours fill quickly. Morning tours have calmer water and better visibility. The sandbar is accessible to all; no swimming ability required.

### 3. Scuba Diving and Snorkelling — The Cayman Wall
- **Hours:** Dive operators depart 8–9 AM typically
- **Admission:** Snorkelling tours ~USD $40–$70/person; 2-tank dive ~USD $100–$150/person
- **Description:** Grand Cayman sits at the edge of the **Cayman Trench**, and the island's underwater topography is dramatic — the shallow reef drops precipitously to oceanic depths along the Cayman Wall, one of the premier wall dives on earth. Visibility regularly exceeds **30 metres (100 feet)**. The waters around Grand Cayman are home to sea turtles (very commonly encountered), eagle rays, nurse sharks, reef sharks, giant green moray eels, and extraordinary coral formations. The USS Kittiwake — a 76-metre US Navy submarine rescue vessel intentionally sunk in 2011 — is one of the best wreck dives in the Caribbean, accessible to snorkellers and divers alike.
- **Operators:** Divers Down, Eden Rock Dive Centre (George Town — can walk in from the harbour; the Eden Rock reef is excellent for easy shore diving), Red Sail Sports, Don Foster's Dive Cayman, and many others. Grand Cayman has more PADI dive operations per kilometre of coastline than almost anywhere in the world.
- **Tip:** Even if you have never dived before, an introductory "discover scuba" experience with a certified instructor is available and costs approximately USD $120–$150. The conditions at Grand Cayman — calm water, excellent visibility, abundant marine life — make it an ideal place to try diving for the first time.

### 4. Cayman Turtle Centre
- **Hours:** Daily 8 AM–5 PM
- **Admission:** ~USD $45/adult (USD $35 for children); includes all attractions
- **Description:** The world's only green sea turtle farm, located in West Bay. The centre breeds and raises green sea turtles — a species that was hunted nearly to extinction on the island — for both conservation release and (controversially) limited culinary purposes. Visitors can handle young turtles, swim with adult turtles in a large lagoon, tour the hatchery, see the touch pools, and explore the attached nature park with native bird species, iguanas, and a small aviary. Also includes a water park area and beach access. An interesting half-day combining conservation education, hands-on animal experience, and swimming.
- **Tip:** Combine with lunch at Calypso Grill in nearby Morgan's Harbour (10 minutes away by car) for an efficient West Bay half-day.

### 5. George Town and the National Museum
- **Hours:** Museum: Mon–Fri 9 AM–5 PM, Sat 10 AM–2 PM
- **Admission:** Museum: ~USD $10/adult; George Town is free to walk
- **Description:** Grand Cayman's capital city is small and walkable — a compact grid of pastel-painted colonial-era buildings, duty-free jewellery and liquor shops, banks (the Cayman Islands is the world's fifth-largest banking centre), and excellent seafood restaurants. The **Cayman Islands National Museum** in the historic Court House building covers natural history, maritime heritage, and the history of the Cayman Islands people from indigenous times to the present. George Town is also where cruise ships dock — avoid it on days when multiple cruise ships are in port if you want a quieter experience.

### 6. Rum Point
- **Hours:** Beach accessible at all times; restaurant/bar open daily
- **Admission:** Free (parking charges may apply)
- **Description:** A calm, shallow bay on the north coast of Grand Cayman, opposite the North Sound from George Town. The water here is extraordinarily clear and shallow — perfect for paddling, snorkelling, and kayaking. The beach is wide, white-sand, and backed by sea grape trees providing natural shade. The Rum Point Club restaurant and bar serves the island's famous Mudslide cocktail (said to have been invented here) and good casual food. Far quieter than Seven Mile Beach; a half-day trip by car (~40 min) or water taxi.
- **Tip:** Combine with a stop at **Starfish Point** — a nearby shallow bay where large cushion sea stars (starfish) are abundant in the water. A 10-minute drive from Rum Point.

### 7. Bodden Town and Historical Sites
- **Hours:** Open access
- **Admission:** Free (Pedro St. James historic site ~USD $10)
- **Description:** Bodden Town was the original capital of the Cayman Islands before George Town grew to prominence. It retains a quieter, more authentic character than the tourist-facing west coast. Nearby **Pedro St. James National Historic Site** (often called "the castle") is the oldest standing building in the Cayman Islands — a well-preserved 1780 plantation house that served as the birthplace of democracy in the Caymans, where the decision to abolish slavery was announced. The site includes a museum, video presentation, and ocean views from the hilltop grounds.

### 8. Hell
- **Hours:** Post office: weekdays; the area itself is always accessible
- **Admission:** Free
- **Description:** A small area of black limestone rock formations in West Bay with a genuinely striking, otherworldly appearance — sharp black spires resembling a lunar landscape. It was named "Hell" by the first Caymanians who saw it. The tiny area is a tourist attraction primarily for its novelty (the post office inside sells stamps and postcards postmarked "Hell, Grand Cayman") and for the excellent photographs the formations produce. 15 minutes from Seven Mile Beach by car; worth a quick stop combined with a West Bay excursion.

## Beaches

### Seven Mile Beach
Grand Cayman's signature beach and one of the finest in the Caribbean. Five and a half continuous miles of bright white sand along the calm western shore, with clear turquoise water and a sandy bottom perfect for swimming. Water temperature in late April is approximately **27°C (80°F)**. The beach is lined by the island's main resort corridor but remains public throughout. Late afternoon light turns the water a vivid cobalt-green. It earns its consistent top-10 Caribbean beach rankings.

### Rum Point Beach
A calm, shallow bay on the north coast, accessible by car (~40 min) or water taxi. The water is exceptionally clear and shallow, sandy-bottomed, and perfect for paddling and snorkelling. Backed by sea grape trees providing natural shade. Quieter and less developed than Seven Mile Beach; a popular excursion for couples wanting to escape the resort corridor. The Rum Point Club bar and restaurant is directly on the beach.

### Smith Cove
A small, sheltered cove just south of George Town — one of Grand Cayman's best kept secrets among visitors. Protected by rocks and reef, the cove has calm, clear water ideal for swimming, free snorkelling (sea turtles are regularly seen here), and picnicking. No resort infrastructure; bring your own shade and towels. A favourite of local families and independent travellers. Free to access.

### Starfish Point
Near Rum Point on the north coast — a very shallow, crystal-clear bay where large orange cushion sea stars (starfish) live on the sandy bottom in just 30–60 cm of water. Visitors can wade among them. Not a conventional beach for swimming, but one of the most photogenic and memorable spots on the island. Combine with Rum Point for a full half-day.

### East End Beaches
The eastern tip of Grand Cayman is windswept, rugged, and largely undeveloped. Small public beaches at Colliers Bay and near the East End lighthouse offer a dramatically different character from Seven Mile Beach — more exposure, cleaner waves, fewer people, and excellent snorkelling directly off the ironshore reef. A rental car is required.

## Practical Info

### April Weather

Late April falls at the end of Grand Cayman's dry season, offering near-ideal beach weather with minimal rain and comfortable trade wind conditions.

| Metric | Late April Average |
|---|---|
| Daytime high | 30°C (86°F) |
| Overnight low | 24°C (75°F) |
| Average temperature | 27°C (80°F) |
| Sea temperature | 27°C (80°F) |
| Humidity | ~65–70% |
| Rainfall | ~30 mm over ~4 days |
| Sunshine | ~9 hours/day |
| UV Index | 11 — **Extreme** |

- Late April sits at the edge of the transition between the dry season and the beginning of the wet season (May–October). Expect occasional brief afternoon showers but predominantly sunny and dry conditions.
- Trade winds from the east/northeast keep conditions comfortable on the beach and are excellent for sailing and kitesurfing.
- **UV warning:** UV Index 11 is classified as extreme — unprotected skin can burn in under 15 minutes at midday. Use SPF 50+ and reapply frequently.
- Hurricane season officially begins June 1. Late April carries essentially no hurricane risk.

### Currency

The **Cayman Islands Dollar (CI$)** is the official currency. The exchange rate is fixed: **CI$1 = USD $1.20**. However, in practice, the **US Dollar** is the currency used for virtually all tourist transactions. Hotels, restaurants, dive shops, taxis, tours, and shops all price in USD and accept USD without surcharge.

- **Canadian dollars are not widely accepted.** Exchange CAD to USD before departing Canada.
- **Credit cards** (Visa, Mastercard) are accepted at all hotels, most restaurants, and many shops. Inform your bank before travel.
- **ATMs** are widely available in George Town and along Seven Mile Beach. Most dispense CI$ or USD.
- Budget approximately **USD $200–$400 per couple per day** for food, drinks, activities, and transport, exclusive of accommodation. Grand Cayman is expensive — roughly comparable to Turks & Caicos in overall cost.

### Electricity

- **Voltage:** 120V, 60 Hz — identical to Canada and the United States.
- **Plugs:** Type A and B (same flat-prong plugs used throughout Canada and the US).
- **Canadian visitors need no power adapter.** All Canadian electronics, phone chargers, and appliances work directly.

### Language

English is the official language and is spoken universally. Grand Cayman has a large expatriate population (approximately 50% of residents) drawn by the financial sector; the island is linguistically diverse but English is standard everywhere. No language barrier for Canadian visitors.

### Time Zone

Grand Cayman observes **Eastern Standard Time (EST) year-round — UTC−5** — with no daylight saving time. In late April, Toronto is on EDT (UTC−4). This means Grand Cayman is **1 hour behind Toronto** during late April. A minor consideration for flight timing but effectively no jet lag concern.

### Canada Travel Advisory

As of early 2026, the Government of Canada rates the Cayman Islands at **"Exercise Normal Security Precautions"** — the lowest advisory level. Grand Cayman is considered one of the safest destinations in the Caribbean and among the safest in the world relative to its region. Violent crime against tourists is extremely rare. The primary safety concerns are petty theft and road safety (left-hand traffic).

### Health Notes

- **No mandatory vaccinations** are required for entry to the Cayman Islands for Canadian citizens.
- **Medical facilities:** Grand Cayman has the **Cayman Islands Hospital (Health City Cayman Islands)** — a modern facility by Caribbean standards. The island's financial wealth means healthcare infrastructure exceeds most Caribbean destinations. Serious conditions may still require air evacuation to Miami (~1 hour away), but the probability of requiring this is low.
- **Travel medical insurance is essential.** Canadian provincial health plans provide no coverage outside Canada. Ensure your policy covers emergency medical treatment and evacuation.
- **Sun and heat:** The primary health risk. Apply SPF 50+, wear a hat and UV-blocking sunglasses, stay hydrated, and seek shade during peak midday hours (11 AM–3 PM).
- **Water:** Tap water in Grand Cayman is desalinated seawater and is safe to drink. Bottled water is widely available.

### Tipping Customs

Grand Cayman follows US-style tipping conventions:

| Situation | Suggested Tip |
|---|---|
| Restaurant (sit-down) | 15–20% (verify no automatic service charge first) |
| Resort housekeeper | USD $3–$5/night, left daily |
| Resort porter/bellhop | USD $2–$3 per bag |
| Taxi driver | 15% of fare (cash preferred) |
| Dive guide / boat captain | USD $10–$20/person for a dive or tour |
| Spa therapist | 15–20% |

- Many Grand Cayman restaurants add an automatic 10–15% service charge. Always check the bill before adding a further tip.
- Budget approximately **USD $150–$250 per couple** specifically for gratuities over a 5-night stay.

### Entry Requirements for Canadians

- **Passport:** Valid Canadian passport required. Should be valid for at least 6 months beyond your return date.
- **Visa:** Not required for Canadian citizens for tourism stays.
- **Landing card:** A landing/embarkation card will be distributed on the aircraft or completed at immigration on arrival. Standard procedure; no advance action required.
- **Proof of onward travel:** Cayman immigration officers may ask to see your return ticket. Have your booking confirmation accessible.

### 2SLGBTQI+ Travellers

Grand Cayman's legal and social landscape for LGBTQ+ travellers has been evolving:

- **Legal status:** Same-sex sexual relations were decriminalized in 2000. In 2020, the Cayman Islands Court of Appeal ruled that the government must legally recognize same-sex partnerships; the Cayman Islands legislature subsequently passed the **Domestic Partnership Act in 2020**, providing same-sex couples with legal recognition and certain rights, though full marriage equality has not been legislated.
- **In practice at resorts:** The major luxury resort properties — Ritz-Carlton, Kimpton Seafire, Westin, Marriott — cater to an international clientele and are welcoming and professional to all couples regardless of orientation. Same-sex couples are a common and unremarkable presence along the Seven Mile Beach resort corridor.
- **Outside the resort zone:** Grand Cayman remains a relatively conservative Caribbean community. Public displays of affection between same-sex couples may draw attention outside the resort corridor. Exercising some awareness in local communities is practical.
- The **Canada travel advisory** contains no specific 2SLGBTQI+ safety warnings for the Cayman Islands.
- **Bottom line:** For a beach-focused couples' vacation centred on Seven Mile Beach and resort-area dining and diving, Grand Cayman is a comfortable and welcoming destination. The legal framework for partnership recognition is more developed than most Caribbean jurisdictions.
