# Willemstad, Curaçao (CUR) — Vacation Guide for Couples from Toronto

**Trip profile:** Two adults departing Toronto Pearson (YYZ), late April 2026, approximately 5 nights. Full country name: Curaçao, an autonomous country within the Kingdom of the Netherlands.
**Currency note:** Flights are priced in CAD. All on-the-ground costs in Curaçao are in **ANG** — the Netherlands Antillean Guilder (also called the florin, symbol NAf) — pegged at a fixed rate of approximately **1 USD = 1.79 ANG**. USD is widely accepted at hotels, restaurants, and tourist-facing businesses; EUR is accepted in many tourist areas as well. CAD is not commonly accepted; use USD or credit cards on the ground. Credit cards (Visa, Mastercard) are broadly accepted across the island.

## Hotels

Curaçao's accommodation scene reflects the island's character: a mix of polished boutique resorts, historic properties converted from Dutch colonial estates, and large beach resorts anchored on the island's best stretches of sand. Unlike the Dominican Republic or Cuba, there is no mass all-inclusive monoculture here — the options span ultra-luxury boutique to comfortable beach resort, and several of the most interesting properties are genuinely distinctive. Most hotels are concentrated near Willemstad, in the Seaquarium Beach area east of the city, and along the western and northern coasts.

Prices below are in USD per night for two adults unless otherwise noted. Hotel taxes and service charges vary — confirm at booking.

- **Notes:** Not all-inclusive. This is a special-occasion property — honeymoon, milestone anniversary, or a deliberate splurge. Reserve well in advance; it is a small property with very limited availability. The Baoase Culinary Beach restaurant is accessible to non-resort guests by reservation.

## Dining

Curaçaoan cuisine is a product of the island's diverse heritage — Dutch colonial history, West African culinary tradition, Indigenous Arawak influence, and Caribbean abundance — and it has developed into one of the most genuinely distinctive food cultures in the Dutch Caribbean. The **national dish is keshi yena** — a whole Edam cheese hollowed out and stuffed with a rich, spiced filling of chicken or ground meat, olives, capers, and raisins, then baked. It is unlike anything else in the Caribbean and worth seeking out at least once. **Stoba** is a slow-braised goat or beef stew, deeply seasoned, served with **funchi** — a firm cornmeal polenta similar to Italian polenta, the carbohydrate staple of the Curaçaoan table. Fresh seafood is excellent: **wahoo**, **mahi-mahi**, and locally caught snapper appear on menus throughout the island. Dutch influence shows up in **bitterballen** (deep-fried beef ragù croquettes) served as bar snacks.

And then there is the one drink that cannot be missed: **Blue Curaçao liqueur**, made on the island from the dried peel of the laraha orange — a bitter citrus fruit that grows only on Curaçao, descended from Valencia oranges brought by the Spanish. The original producer, Senior & Co. at Landhuis Chobolobo, has been making it since 1896. Try it in a cocktail or take the distillery tour (see Attractions). The island's everyday beer is **Amstel Bright**, brewed on Curaçao itself — a lighter lager suited to the climate, served ice-cold everywhere.

**Budget note for dining:** Curaçao is considerably more affordable than Turks & Caicos or St. Barts, and roughly comparable to Aruba. Expect USD $25–$60/person for a sit-down dinner at a mid-range restaurant before drinks, and USD $60–$100/person at the finer establishments such as Baoase Culinary Beach. The local lunch options — Plasa Bieu in particular — are genuinely inexpensive and offer some of the best value eating on the island.

**Willemstad's Pietermaai district** has emerged as the island's most dynamic dining neighbourhood in recent years — a strip of restored Dutch colonial buildings converted into restaurants, bars, and boutique hotels. The old Otrobanda and Punda waterfront areas offer more traditional and casual options alongside the tourist infrastructure.

### Restaurants

**Gouverneur de Rouville** — A beautifully restored 19th-century mansion perched above the St. Anna Bay waterfront in Otrobanda, with a terrace overlooking the Queen Emma floating pontoon bridge. This is one of the most atmospheric dining settings in Willemstad — watch the bridge swing open for passing ships as you eat. The menu covers Antillean seafood and local dishes, with good versions of keshi yena and fresh fish. The sunset terrace view is worth the trip alone. Reservations recommended for evening.

**Jaanchie's Restaurant** — A Curaçaoan institution in the village of Westpunt on the island's far northwest coast, worth the 45-minute drive for the experience. Jaanchie's is a legendary family-run local spot famed for its iguana stew — a traditional Curaçaoan delicacy with an earthy, gamey flavour that has been feeding locals and curious visitors for generations. Cash only. Arrive early at lunch; it fills quickly and food runs out when it's gone. Completely authentic, completely unpretentious.

**Plasa Bieu (Old Market)** — Willemstad's beloved indoor food hall, where a rotation of local cooks — many of them older women known as "grandmothers of the island" — prepare traditional Curaçaoan dishes from simple stalls. This is the best place on the island for an authentic, inexpensive lunch: keshi yena, stoba with funchi, fresh fish plates, and local soups served at long communal tables. It is a genuinely living piece of Curaçaoan food culture, not a tourist recreation. Go for lunch; it closes in the early afternoon.

**Baoase Culinary Beach** — The restaurant of the Baoase Luxury Resort, open to non-guests by reservation. Fine dining directly on the beach, with an international menu built around exceptional local ingredients, prepared with the refinement you would expect from the island's most exclusive property. The setting — tables on the sand, the sea immediately in front — is quietly spectacular. The best special-occasion restaurant on the island. Reserve well in advance.

**Mundo Bizarro** — In the Pietermaai district, Mundo Bizarro captures the neighbourhood's up-and-coming energy with a menu of tapas-style sharing plates and creative cocktails in a colourful, design-forward space. A good choice for a relaxed evening that prioritises atmosphere and people-watching over formality. Popular with the local creative and expat community; unpretentious and fun.

**Karakter Beach Bar** — Positioned directly on Jan Thiel Beach on the east coast, Karakter is the island's definitive casual beach bar: bare feet on the sand, excellent fresh fish and grilled plates, very good cocktails, and one of the best sunset views available from a restaurant on Curaçao. Low-key, relaxed, and genuinely enjoyable. Arrive by 5:00 PM for a table with sunset views.

**The Tasting Room** — A boutique wine bar and small-plates restaurant in the Pietermaai district, popular with couples for its intimate scale, thoughtfully curated wine list, and romantic atmosphere in a restored colonial building. The food — charcuterie, cheese, and carefully prepared small plates — is well-executed without being pretentious. One of the better options for a quiet, unhurried evening drink and dinner in the neighbourhood.

## Transportation

### CUR Airport to Hotel

Hato International Airport (CUR) is located on the northern coast of Curaçao, approximately **12–15 km from Willemstad** — typically a **15-minute drive** to central Willemstad hotels. The northwest beaches (Westpunt area) are approximately 40 minutes away.

**Taxis:**
- Official taxis operate from the arrivals terminal. Curaçao taxis do not use meters — fares are fixed by government rate schedule. **Confirm the fare before getting in.**
- Approximate fares from CUR airport:
  - Airport to Willemstad hotels (Avila, Renaissance): approximately **USD $20–$25 per vehicle**
  - Airport to Seaquarium Beach / Jan Thiel area: approximately **USD $25–$35**
  - Airport to northwest coast (Westpunt area): approximately **USD $50**
- For two passengers, a taxi is the most straightforward arrival option.

**Resort transfers:**
- Several hotels (Baoase, Santa Barbara) can arrange pre-paid airport transfers — confirm at booking.

**Note:** Rideshare apps are not reliably available in Curaçao. Do not expect an Uber on arrival.

### Getting Around Curaçao

> **IMPORTANT: Curaçao drives on the RIGHT side of the road** — the same as Canada. Unlike many Caribbean islands that inherited British left-hand driving conventions, Curaçao follows Dutch/continental European convention (right-hand traffic). **Canadian drivers will find this completely natural — no adjustment needed.** This is a practical advantage for couples planning to self-drive.

**Rental Cars:**
- Car rental is strongly recommended for exploring Curaçao beyond Willemstad. The island is approximately **60 km long** — compact enough to drive end-to-end in under an hour — with well-maintained roads throughout. A rental car makes the national parks, western beaches, and village restaurants genuinely accessible.
- International agencies (Avis, Budget, Hertz) operate at CUR airport; local operators are also available.
- A valid Canadian driver's licence is accepted. Minimum age is typically 21–25 depending on agency.
- Rates: approximately **USD $40–$80/day** for a compact or small SUV.
- Roads are generally well-maintained by Caribbean standards. A standard car is adequate for most destinations; a small SUV adds comfort on the rougher tracks to some remote beaches.
- **Parking in Willemstad historic centre can be limited** — the UNESCO area has narrow colonial-era streets. Consider walking or taking a taxi within the city core rather than driving in.

**Taxis:**
- Available throughout the island; **agree the fare before you travel**, which is standard practice. Tips of 10–15% are customary.
- For a half-day or full-day of island exploration without a rental car, a negotiated day rate with a driver (approximately USD $100–$150 for a full day) is a practical alternative to multiple individual rides.

**Konvooi Buses (Yellow Buses):**
- Curaçao's public bus network (known locally as "konvooi") serves the main routes between Willemstad and the surrounding areas at low cost. Useful for getting between central Willemstad and Otrobanda or nearby neighbourhoods. Schedules can be irregular and frequency is lower than in larger cities; the bus is practical for short-hop errands within Willemstad but is not the most efficient way to reach remote beaches or national parks.
- Fares are very low — typically ANG $1–$2 (under USD $2) per trip. Exact change is useful; the bus stops are not always signed in English.

**Key geography to understand:**
Curaçao is oriented northwest–southeast, approximately 60 km in length. Willemstad, the capital, sits roughly in the middle of the south coast and is the logical base for most visitors. The **west coast** (Playa Lagun, Cas Abao, Kenepa) holds the island's finest beaches and is 25–40 minutes from Willemstad by car. The **northwest** (Westpunt, Christoffel National Park) is the wild end of the island — remote, scenic, and rewarding for a full-day excursion. The **east coast** (Jan Thiel, Seaquarium Beach, Spanish Waters) is more developed, closer to the airport, and the address of the all-inclusive resort strip. Most couples will naturally divide their time between Willemstad's cultural attractions and beach days on the west coast.

## Attractions

### 1. Willemstad Historic Waterfront & Handelskade
- **Hours:** Always accessible at street level
- **Admission:** Free
- **Description:** The face of Curaçao — and one of the most recognisable cityscapes in the Caribbean. Willemstad's Handelskade is a row of brightly painted Dutch colonial buildings in candy colours lining the St. Anna Bay waterfront, a skyline that appears on virtually every piece of Curaçao promotional material. The **Queen Emma Pontoon Bridge** — a floating pedestrian bridge assembled from 16 pontoons, built in 1888 — swings open to allow ship traffic to pass through the bay and closes again for pedestrians, free of charge. Walking across it and looking back at the Handelskade is one of those simple Caribbean moments that stays with you. The historic core of Willemstad (both the Punda and Otrobanda sides of the bay) was inscribed as a **UNESCO World Heritage Site** in 1997 for its exceptional example of Dutch colonial architecture transplanted to the tropics. Allow 2–3 hours to explore properly.

### 2. Hato Caves
- **Hours:** Daily, approximately 9 AM–4 PM (confirm at time of visit)
- **Admission:** Approximately USD $12–$15/person
- **Description:** A system of limestone caverns on the northern coast near the airport, featuring dramatic stalactites, stalagmites, a subterranean lake, and ancient **Arawak petroglyphs** drawn by the island's pre-Columbian inhabitants centuries before European contact. Guided tours lead visitors through the illuminated chambers, which also shelter a colony of fruit bats. A compact but genuinely interesting excursion — easily combined with the drive back from the airport or a morning's exploration of the north coast.

### 3. Shete Boka National Park
- **Hours:** Daily, approximately 8 AM–5 PM
- **Admission:** Approximately USD $10/person
- **Description:** The dramatic, wild north coast of Curaçao, where Atlantic swells funnel into sea caves and narrow inlets carved from the ironshore limestone coastline. The park's signature feature is a series of **blowholes** — natural vents in the rock where wave surge erupts through openings with a thunderous crash. **Boka Pistol** is particularly spectacular. The park is also a critical **sea turtle nesting site** — hawksbill turtles nest on the small protected coves within the park between May and December. A marked trail connects the main boka (inlets) along the cliff top. The rugged landscape is a meaningful contrast to the calm Caribbean beaches on the south coast.

### 4. Curaçao Sea Aquarium
- **Hours:** Daily, approximately 8 AM–5 PM
- **Admission:** Approximately USD $20–$25/person general admission; additional fees for interactive programmes
- **Description:** A well-maintained marine park in the Seaquarium Beach area east of Willemstad, featuring displays of Caribbean marine life alongside interactive programmes that allow visitors to snorkel or dive with **sea turtles**, sharks, rays, and reef fish in controlled reef environments. The turtle encounter is the highlight for many visitors. A good option for a half-morning before the beach.

### 5. Christoffel National Park
- **Hours:** Daily; early morning access recommended for the summit hike
- **Admission:** Approximately USD $12–$15/person; additional fee for guided tours
- **Description:** The island's largest nature reserve, occupying the northwest tip of Curaçao and home to **Mount Christoffel** — the island's highest point at 375 metres (1,230 feet). The summit hike takes approximately 2–3 hours return and rewards with panoramic views across the entire island. The park also supports populations of **white-tailed deer**, native orchids, cactus scrub, and abundant birdlife, including the endemic Curaçao white-tailed deer and several endangered parakeet species. Start the hike before 8 AM to avoid the midday heat — the exposed upper slopes are unshaded.

### 6. Blue Curaçao Distillery (Landhuis Chobolobo)
- **Hours:** Monday–Saturday; confirm current tour times at senior.an
- **Admission:** Free tasting; nominal tour fee for guided experience
- **Description:** The original distillery of **Senior & Co.**, producers of the authentic Blue Curaçao liqueur since 1896. Located in a historic plantation manor house (landhuis) in the Salinja neighbourhood, the distillery tour explains how the liqueur is made from the dried peel of the laraha orange — a bitter citrus grown only on Curaçao — and concludes with a tasting of the full product range, including the iconic blue, orange, and clear variations. For a destination that gave its name to an internationally famous liqueur, visiting the source is a natural priority. The landhuis building itself is architecturally beautiful. Tours are relaxed and informal; walk-ins are typically welcome.

### 7. Pietermaai District
- **Hours:** Always accessible; restaurants and bars typically from noon onwards
- **Admission:** Free
- **Description:** Willemstad's most vibrant and photogenic neighbourhood — a strip of 19th-century Dutch colonial townhouses along the waterfront east of Punda, many of them colourfully restored in recent years as restaurants, boutique hotels, galleries, and bars. Pietermaai has become the island's creative and social hub, with a character that is genuinely Curaçaoan rather than generic tourist-resort. Walking the district at golden hour, when the painted facades glow in the late afternoon light, is one of the most pleasurable things you can do in Willemstad. Several of the dining recommendations above are located here.

## Beaches

Curaçao's beaches are on the southern and western Caribbean coast — protected from Atlantic swell, clear, and calm. The island has no continuous beach strip like Aruba or the Dominican Republic; instead, the beaches are individual coves and bays separated by rocky ironshore coastline, which means each one feels distinct and the best ones are genuinely uncrowded. Water visibility is exceptional — 20–30 metres in most locations — making snorkelling directly from the beach one of the island's highlights. The contrast between the sheltered southern shore (for swimming and snorkelling) and the dramatic Atlantic-exposed north coast (for scenery and national park exploration) gives the island a meaningful geographic range for a short trip. A rental car unlocks all of the options below.

### Cas Abao Beach
The west coast's most celebrated beach — a long arc of powdery white sand and brilliant turquoise water, consistently rated among the top beaches in the entire Caribbean. Entry is gated (approximately **USD $12/person** day-use fee), which keeps crowds manageable. Full facilities: sun loungers, umbrellas, a restaurant/bar, and changing rooms. Excellent snorkelling directly off the beach on the surrounding reef. The drive from Willemstad takes approximately 25 minutes. The combination of sand quality, water colour, and facilities makes this the beach not to miss.

### Playa Kenepa Grandi (Grote Knip)
The most photographed beach on Curaçao — a dramatic cove on the far northwest coast framed by high rocky cliffs, with vividly turquoise water and white sand at the base. The drive from Willemstad takes about 40 minutes, but the approach alone — winding through the arid northwest landscape before the sea suddenly appears below — is part of the experience. Snorkelling is excellent. Day-use facilities and a small bar are on site. Arrive early; parking and shade can be limited in high season.

### Jan Thiel Beach
The most accessible beach hub east of Willemstad, approximately 20 minutes by car. Jan Thiel has developed into a genuine beach destination with multiple beach clubs, restaurants (including Karakter), watersports, and a social atmosphere that suits couples who want amenities and activity alongside their beach day. The water is calm and clear; the facilities are well-maintained. The Papagayo Beach Hotel is nearby, and the beach is a natural base for a full afternoon into sunset.

### Playa Lagun
A small, protected cove on the west coast between Willemstad and Westpunt, set within a traditional fishing village where colourful wooden boats are moored in the calm water. Playa Lagun is known for consistently excellent shore snorkelling — sea turtles, eagle rays, and a diverse reef fish population are regularly encountered directly from the sand. The cove is narrow and shaded in the morning; arrive mid-morning for calm, clear water. No large facilities — a small snack bar serves cold drinks. One of the best snorkelling spots on the island without a boat.

### Mambo Beach (Seaquarium Beach Strip)
Located in the Seaquarium Beach area southeast of Willemstad, Mambo Beach is a well-developed beach club and watersports hub — a walkable strip of beach clubs, bars, restaurants, and rental operators. Good for a high-activity beach day: jet ski rental, parasailing, stand-up paddleboarding, and beach volleyball are all available here. Lively and social; closest to Willemstad of all the main beach areas. The Mangrove Beach Corendon resort is located on this strip.

### Westpunt Beach (Playa Forti)
The most remote of the recommended beaches — a dramatic cliff-and-cove setting on the far northwest tip of the island near the village of Westpunt, about 45 minutes from Willemstad. Playa Forti is known locally as a **cliff-jumping spot** — a 10-metre cliff above the water attracts local swimmers who leap into the clear sea below (exercise caution and observe before attempting). The surrounding scenery is the most dramatic on the island. A small bar serves cold drinks and simple food. Combined with lunch at nearby Jaanchie's Restaurant, this makes a rewarding full-day trip to the northwest.

## Practical Info

### Weather

Curaçao sits **outside the Caribbean hurricane belt** — this is one of its most significant practical advantages as a destination. The island lies at approximately 12° north latitude, south of the zone where Atlantic hurricanes typically develop or track. Rainfall is consistently low year-round — Curaçao is one of the driest islands in the entire Caribbean, receiving less than 600 mm of rain annually. The "wet season" that affects other Caribbean islands is barely perceptible here. What this means for visitors: **reliable sunshine virtually year-round**, without the rain risk that affects higher-latitude Caribbean islands even in their dry seasons. Consistent northeast trade winds keep temperatures comfortable.

| Metric | April | May |
|---|---|---|
| Daytime high | 31°C (88°F) | 31°C (88°F) |
| Overnight low | 26°C (79°F) | 26°C (79°F) |
| Average rain days | ~3 days | ~4 days |
| Sea temperature | 27°C (81°F) | 28°C (82°F) |
| Sunshine | ~9 hours/day | ~9 hours/day |
| UV Index | 11–12 — **Extreme** | 11–12 — **Extreme** |

- Both April and May are exceptional months to visit. Rainfall is minimal; sunshine is near-guaranteed. Trade winds from the northeast keep humidity comfortable even at peak daytime temperatures.
- **Sea temperature** averages 27–28°C — warm enough for comfortable swimming, snorkelling, and diving without a wetsuit throughout late April and May.
- **UV warning:** A UV Index of 11–12 is classified as extreme — unprotected skin can burn in under 10 minutes at midday. Apply SPF 50+ sunscreen liberally and reapply every 90 minutes. Wear a broad-brimmed hat and UV-protecting sunglasses, especially during beach days and excursions on exposed terrain such as Christoffel National Park.
- **Hurricane season note:** Curaçao is largely unaffected by the Atlantic hurricane season (June 1–November 30). Even during the nominal hurricane season, the island's southern location means it rarely sees hurricane-force weather. This makes it a viable destination throughout the year — a meaningful distinction from most Eastern Caribbean islands.
- **Bottom line:** If you are choosing between Caribbean destinations based on weather reliability, Curaçao consistently delivers more guaranteed sunshine and less rain than comparable islands further north, in any month of the year.

### Currency

The official currency is the **Netherlands Antillean Guilder (ANG)**, also called the **florin** and abbreviated **NAf**. It is pegged at a fixed rate of approximately **1.79 ANG = 1 USD**.

- In practice, **US Dollars are accepted almost everywhere** in tourist areas — hotels, restaurants, taxis, shops, and beach clubs. EUR is also widely accepted in tourist-facing businesses. You will typically receive change in ANG.
- **Canadian dollars are not commonly accepted.** Use USD or credit cards.
- **Credit and debit cards** (Visa, Mastercard) are broadly accepted across the island, including at smaller restaurants and shops. Inform your bank before travel to avoid fraud holds.
- **ATMs** are available in Willemstad and at major shopping areas; they typically dispense ANG. The conversion to USD at the fixed rate is automatic.
- Exchange Canadian dollars to USD **before departing Canada** for the most straightforward on-island experience.

### Electricity

- **Voltage:** 127V, 50 Hz.
- **Plugs:** Type A and B — the same two-flat-prong and three-prong plugs used throughout Canada and the United States.
- **For Canadian visitors:** The plug type is identical to Canadian plugs, so **no adapter is needed**. The voltage (127V) is close to the North American standard of 120V and will not damage Canadian devices. The frequency difference (50 Hz vs. 60 Hz used in Canada) is worth noting: most modern electronics — smartphones, laptops, camera chargers, and dual-voltage travel appliances — are designed to handle both 50 Hz and 60 Hz automatically and will work without any issue. Older single-voltage appliances (certain hair dryers, curling irons, electric shavers) designed specifically for 60 Hz may run slightly slower or warmer at 50 Hz. In practice, virtually all modern Canadian electronics work perfectly on Curaçao's power. Check the label on your device's power adapter: if it reads "100–240V, 50/60 Hz," you are fully compatible.

### Language

Curaçao is officially multilingual. **Papiamentu** (also spelled Papiamento) is the local Creole language — a warm, melodic blend of Portuguese, Spanish, Dutch, and African languages that has been spoken on the island for centuries; it is the everyday language of most Curaçaoans and is widely used in media, signage, and local conversation. **Dutch** is the official language for government and formal purposes. **English** and **Spanish** are widely spoken in tourism, hospitality, and commerce.

For Canadian visitors: **English is universally spoken in hotels, restaurants, shops, and tourist-facing businesses.** There is no language barrier of any kind within the tourism environment. Learning a word or two of Papiamentu — "Bon dia" (good morning), "Danki" (thank you) — is appreciated by locals and a small gesture that goes a long way.

### Time Zone

Curaçao observes **Atlantic Standard Time (AST), UTC−4**, year-round with no daylight saving time adjustments. In late April, Toronto operates on Eastern Daylight Time (EDT, UTC−4). **There is no time difference between Curaçao and Toronto in late April.** No jet lag adjustment is needed.

### Entry Requirements for Canadians

- **Passport:** A valid Canadian passport is required for entry. Most travel advisors recommend at least 6 months of passport validity beyond your return date as a general precaution.
- **Visa:** Canadian citizens do not require a visa for tourist visits to Curaçao.
- **Return/onward ticket:** A confirmed return or onward airline ticket is required and may be requested by immigration on arrival.
- **Entry form:** An embarkation/disembarkation form (ED card) is typically completed before landing; have your accommodation address and return flight details accessible.
- **Length of stay:** Canadians may stay as tourists for up to 30 days.

### Canada Travel Advisory

As of early 2026, the Government of Canada rates Curaçao at **"Exercise Normal Security Precautions"** — the lowest advisory level, equivalent to countries considered safe for routine travel. This is the same advisory level applied to the United States, the United Kingdom, France, and Germany. **Always check travel.gc.ca** for the most current advisory before departure.

### Health Notes

- **No mandatory vaccinations** are required for entry to Curaçao for Canadian citizens.
- **Routine vaccinations** should be current: COVID-19, influenza, MMR, tetanus/diphtheria.
- **Hepatitis A** vaccination is recommended as a standard precaution for any Caribbean destination.
- **Tap water is safe to drink** in Curaçao. The island operates one of the world's oldest continuous desalination facilities; the water supply is well-managed and potable at the tap throughout the island. Bottled water is widely available for those who prefer it.
- **No malaria risk** in Curaçao. No anti-malarial medication is required or recommended.
- **Medical facilities:** Curaçao has the **Curaçao Medical Center (CMC)** in Willemstad — a modern, well-equipped hospital by Caribbean standards that handles general emergencies and most medical needs. Several private clinics and a hyperbaric chamber (relevant for divers) are also available. For serious trauma or highly specialized care, medical evacuation to the United States or the Netherlands may be required.
- **Travel medical insurance is essential.** OHIP and all Canadian provincial health plans provide no meaningful coverage outside Canada. A policy covering emergency medical treatment, hospitalization, and medical evacuation is strongly recommended.
- **Sun and heat:** The primary health risk for most visitors is sunburn and heat exhaustion, particularly given Curaçao's extreme UV levels and low cloud cover. Apply SPF 50+ liberally, stay hydrated, and seek shade during peak midday sun (11 AM–3 PM).
- **Mosquitoes:** Present at low levels; standard DEET-based repellent precautions are sensible at dawn and dusk. No significant dengue or Zika advisory specific to Curaçao at time of writing — check travel.gc.ca for current health advisories before departure.

### Tipping Customs

Curaçao follows broadly US/Caribbean convention on tipping, with some Dutch European influence (where tipping is less obligatory) — in practice, the tourism sector operates on a tip-expected model:

| Situation | Suggested Tip |
|---|---|
| Restaurant (sit-down) | 10–15% (check bill for automatic service charge — many tourist restaurants add 10% automatically) |
| Resort housekeeper | USD $3–$5/night, left daily |
| Resort porter/bellhop | USD $2–$3 per bag |
| Taxi driver | 10–15% of fare, or round up to nearest round number |
| Tour guide | USD $10–$15/person for a half- or full-day excursion |
| Spa therapist | 15–20% |
| Bar staff | USD $1–$2 per round |

- **Always check your bill first:** A service charge of 10% is commonly added at tourist restaurants. Confirm before adding an additional tip on top.
- Budget approximately **USD $150–$200 per couple** specifically for gratuities over a 5-night stay as a reasonable baseline.

### 2SLGBTQI+ Travellers

Curaçao stands out as one of the most welcoming destinations for 2SLGBTQI+ couples in the entire Caribbean — a genuine differentiator that is worth stating clearly.

**Same-sex marriage has been legal in Curaçao since 2012**, when the Supreme Court of the Netherlands ruled that Kingdom of the Netherlands law — which includes marriage equality — applies to all constituent countries of the Kingdom, including Curaçao. This makes Curaçao one of only a small number of Caribbean jurisdictions where same-sex couples have full marriage rights.

- **Legal environment:** 2SLGBTQI+ identity and relationships are fully protected under law. There are no criminal or civil penalties for same-sex relations or public expression of identity.
- **Social environment:** Willemstad and the main tourist areas are generally welcoming and open. Curaçao's diverse, internationally connected population — shaped by Dutch, Caribbean, Latin American, and global influences — tends toward social tolerance that is not universal elsewhere in the Caribbean. Same-sex couples are a visible and unremarkable presence in the hotel, dining, and beach environments.
- **Pride events:** Curaçao hosts Pride events, and the island has an established local LGBTQ+ community. Pietermaai district in Willemstad has a number of LGBTQ+-friendly bars and establishments.
- **Overall assessment:** For 2SLGBTQI+ couples, Curaçao is one of the most comfortable and affirming destinations available in the Caribbean — comparable to Aruba and significantly ahead of most Eastern and Southern Caribbean islands. Couples can expect to travel, dine, and stay with the same ease and openness they would in a major Canadian city. This is a genuine selling point for the destination, not merely a legal technicality.
