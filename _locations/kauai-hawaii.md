## Flights

### Route & Schedule

> **NO DIRECT YYZ–LIH SERVICE — TWO-LEG JOURNEY:**
> No airline operates a nonstop flight from Toronto (YYZ) to Lihue, Kauai (LIH). The standard routing for Canadians is:
> 1. **YYZ → HNL (Honolulu):** Air Canada flight **AC589**, a non-stop service on a Boeing 787 Dreamliner. Operates approximately **2x per week** — confirm exact days at aircanada.com for your travel dates. Departure from Toronto Terminal 1 typically in the **17:35–18:35** window; flight time approximately **10 hours**. Arrives Honolulu (Terminal 2) early morning local time.
> 2. **HNL → LIH (Honolulu → Kauai/Lihue):** A separate interisland leg of approximately **40 minutes** operated by **Hawaiian Airlines** (~9 flights/day), **Southwest Airlines**, or **Alaska Airlines**. Must be booked separately unless ticketed as a codeshare. Budget approximately **$49–$91 USD per person one-way** (~$70–$130 CAD); Southwest fares are often around $59 USD one-way.
>
> On days when AC589 does not operate, alternative routings via US mainland hubs (LAX, SFO, YVR with a partner carrier) are possible but add significant travel time.
> **Confirm the current AC589 schedule at aircanada.com before booking.** Frequency is limited — book the YYZ–HNL leg as early as possible.

**Flight duration:** YYZ → HNL approximately **10 hours**; HNL → LIH approximately **40 minutes**. Total westbound travel: ~11 hr in the air plus connection time in Honolulu.

**Terminal at YYZ:** Air Canada flights depart from **Terminal 1** at Toronto Pearson International Airport.

## Hotels

> 5-night stay, 2 adults. Rates are USD × ~1.43 = CAD (approx., Mar 2026). Late April is post-peak for Hawaii — rates are generally lower than January–March but demand remains solid. Book at least 3–4 months in advance.

**Grand Hyatt Kauai Resort & Spa** — Kauai's grandest resort, spread across 50 beachfront acres in Poipu (South Shore). The 604-room property centers on an extraordinary multi-level saltwater pool complex including a 1.5-acre lagoon pool and lava-rock lazy river. Home to Anara Spa (largest on the island), multiple restaurants including the celebrated Tidepools, and direct access to the beach. Reliable sunshine — Poipu is the driest part of the island.
- **Price range:** Approximately **$532–$900+ USD/night** depending on room category → **~$3,800–$6,450+ CAD / 5 nights**
- **Best for:** Couples wanting a full grand-resort experience with every amenity on-site.

> **Booking tip:** Grand Hyatt regularly offers "Book 4+ nights, save 25% + $250 resort credit" promotions. Hyatt Globalist and Explorist members receive complimentary breakfast and room upgrades subject to availability. Book directly at hyatt.com for best rate guarantee.

**Ko'a Kea Hotel & Resort** — Intimate boutique luxury directly on Poipu Beach. Only 121 rooms, refined adults-oriented atmosphere, 10-time *Travel + Leisure* World's Best winner and the only Kauai property in the T+L Hall of Fame. Every room faces the ocean. Home to Red Salt restaurant. The most romantic resort setting on the island.
- **Price range:** Approximately **$660–$1,000+ USD/night** → **~$4,700–$7,150+ CAD / 5 nights**
- **Best for:** Couples prioritizing intimacy, boutique scale, and direct beachfront romance over large-resort amenities.

**Koloa Landing Resort at Poipu (Autograph Collection)** — Upscale condo-style resort (studios to 4-bedroom villas) a short walk from Poipu Beach. Three pools including an adults-only pool voted "Best Hotel Pool in America" by USA Today 10Best for five consecutive years. Full kitchens in every unit. Part of Marriott Bonvoy.
- **Price range:** Approximately **$390–$600 USD/night** → **~$2,800–$4,300 CAD / 5 nights**
- **Best for:** Couples wanting apartment-style space, cooking facilities, and pool serenity at a slightly lower price point than the beachfront luxury resorts.

**Sheraton Kauai Resort** — 20-acre South Shore beachfront property with two pools and the excellent RumFire Poipu Beach restaurant, renowned for sunset views and craft cocktails. Good value for a genuine beachfront South Shore resort experience. Part of Marriott Bonvoy.
- **Price range:** Approximately **$195–$350 USD/night** → **~$1,400–$2,500 CAD / 5 nights**
- **Best for:** Couples wanting South Shore beach access and resort amenities at a more accessible price than Grand Hyatt or Ko'a Kea.

**Timbers Kauai / Hōkūala Ocean Club** — 450-acre sustainably-focused oceanfront property 5 minutes from LIH airport in Lihue. Award-winning golf course, 17 miles of hiking trails, organic spa, lagoons, and Hualani's oceanfront farm-to-table restaurant. Guests greeted with lei and welcome drink.
- **Price range:** Premium/ultra-luxury — contact directly for rates
- **Best for:** Couples wanting ultra-luxury with a sustainability and nature focus close to the airport.

**Sheraton Kauai Coconut Beach Resort** — Mid-range East Shore option in Kapaa town with mountain and ocean views, infinity pool, and a more local-feeling setting away from the resort corridor.
- **Price range:** Approximately **$120–$200 USD/night** → **~$860–$1,430 CAD / 5 nights**
- **Best for:** Budget-conscious couples or those who want to experience the East Shore's local character.

## Dining

Kauai's food scene punches well above its population size — the island has a serious farm-to-table movement, an abundance of fresh Pacific seafood, and several chefs of national reputation. The "Hawaii Regional Cuisine" movement (pioneered in the 1990s) emphasizes locally sourced fish, Kauai-grown produce, and the island's rich agricultural bounty. Expect fresh ahi (yellowfin tuna), mahimahi, opakapaka (Hawaiian snapper), locally grown sweet onions, Kauai shrimp, and produce from farms across the island. Poke (marinated raw fish) is the quintessential local dish — try it from a local grocery or plate lunch window for the authentic experience, alongside resort fine dining for special evenings.

**Tidepools** (Grand Hyatt Kauai, Poipu) — Consistently ranked the most romantic restaurant on Kauai and one of the most romantic in the US. Open-air thatched-roof bungalows set over a koi pond at the base of a waterfall. Menu highlights: macadamia nut-crusted fish, grilled ahi, local greens from an on-site hydroponic garden, 200-bottle wine list. Reservations essential — book before you arrive. $$$$

**The Beach House** (Koloa/Poipu, on Lawai Beach) — Ranked among the most romantic restaurants in the US by *Travel + Leisure*. Set directly on the ocean between lava rock and waves, with Pacific Rim seafood executed by chef Peter Merriman. Best visited at sunset — the view is extraordinary. Book the 6 or 7 pm seating for late April. $$$

**Red Salt** (Ko'a Kea Hotel, Poipu Beach) — Intimate oceanfront restaurant with ocean views from every table. Chef Noelani Planas (a Kauai native) elevates classic Hawaiian regional cuisine. Small, romantic, book well in advance. $$$–$$$$

**Merriman's Kauai** (Poipu) — Peter Merriman's South Shore location in an Old Hawaii Plantation setting. A pioneer of the farm-to-table movement in Hawaii; at least 90% of ingredients locally sourced. Open-air elegant-casual with live music and happy hour. Strong wine list. $$$

**Bar Acuda** (Hanalei, North Shore) — Kauai's premier North Shore restaurant for couples. Creative tapas and small plates ideal for sharing — Hanalei Bison Ranch meatballs, blackened Hawaiian fish, saffron couscous, exceptional cocktails and European wine list. Urban-chic atmosphere in the heart of charming Hanalei town. A destination worth the North Shore drive. $$$

**Gaylord's at Kilohana** (Kilohana Plantation, Lihue) — Set in the historic Kilohana Plantation manor house (1935) with open-air courtyard dining overlooking the plantation grounds and Mt. Waialeale. Farm-to-table American/Hawaii regional cuisine from the 67-acre surrounding farm. A beautiful setting for a special dinner away from the resort strip. $$$

**RumFire Poipu Beach** (Sheraton Kauai, Poipu) — 180-degree ocean views from every seat with fire pits lit at dusk. Famous for craft cocktails and spectacular sunsets. Casual-romantic; an excellent option for sundowners and dinner without the formality or price of Tidepools. $$–$$$

## Transportation

### Getting from LIH Airport to Hotels

Lihue Airport (LIH) is a small, easy airport — baggage claim is fast and the whole process is relaxed.

| Option | Est. Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Uber / Lyft | ~$10–$20 to Lihue hotels; ~$35 to Poipu; ~$60 to Hanalei/Princeville | Both apps operate on Kauai; availability can be limited in off-peak hours |
| Holoholo (local rideshare) | Fixed flat rates; varies by zone | Pre-bookable up to 1 year in advance; no surge pricing; recommended for confirmed arrival transfers |
| SpeediShuttle (shared) | ~$14–$48 depending on destination | Shared shuttles; slower than direct but cheaper |
| Rental car pickup | No transfer needed — counters across the street or a short shuttle | Recommended for the entire trip; see below |

### Rental Car — Essential

**A rental car is strongly recommended for any meaningful exploration of Kauai.** The island has no rail, no practical tourist bus network, and nearly every attraction (Waimea Canyon, Na Pali trailheads, North Shore beaches, Wailua Falls) requires independent transport. The Kauai Bus exists but is slow and not useful for most visitors with luggage or time constraints.

- **Driving side:** Kauai drives on the **RIGHT** (same as Canada). Speed limits are in **miles per hour**.
- **The road does not circle the island.** Kuhio Highway (Route 56/50) runs north–east–south; the road ends at Hāʻena State Park on the North Shore and at Polihale on the West Shore. There is no road connecting the North Shore and West Shore.
- **Companies at LIH:** ~19 rental companies including Alamo, Enterprise, Hertz, National, Budget, Avis, Dollar. Desks are at or very near the terminal.
- **Rates (late April 2026):** Economy ~$37–$50 USD/day; SUV/Jeep ~$64–$101 USD/day. Book early — rental car availability on Kauai is limited and prices rise with booking proximity.
- **Jeep/SUV advantage:** Unpaved roads to certain beaches and trailheads require higher clearance. A compact SUV is recommended over a sedan.
- **Parking:** Most beaches and state parks have free or low-cost parking; state park lots now require credit card payment via kiosk.

## Attractions

**Na Pali Coast** — 17 miles of sheer sea cliffs rising 3,000 feet above the Pacific, with hanging valleys, sea caves, and waterfalls. Widely considered the most dramatic coastal scenery in the United States — the crown jewel of Kauai. Accessible only by foot (Kalalau Trail), boat tour, or helicopter; there is no road. Day hiking to Hanakāpīʻai Beach (first 2 miles of Kalalau Trail) requires a parking reservation at Ha'ena State Park via [gohaena.com](https://gohaena.com). Hiking beyond Hanakāpīʻai Valley requires a separate permit via Recreation.gov. For couples, a sunset boat tour from Port Allen (~$150–200 USD/person) or a helicopter tour is the most dramatic and accessible experience.

**Waimea Canyon State Park ("The Grand Canyon of the Pacific")** — A 14-mile long, 1-mile wide, 3,600-foot deep canyon of red, brown, and green rock carved by the Waimea River. Overlooks accessible by car from Route 550; a 45-minute drive from Poipu. Combined with Kōkeʻe State Park at the top (sweeping Kalalau Valley views at 4,000 ft elevation), this is a full-day outing.
- **Admission:** $5 USD/adult + $10 USD parking (one payment covers Waimea Canyon and Kōkeʻe). Credit card only at self-serve kiosks.

**Hanalei Bay** — The 2-mile crescent bay backed by the fluted green Na Pali foothills. One of the most photographed landscapes in Hawaii. In late April, waves are transitioning from winter swell to calm — good conditions for beginner surfing near the pier, kayaking on the Hanalei River, and swimming. The town of Hanalei (walkable, excellent restaurants and independent shops) is the social heart of the North Shore.
- **Admission:** Free

**Helicopter Tour** — Considered by many the single best way to see Kauai, and the only way to truly experience the Na Pali Coast cliffs, Manawaiopuna Falls ("Jurassic Falls" from *Jurassic Park*), and the inaccessible interior. Strongly recommended for first-time visitors.
- **Operators:** Jack Harter Helicopters (Lihue; most highly rated); Blue Hawaiian Helicopters (Lihue and Princeville)
- **Duration:** 55–65 minutes for full-island tour
- **Cost:** ~$399 USD/person (Jack Harter 60-min doors-on tour). Shorter tours available at lower prices.

**Wailua Falls** — Twin-cascade waterfall dropping 80 feet, a 10–15-minute drive north of Lihue off Maalo Road (Route 583). Easily accessible from the road. Featured in the opening credits of the original *Fantasy Island* TV series.
- **Admission:** ~$5 USD parking fee at the state park lot (credit card)

**Spouting Horn** — Natural blowhole on a lava shelf near Poipu that shoots seawater up to 50 feet in the air with each wave. A 15–20-minute stop easily combined with a South Shore beach day. Free parking on-site. In winter/spring (December–May), watch for humpback whales offshore.
- **Admission:** Free

**Limahuli Garden & Preserve** (North Shore, near Ke'e Beach) — One of the finest botanical gardens in the US, operated by the National Tropical Botanical Garden. Native Hawaiian plants, ancient taro terraces, stream views, and surrounding Na Pali ridgelines. Set in an area inhabited for over 1,500 years.
- **Hours:** Tuesday–Saturday; last entry 2:15 pm. Closed Sunday–Monday.
- **Admission:** Self-guided $30 USD/adult; guided tour $60 USD/adult (Tuesdays & Thursdays at 9 am). Advance reservations required at [ntbg.org](https://ntbg.org).

**Fern Grotto (Wailua River)** — A natural cave draped in hanging ferns, accessible only by riverboat. Operated by Smith's Motor Boat Service from Wailua Marina — a 2-mile boat ride each way with onboard ukulele and hula music. A classic, relaxed Kauai excursion.
- **Cost:** ~$22–$25 USD/adult

## Beaches

**Poipu Beach** (South Shore) — Voted the #1 beach in America (Travel Channel). Two connected coves: the western "Baby Beach" (calm, shallow, Hawaiian monk seals rest here regularly) and the eastern Waiohai Beach (snorkeling, bodyboarding). Lifeguard on duty. Consistent sunshine year-round — Poipu is the driest part of the island.

**Hanalei Bay** (North Shore) — The 2-mile crescent bay framed by Bali Hai peaks; the most photogenic beach in Hawaii for most visitors. Moderating surf in late April. Best swimming near the pier; the western end has more consistent waves for surfing. Spectacular mountain backdrop at every angle.

**Ke'e Beach / Ha'ena State Park** (North Shore — end of road) — The road ends at one of Kauai's most dramatic settings: a reef-enclosed lagoon backed by the first ridgelines of the Na Pali Coast. Outstanding snorkeling in the lagoon (calmer in late April). The Kalalau Trail begins here.
- **Advance reservation required** via [gohaena.com](https://gohaena.com). Open daily 7 am–6:45 pm.

**Tunnels Beach / Makua Beach** (North Shore) — Hawaii's best snorkeling beach by many accounts. Named for its underwater lava tube formations. Exceptional marine life, coral, and sea turtles. Late April (the start of Hawaii's calm season) offers ideal snorkeling conditions. Very limited roadside parking — arrive before 8 am or walk in from Ha'ena Beach Park.

**Anini Beach** (North Shore) — Protected by Hawaii's longest fringing reef, making it flat and calm inside the reef — ideal for stand-up paddleboarding, windsurfing, and beginner snorkeling. Large coconut-shaded beach park with picnic tables. Less crowded than Hanalei.

**Kalapaki Beach** (Lihue — East Shore) — The most accessible beach from LIH airport. Horseshoe-shaped, wide, calm, and good for swimming and beginner surfing. Adjacent to the Marriott Beach Club and JJ's Broiler restaurant. Useful on arrival or departure day.

## Practical Info

### Weather — Late April

| | Average |
|---|---|
| Daytime high | ~24–26°C (75–79°F) |
| Overnight low | ~18°C (64°F) |
| Sea temperature | ~24°C (75°F) |
| Rain days (full month) | ~1–2 days in the final week of April |
| Sunshine per day | ~8–9 hours |

Late April is the transition from Kauai's wetter winter to its dry season. The final week of April is among the year's most reliable. **South Shore (Poipu) is significantly drier than the North Shore (Hanalei/Princeville)** — if maximizing beach and sunshine time, South Shore accommodation is recommended. Trade winds provide cooling breezes despite 70–73% humidity; conditions feel comfortable. Ocean is calming from winter swell — good snorkeling and calm-water conditions emerging.

### Currency

US Dollars (USD) are the only currency used in Hawaii — no exchange needed at destination. Obtain USD before departure (your Canadian bank, airport exchange, or ATM in Honolulu or Kauai). Rate context: **1 USD ≈ 1.43 CAD** (March 2026) — a $500 USD hotel night costs approximately $715 CAD. Major credit cards accepted universally; inform your bank of travel dates.

### Electricity

**110V, Type A/B outlets** — identical to Canada. No adapter or voltage converter needed. All Canadian devices work without modification.

### Language

English only. Some Hawaiian words used for place names and local directions (mauka = toward the mountains, makai = toward the ocean). No language barrier.

### Time Zone

Hawaii does **not** observe Daylight Saving Time. In late April, Toronto is on **Eastern Daylight Time (EDT, UTC−4)**. Hawaii is on **Hawaii-Aleutian Standard Time (HST, UTC−10)**.

**Hawaii is 6 hours behind Toronto** in late April — when it is noon in Toronto, it is 6:00 am in Kauai. Sunrise in late April is around 6:00–6:10 am local time; sunset around 7:00–7:15 pm. The westbound overnight flight departs Toronto in the evening and arrives in Honolulu in the early morning — a good start to the day after the short interisland connection.

### Entry Requirements

Hawaii is a **US state** — US entry rules apply.

- **Canadian citizens do NOT need an ESTA.** ESTA is only for Visa Waiver Program nationals. Canadians enter under a separate regime.
- **Required:** A valid **Canadian passport** for air travel. Passport cards and NEXUS cards are NOT accepted for air entry to the US (land/sea only).
- **No visa required** for Canadian citizens for tourism stays up to 6 months.
- First Nations members should note that Secure Certificate of Indian Status (SCIS) cards are not accepted for air entry to the US.
- US border agents have the authority to search electronic devices without cause under current US policy — be aware.

### Canada Travel Advisory

**Level 1: Take normal security precautions** — the lowest advisory level. No elevation for Hawaii.

The advisory notes: gun violence rates, border agent device search authority, immigration registration requirement for stays over 30 days, and FIFA World Cup health notice (not applicable for Kauai in April). Source: [travel.gc.ca/destinations/united-states](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/united-states)

### Health

- No vaccinations required or recommended specifically for Hawaii.
- **Travel insurance with emergency medical coverage is strongly recommended** — US healthcare costs are very high; provincial health plans provide minimal out-of-country coverage.
- **Sun protection is critical.** UV index in Kauai in April is high (8–10+). Hawaii law bans sunscreens containing oxybenzone or octinoxate in state waters — use reef-safe biodegradable sunscreen.
- Tap water is safe to drink throughout Kauai.
- Ocean safety: follow posted lifeguard signs, check surf conditions, and swim near lifeguard stations where available.

### 2SLGBTQI+ Travel

Hawaii has **among the strongest LGBTQI+ legal protections in the United States.**

Same-sex marriage has been legal in Hawaii since **December 2, 2013** (Hawaii Marriage Equality Act). In November 2024, Hawaii voters approved a constitutional amendment (55.9% approval) removing the 1998 anti-gay language from the state constitution, further entrenching equality. Anti-discrimination protections cover sexual orientation and gender identity in employment (since 1991), housing (2005), and public accommodations (2006). Hawaii established a State LGBTQ+ Commission in 2022. Approximately 74% of Hawaii residents support anti-discrimination protections. Kauai is welcoming — all resorts, restaurants, and operators listed in this guide are inclusive environments.
