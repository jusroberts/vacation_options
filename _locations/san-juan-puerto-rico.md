## Hotels

San Juan's accommodation scene divides naturally across three neighbourhoods, each with a distinct character. **Isla Verde** is the traditional resort strip — wide beach, casino hotels, the closest area to the airport, and a lively party-friendly energy. **Condado** is the upscale urban resort neighbourhood — a peninsula with both lagoon and ocean beaches, concentrated dining, rooftop bars, and a more sophisticated atmosphere. **Old San Juan** is the historic walled city — cobblestone streets, 500 years of colonial architecture, and a handful of boutique properties that deliver an entirely different experience from the beach resorts. Choosing between them is the first and most important hotel decision.

Prices below are in USD per night for two adults unless otherwise noted. Puerto Rico does not have a dominant all-inclusive resort market in the way that Cuba, the Dominican Republic, or Jamaica do — the island's proximity to the US mainland and its role as a sophisticated urban and cultural destination has shaped a hotel landscape of branded resorts, independent boutique properties, and historic conversions. This works well for couples who want variety and the flexibility to dine out; it means there is no single-payment all-in option, and daily food and activities costs should be budgeted separately.

### 1. El San Juan Hotel (Autograph Collection)
- **Area:** Isla Verde beachfront
- **Price range:** Approximately **USD $350–$700/night** depending on room category and season
- **Style:** Iconic landmark; glamorous; full-service; beachfront with casino
- **What makes it special:** Opened in 1958, El San Juan is Puerto Rico's most celebrated resort hotel — the mahogany-panelled lobby with its hand-carved ceiling is one of the most dramatic interior spaces in the Caribbean, and the property's mid-century glamour has been carefully preserved through multiple renovations. The hotel fronts a long stretch of Isla Verde beach, operates a full casino, multiple restaurants and bars, and draws a sophisticated crowd that appreciates the architecture as much as the beach. It is an experience in itself — guests often photograph the lobby before they even reach their room. For couples celebrating a milestone or simply wanting to stay somewhere genuinely iconic, El San Juan is the obvious choice.
- **Notes:** Not all-inclusive. Rates at the upper end reflect ocean-view rooms and suites. The Isla Verde location puts the airport approximately 10 minutes away — convenient for arrival and departure. Book well in advance; this is a popular property for both leisure and event travel.

### 2. La Concha Renaissance San Juan Resort
- **Area:** Condado, oceanfront
- **Price range:** Approximately **USD $280–$550/night**
- **Style:** Sleek modern resort; rooftop pool; vibrant social atmosphere
- **What makes it special:** La Concha is Condado's most visually arresting resort — a distinctive curved tower with an eye-catching design that has made it a neighbourhood landmark. The rooftop pool and bar are among the liveliest in San Juan, with views across the Condado beach and ocean. The property attracts a younger, energetic crowd and is well-positioned for the Condado dining and nightlife scene directly outside its doors. The casino and multiple dining outlets keep the energy going well into the evening.
- **Notes:** Not all-inclusive. The Condado location is walkable to excellent restaurants and the Condado lagoon area. La Concha suits couples who want beach access alongside a social, design-forward atmosphere rather than seclusion.

### 3. Condado Vanderbilt Hotel
- **Area:** Condado beachfront
- **Price range:** Approximately **USD $400–$900/night**
- **Style:** Historic grande dame; elegant; Spanish Revival architecture; oceanfront
- **What makes it special:** Built in 1919 and designed by Warren & Wetmore (the architects of New York's Grand Central Terminal), the Condado Vanderbilt is Puerto Rico's most historically distinguished hotel. Its Spanish Revival facade, formal pool terrace, and understated elegance contrast deliberately with the modern resort properties around it. The service is attentive and personal in a way that larger contemporary hotels rarely match. The beachfront position and the building's extraordinary architecture make it a hotel worth visiting even if you are staying elsewhere. For couples who want classic luxury and a genuine sense of history, this is the finest address in Condado.
- **Notes:** Not all-inclusive. Rates at the upper end reflect oceanfront suites. The Vanderbilt operates at a notably more refined pace than its neighbours; if vibrant nightlife is the priority, look to La Concha. If quality, history, and impeccable service are the criteria, this is the property.

### 4. Gallery Inn
- **Area:** Old San Juan
- **Price range:** Approximately **USD $200–$400/night**
- **Style:** Boutique; 17th-century mansion; artistic; labyrinthine; no beach
- **What makes it special:** The Gallery Inn is unlike any other hotel in Puerto Rico — a 17th-century colonial mansion in Old San Juan converted by artist Jan D'Esopo into an extraordinary living work of art. The property's labyrinthine courtyards, terraces, and rooftop lookouts are filled with sculptures, paintings, plants, and original works. No two rooms are alike. From the rooftop terrace, views extend over the red-tile rooftops of Old San Juan, the harbour, and El Morro. There is no beach (Old San Juan is a walled city peninsula), but the hotel's location puts you directly within the historic district. For couples who prize character, culture, and an utterly singular experience over a beach chair, the Gallery Inn is unforgettable.
- **Notes:** Not all-inclusive; not a conventional resort. The intimate scale means advance booking is essential. A rental car or Uber is needed to reach beaches. This is a property for travellers who understand that the experience itself is the point.

### 5. Olive Boutique Hotel
- **Area:** Condado
- **Price range:** Approximately **USD $180–$320/night**
- **Style:** Boutique; rooftop pool; stylish; great value for couples
- **What makes it special:** The Olive is the Condado neighbourhood's best smaller boutique option — a well-designed, intimate property with a rooftop pool that offers genuine style at a price point well below the Vanderbilt or La Concha. The location puts guests within easy walking distance of Condado's best restaurants, the lagoon, and the ocean beach. For couples who want a characterful Condado base without the price of the major resort hotels, the Olive consistently receives strong reviews for its attentive service and design sensibility.
- **Notes:** Not all-inclusive. The smaller property scale means fewer on-site amenities than the large resorts, but the surrounding neighbourhood fills that gap effectively. Book early — with limited rooms, availability fills quickly.

### 6. AC Hotel by Marriott San Juan Condado
- **Area:** Condado
- **Price range:** Approximately **USD $150–$280/night**
- **Style:** Mid-range; modern; reliable brand; good location
- **What makes it special:** The AC Hotel delivers Condado's best combination of price, location, and reliable comfort for couples who want a well-maintained, modern hotel without boutique-property prices or full-service resort costs. The European-influenced AC Hotels brand emphasises clean design, a good bar programme, and consistent quality — all of which it delivers here. The Condado location gives easy access to the neighbourhood's dining and beach. For couples on a more measured budget who still want to be in the right part of San Juan, this is the practical choice.
- **Notes:** Not all-inclusive. A solid mid-market option — not as distinctive as the Olive or Vanderbilt, but more reliably available, frequently bookable through Marriott Bonvoy points, and well-reviewed for value.

> **Booking tip:** Puerto Rico has a developed independent hotel market and does not feature as prominently in Canadian mass-market package deals as all-inclusive destinations. Air Canada Vacations occasionally offers San Juan packages — worth checking — but most of the best properties above are best booked directly or through a travel agent. Marriott Bonvoy members should check the AC Hotel and Renaissance (La Concha) for points opportunities. For Old San Juan properties and boutique hotels, direct booking is typically best. Late April is transitioning from peak to shoulder season in San Juan — booking 2–4 months in advance is generally sufficient, though the Condado Vanderbilt and Gallery Inn fill quickly for weekends.

## Dining

Puerto Rican cuisine is one of the Caribbean's richest and most deeply rooted food cultures, shaped by the island's Taíno Indigenous heritage, Spanish colonial influence, and West African culinary tradition — a combination the island calls **"la cocina criolla."** The national dish is **mofongo** — green plantains fried and mashed with garlic, olive oil, and chicharrón (pork cracklings), then shaped into a bowl or mound and filled with seafood, chicken, or pork stew. It appears on virtually every menu in some form and is the first thing to order. **Tostones** (twice-fried green plantain rounds, crispy and served with garlic dipping sauce) and **maduros** (sweet fried ripe plantains) are the definitive side dishes. **Pernil** — slow-roasted pork shoulder marinated overnight in garlic, oregano, and recao — is the centerpiece of any serious Puerto Rican feast. **Arroz con gandules** (rice cooked with pigeon peas and sofrito) is the island's essential rice dish, always served alongside pernil. For the full traditional experience, the road to Guavate in the central mountains is lined with lechoneras — open-air pork restaurants serving whole spit-roasted **lechón** over wood coals, a Sunday pilgrimage for Puerto Ricans.

On the drinks front, San Juan makes two strong claims: it is the **birthplace of the piña colada** (both the Hotel Barrachina in Old San Juan and the Caribe Hilton's Beachcomber Bar have been claiming the honour since the 1950s — order one at each and form your own view). **Medalla Light** is the island's everyday beer — light, cold, and cheap enough that it appears in every corner store, beach kiosko, and neighbourhood bar. And Puerto Rico is home to two of the world's great rum producers: **Bacardí**, whose massive distillery in Cataño is a short ferry ride from Old San Juan and open for tours, and **Don Q**, produced by the Serrallés family in Ponce since 1865 and ubiquitous in local bars. A rum-based coquito — Puerto Rico's coconut-cream holiday drink — may appear on menus in some traditional restaurants year-round.

San Juan's dining scene has matured significantly since Hurricane Maria in 2017; the post-recovery period brought a wave of young Puerto Rican chefs reinvesting in the local food culture, and the result is a restaurant landscape well beyond what most first-time visitors expect from a Caribbean destination.

**Marmalade** — Old San Juan's most acclaimed fine-dining destination, from chef Peter Schintler whose work has received James Beard recognition. The intimate candlelit room delivers creative New American tasting menus built around Puerto Rican ingredients — locally caught fish, tropical fruits, island herbs — with a level of technical polish rare anywhere in the Caribbean. This is the restaurant to book for a special evening; it is romantic, quiet, and superbly executed. Reserve well in advance; it fills weeks ahead.

**La Factoria** — An Old San Juan institution that has earned a reputation as one of the finest cocktail bars in Latin America. Behind a simple storefront on Calle San Sebastián, a series of interconnected rooms unfold — each with a different atmosphere, a different bar, live music in some, a quieter nook in others. The cocktail programme is serious and inventive. Food is available but the drinks are the reason to come. A mandatory stop on any Old San Juan evening.

**Santaella** — Chef José Santaella's flagship restaurant in the Santurce neighbourhood's Mercado de Santurce area, widely regarded as one of the most exciting kitchens in Puerto Rico. The cooking is modern Puerto Rican — rooted in criolla tradition but technically refined, with creative presentations and a genuine sense of place. The neighbourhood around it, Santurce, is San Juan's most dynamic arts and dining district. Reservations recommended.

**Pirilo** — Wood-fired pizza may not seem like the most obvious Puerto Rican experience, but Pirilo has earned a devoted following for the best pizza on the island across its multiple San Juan locations. High-quality ingredients, properly blistered crust, and a casual, welcoming atmosphere make it an ideal lunch stop or a relaxed dinner when you want something approachable. A favourite with locals and expats alike.

**La Casita Blanca** — A Santurce legend: a small, no-frills dining room serving what many consider the finest traditional Puerto Rican home cooking in San Juan. The menu is handwritten daily, lunch only, and rotates around classics — pernil, mofongo, arroz con gandules, stews, and sofrito-based dishes executed the way they have been for generations. Reserve ahead; it fills quickly and when the food is gone, it is gone. This is the correct answer to "where should I eat traditional Puerto Rican food."

**Lote 23** — A curated food truck park in Santurce where a rotating selection of San Juan's most interesting food vendors set up in a lively open-air courtyard with shared seating, craft beer on tap, and a casual social atmosphere. An ideal way to sample several styles of Puerto Rican and international cooking in one place — excellent for an exploratory lunch or early dinner in the Santurce neighbourhood.

**Raíces** — Old San Juan's most accessible introduction to traditional Puerto Rican cuisine, with a festive, colourful dining room, live music on many evenings, and a menu covering all the classics — mofongo, pernil, tostones, arroz con gandules, fresh red snapper. The atmosphere is warm and convivial; it suits first-time visitors to the cuisine who want context and energy alongside their meal. Good for a first evening in San Juan.

## Transportation

### SJU Airport to Hotel

Luis Muñoz Marín International Airport (SJU) is located in the Isla Verde area, approximately **15–20 minutes by taxi or rideshare** from Condado and Old San Juan, and effectively at the doorstep of Isla Verde hotels.

**Taxis and Rideshare:**
- **Uber and Lyft** both operate widely in the San Juan metro area — a significant practical advantage over most Caribbean destinations. Opening your usual app on arrival works exactly as it would at home. This is one of Puerto Rico's most convenient differentiators for North American visitors.
- Official taxi fares from SJU are regulated:
  - Airport to Isla Verde hotels: approximately **USD $10–$14 per vehicle**
  - Airport to Condado: approximately **USD $15–$19 per vehicle**
  - Airport to Old San Juan: approximately **USD $19–$24 per vehicle**
- For two passengers, a taxi or Uber is the most straightforward arrival option. Confirm fare with taxi drivers before departing.

### Getting Around San Juan

> **Puerto Rico drives on the RIGHT side of the road — the same as Canada.** No adjustment to driving habits is needed. Road signs are in Spanish, but distances are marked in kilometres and the road network follows familiar conventions. Waze and Google Maps work reliably throughout the metro area and are strongly recommended given the complexity of San Juan's highway interchanges.

**Rental Cars:**
- Car rental is available from all major agencies (Avis, Budget, Hertz, Enterprise, National) at SJU airport — the widest selection of any Caribbean destination covered in this series.
- A valid Canadian driver's licence is accepted. Minimum age is typically 21–25 depending on agency.
- Rates: approximately **USD $40–$80/day** for a compact or small SUV.
- A rental car is strongly recommended for day trips outside San Juan — El Yunque rainforest, Luquillo Beach, and Guavate are all 45–60 minutes from the city and best reached independently. For couples planning to spend most of their time in Condado and Old San Juan (both walkable or Uber-accessible), a car is optional.
- **Traffic in metropolitan San Juan can be significant**, particularly on PR-18 and PR-26 during morning and evening rush hours. Use Waze for real-time routing.

**Uber and Lyft:**
- Both platforms operate throughout the San Juan metro area, making them the most convenient and familiar option for couples who want rideshare convenience without a rental car. Particularly useful for evenings in Old San Juan when parking is difficult.

**Tren Urbano:**
- San Juan has a metropolitan rail system (Tren Urbano) connecting the Bayamón area through Santurce to Hato Rey. Useful for reaching some points within the metro area but does not serve the airport, Old San Juan, or the beach districts directly. A supplementary option rather than a primary transport solution for most visitors.

**Old San Juan:**
- The historic walled city is best explored on foot — the cobblestone streets, plazas, and colourful buildings are the experience. A **free trolley** circulates through Old San Juan on a regular loop, connecting the cruise terminal area to the main plazas and providing a break from the hills.

## Attractions

San Juan offers a depth of attractions unusual in the Caribbean — a UNESCO World Heritage historic district, two US National Historic Site fortresses, the only tropical rainforest in the US National Forest system, one of the world's great rum distilleries, and one of earth's few bioluminescent bays, all within roughly 90 minutes of the hotel districts. The combination of world-class history, genuine natural wonders, and vibrant neighbourhood culture gives a 5-night stay more to work with than most Caribbean islands of comparable flight time.

### 1. Old San Juan Historic District
- **Hours:** Always accessible at street level; individual sites have their own hours
- **Admission:** Free to explore; individual forts charge admission
- **Description:** The oldest continually inhabited European-established settlement in US territory, Old San Juan is a 500-year-old walled colonial city on a small peninsula at the mouth of San Juan Bay — and a **UNESCO World Heritage Site**. The streets are paved with blue-grey adoquines (ballast bricks brought from Spain) and lined with 16th–19th century buildings in vivid ochre, terracotta, and blue, many of them harbouring bars, restaurants, and boutique shops at street level. San Sebastián Street is the social spine; the Cathedral of San Juan Bautista (where Ponce de León is interred) and the Plaza de Armas anchor the historic core. Walking Old San Juan at golden hour, then staying for dinner and drinks in the neighbourhood, is one of the finest urban experiences in the Caribbean.
- **Tip:** Allow a full day. Wear comfortable shoes — the cobblestone streets are beautiful but uneven on sandals.

### 2. Castillo San Felipe del Morro (El Morro)
- **Hours:** Daily, 9 AM–6 PM
- **Admission:** Approximately **USD $10/person** (covers both El Morro and Castillo San Cristóbal)
- **Description:** The fortress that defines San Juan's skyline — a six-level Spanish military fortification built beginning in 1539 at the tip of the Old San Juan peninsula, designed to protect the harbour from seaborne attack. El Morro's dramatic ramparts drop vertically to the Atlantic on three sides; the views from the upper terraces across the ocean and back over the city are extraordinary. Kite-flying on the adjacent field is a San Juan tradition; the wide green esplanade leading to the fort is one of the most pleasant walks in the city. Managed by the US National Park Service as a National Historic Site.
- **Tip:** Arrive early to avoid the cruise ship crowds that arrive late morning. The combination ticket covering El Morro and San Cristóbal is excellent value.

### 3. Castillo San Cristóbal
- **Hours:** Daily, 9 AM–6 PM
- **Admission:** Covered by the combined fort ticket (~USD $10/person)
- **Description:** The largest fortification built by Spain in the Americas — a massive defensive complex at the eastern land entrance to Old San Juan, with 27 acres of tunnels, ramps, barracks, and bastions. Where El Morro guarded the sea approach, San Cristóbal defended against land attack. The engineering of the fort, built over the 17th and 18th centuries, is extraordinary. The views from the upper ramparts extend across the city and coastline. Also managed by the US National Park Service.

### 4. El Yunque National Rainforest
- **Hours:** Daily, approximately 7:30 AM–6 PM; some trails close earlier
- **Admission:** Timed entry passes required (approximately USD $2 per person); book in advance at recreation.gov
- **Description:** The **only tropical rainforest within the US National Forest system** — a 28,000-acre wilderness in the Sierra de Luquillo mountains approximately 45 minutes east of San Juan by car. El Yunque receives up to 3,500 mm of rain annually; the result is a genuine cloud forest of ceiba trees, tree ferns, bromeliads, and orchids, with multiple waterfalls accessible by trail. The endemic **Puerto Rican parrot** — one of the rarest birds in the world — inhabits the upper forest. La Mina Falls and Juan Diego Creek are the most popular destinations within the park. A half-day or full-day excursion and among the most rewarding natural experiences available in Puerto Rico.
- **Tip:** Book timed entry passes well in advance — they sell out weeks ahead, especially on weekends. Go early; afternoon clouds and rain are common.

### 5. La Placita de Santurce and Santurce Arts District
- **Hours:** La Placita market area is active from midday; evening bars from around 6 PM; weekends are most vibrant
- **Admission:** Free to explore
- **Description:** Santurce is San Juan's most creative and rapidly evolving neighbourhood — a grid of streets between Condado and Miramar that has become the city's arts, food, and nightlife hub over the past decade. The Santurce Es Ley street art festival has covered the neighbourhood in remarkable murals; the Museo de Arte de Puerto Rico anchors the cultural scene. La Placita — a small open-air plaza surrounded by bars and vendors — transforms on Thursday, Friday, and Saturday evenings into a lively open-air social scene where locals spill out onto the street with drinks. The neighbourhood restaurants (Santaella, La Casita Blanca, Lote 23) are among San Juan's best. For a couple wanting to experience Puerto Rican city life as it actually functions, an evening in Santurce is essential.

### 6. Bacardí Rum Distillery (Cataño)
- **Hours:** Monday–Saturday, 9 AM–4:30 PM; Sunday, 10 AM–4 PM (confirm at casabacardi.com)
- **Admission:** Tours approximately USD $12–$45/person depending on tour level; tastings included
- **Description:** The world's largest rum distillery — producing more than 100,000 barrels of rum annually — is located in Cataño, across San Juan Bay from Old San Juan. Getting there is part of the experience: a short ferry ride from the Pier 2 area in Old San Juan (approximately USD $2 each way) crosses the bay to Cataño, from where a free shuttle connects to the Casa Bacardí visitor centre. Tours cover the history and production of Bacardí rum from its Cuban origins through its Puerto Rican reinvention, culminating in a structured tasting of aged expressions. An enjoyable half-day excursion easily combined with an Old San Juan morning.
- **Tip:** The VIP Legacy Tour and the cocktail-making classes are popular add-ons for couples — book online in advance.

### 7. Bioluminescent Bay (Laguna Grande, Fajardo)
- **Hours:** Tours depart at dusk; timing varies with season and moon phase
- **Admission:** Guided kayak tours approximately USD $50–$75/person through tour operators; advance booking strongly recommended
- **Description:** One of the most remarkable natural phenomena in the world: Laguna Grande in Fajardo is one of only a small number of bioluminescent bays on earth, where microscopic dinoflagellates (pyrodinium bahamense) produce brilliant blue-green light when agitated by movement. Kayaking through the dark water at night leaves trails of light with every paddle stroke; fish dart beneath the kayaks like living sparks. The effect is most dramatic on moonless nights. The bay is approximately 90 minutes east of San Juan; tour operators provide transport, kayaks, and guides. A night tour here is one of Puerto Rico's most memorable and unique experiences.
- **Note:** Vieques Island's Mosquito Bay is considered the world's brightest bioluminescent bay but requires a ferry and overnight stay. Laguna Grande (Fajardo) is the most practical option for a day-tripping couple based in San Juan.

## Beaches

San Juan's beaches range from the urban and convenient (Condado, Isla Verde) to the genuinely remote and world-class (Flamenco Beach on Culebra). The beaches directly in the city are well-facilitated, lively, and walkable from hotels — strong choices for couples who want beach access without logistics. The eastern beaches at Luquillo and beyond require a car or guided excursion but deliver a meaningfully different and more expansive experience. The outer islands (Culebra, Vieques) are a step further in effort and reward — the water clarity and sand quality at Flamenco Beach in particular are as good as anything in the Caribbean. Water temperature in late April averages a comfortable **27°C (80°F)** throughout.

### Condado Beach
The urban beach of the Condado resort district — accessible directly from Condado hotels and within easy walking distance of the neighbourhood's restaurants and bars. The ocean side has moderate wave action suited to bodysurfing and swimming; the calmer Condado Lagoon side (the protected lagoon a few blocks to the south) is flat-calm and popular with paddleboarders, kayakers, and families. Beach bar amenities are concentrated along the strip. Condado Beach has a social, metropolitan energy — locals and hotel guests mix naturally, vendors circulate, and the Condado skyline provides a backdrop entirely unlike a remote resort island. It is a genuinely urban Caribbean beach experience.

### Isla Verde Beach
A long, wide crescent of sand stretching east from Condado toward the airport, backed by the Isla Verde resort hotels. The water here is generally calm and clear; the beach is wide enough that the presence of multiple resort properties does not feel overcrowded. Water sports, beach bars, and restaurant access are all close at hand. The sand quality is good and the swimming is straightforward. Isla Verde is the most resort-classic beach in San Juan — predictable in the best sense.

### Ocean Park Beach
Situated between Condado and Isla Verde, Ocean Park is the neighbourhood beach — less resort-dominated, more local, and consistently popular with San Juan residents. The beach attracts windsurfers (consistent onshore breeze) and volleyball players alongside swimmers. The atmosphere is genuine and unpretentious; there are fewer tourist-facing beach vendors and more locals simply enjoying a Sunday afternoon. An excellent choice for a relaxed day away from the resort energy.

### Luquillo Beach (Balneario La Monserrate)
The finest beach within practical day-trip distance of San Juan — approximately 45 minutes east on PR-3, a long palm-lined crescent of calm, protected Caribbean water ideal for swimming. The **kioskos** (food kiosks) at the beach entrance are a Puerto Rican institution, selling mofongo, alcapurrias, piña coladas, and cold Medalla from early morning — eating at the kioskos before or after the beach is obligatory. Facilities include lifeguards, changing rooms, and shade structures. A favourite with families and couples alike; arrive early on weekends as the parking fills by midday.

### Flamenco Beach (Culebra Island)
Consistently ranked among the world's finest beaches — a horseshoe bay of brilliant white sand and vivid turquoise water on the small island of Culebra, approximately 27 km off Puerto Rico's northeast coast. Reaching it requires a passenger ferry from Ceiba (approximately 1 hour, around USD $4–$5 each way) and a short taxi or bus ride across Culebra. The round trip is manageable as a long day trip or, ideally, an overnight to properly experience the island. The water at Flamenco is exceptionally clear, the sand powdery and uncrowded outside of peak season, and the snorkelling on the surrounding reef is excellent. One of the Caribbean's genuinely great beaches and worth the logistics.

### Crash Boat Beach (Aguadilla)
On Puerto Rico's northwest coast, approximately two hours from San Juan — vivid blue water, a colourful fishing pier jutting into the sea, and some of the finest snorkelling accessible from a Puerto Rican shore beach. The water here has an almost electric blue colour that photographs exceptionally well; the pier and surrounding rock formations create a natural underwater habitat explored easily with a mask and snorkel. Popular with locals for weekend gatherings and very much off the tourist-resort circuit, which keeps the atmosphere authentically Puerto Rican. Worth combining with the nearby Arecibo Observatory site and the Rincón coast surf scene for a full northwest day trip. Bring your own snorkel gear.

## Practical Info

### Weather

Late April is an excellent time to visit San Juan. Puerto Rico's climate is tropical and warm year-round; the dry season runs broadly from December through April. Late April sits at the very end of the dry season — temperatures are warm but not oppressive, trade winds provide reliable cooling, and rainfall is noticeably lower than the summer months.

| Metric | April | May |
|---|---|---|
| Daytime high | 30°C (86°F) | 31°C (88°F) |
| Overnight low | 23°C (73°F) | 24°C (75°F) |
| Average rain days | ~8 days | ~11 days |
| Sea temperature | 27°C (81°F) | 28°C (82°F) |
| Sunshine | ~8 hours/day | ~7–8 hours/day |
| UV Index | 11–12 — **Extreme** | 12 — **Extreme** |

- April offers the best balance of warm temperatures, low rainfall, and comfortable humidity. May remains warm and very swimmable but sees increasing afternoon showers as the season transitions.
- **Hurricane season officially begins June 1.** A late April departure is well clear of any meaningful hurricane risk. Puerto Rico lies within the main Atlantic hurricane track; the late April timing is one of the best arguments for visiting in this window.
- **UV warning:** A UV Index of 11–12 is classified as extreme — unprotected skin can burn in as little as 10 minutes at midday. Apply SPF 50+ sunscreen liberally and reapply every 90 minutes. Wear a broad-brimmed hat and UV-blocking sunglasses, particularly on open beaches and during the El Yunque excursion.

### Currency

The **US Dollar (USD)** is the sole currency of Puerto Rico, which operates entirely within the US monetary system. Canadian dollars are **not accepted**.

- **Credit and debit cards** (Visa, Mastercard) are accepted universally across San Juan — at all hotels, restaurants, shops, and most food trucks. Tap payment and digital wallets function normally.
- **ATMs** are abundant throughout San Juan and dispense USD. Canadian debit cards (Interac) work at Cirrus/Plus network ATMs; check with your bank regarding international withdrawal fees. US-networked ATMs are widely available given Puerto Rico's status as US territory.
- Exchange Canadian dollars to **USD before departing Canada** — at your bank, a currency exchange, or the airport. This is the most straightforward approach. US dollars are universally exchangeable in Canada.

### Electricity

- **Voltage:** 110V, 60 Hz — identical to Canada.
- **Plugs:** Type A and B — the same two-flat-prong and three-prong plugs used throughout Canada.
- **Canadian visitors need no power adapter or voltage converter whatsoever.** Every Canadian phone charger, laptop, hair dryer, and travel appliance will work directly in Puerto Rico outlets without any modification. This is one of Puerto Rico's most practical advantages over other Caribbean destinations where adapter or converter concerns arise.

### Language

**Spanish and English are both official languages of Puerto Rico.** Spanish is the everyday language of most Puerto Ricans; English is universally spoken in hotels, restaurants, tourist sites, and commercial businesses throughout San Juan. In Old San Juan and the Condado/Isla Verde resort districts, English-only communication creates no barriers of any kind. US federal government signage and services are in English. For couples who speak only English, San Juan is among the most accessible Caribbean destinations available.

### Time Zone

Puerto Rico observes **Atlantic Standard Time (AST, UTC−4) year-round.** Puerto Rico does **not observe daylight saving time.** In late April, Toronto operates on Eastern Daylight Time (EDT, UTC−4). **There is no time difference between San Juan and Toronto in late April.** No jet lag adjustment is needed — a meaningful convenience for a 5-night trip.

### Entry Requirements for Canadians

Puerto Rico is a US territory, and entry rules for Canadians are governed by US federal immigration law. This has important implications:

- **Passport required:** A valid Canadian passport is required to enter Puerto Rico. Unlike travel between Canadian provinces or to some destinations, a birth certificate alone is **not sufficient** — a valid passport is mandatory.
- **Visa:** Canadian citizens **do not require a US visa** for tourist travel to Puerto Rico. Canadians are generally exempt from ESTA requirements when flying directly to Puerto Rico from Canada (Puerto Rico is a US domestic destination under the Visa Waiver Program framework). Confirm with your airline and the current US CBP requirements at time of booking.
- **US Customs and Border Protection (CBP) Preclearance:** Flights departing from Toronto Pearson (YYZ) to US destinations — including San Juan — are subject to **US CBP preclearance at YYZ**. This means Canadian travellers clear US customs and immigration in Toronto before boarding, arriving in San Juan as a domestic US arrival. Allow extra time at YYZ for preclearance queues.
- **Passport validity:** Ensure your passport is valid for the full duration of your trip. Most advisors recommend at least 6 months of validity beyond your return date as a general precaution, though US entry requirements focus on validity through the travel dates themselves.
- **Return/onward ticket:** A confirmed return ticket is standard and may be requested.
- **No arrival card:** Because Puerto Rico is US territory, arriving passengers from preclearance airports do not complete a separate immigration form at destination.

**Practical note:** The US CBP preclearance process at YYZ operates well and is a genuine convenience — you clear customs before departure rather than upon arrival, which speeds up your arrival in San Juan. Factor in an extra 45–60 minutes at YYZ check-in specifically to allow for the preclearance queue, especially in peak travel periods.

### Canada Travel Advisory

As of early 2026, the Government of Canada rates Puerto Rico at the level applicable to **travel within the United States** — **"Exercise Normal Security Precautions"** — the lowest advisory level, equivalent to countries considered safe for routine travel. Puerto Rico shares the same advisory status as the continental United States. **Always check travel.gc.ca** for the most current advisory before departure.

### Health Notes

- **No mandatory vaccinations** are required for Canadian citizens entering Puerto Rico.
- **Routine vaccinations** should be current: COVID-19, influenza, MMR, tetanus/diphtheria.
- **Hepatitis A** vaccination is recommended as a standard precaution for any travel to the Caribbean.
- **Tap water is safe to drink** in Puerto Rico. The island's water supply is managed under US EPA standards and is potable from the tap throughout San Juan. Bottled water is widely available for those who prefer it — but there is no health concern with drinking tap water.
- **Dengue:** Aedes mosquitoes are present in Puerto Rico and dengue is endemic at low levels. Use DEET-based insect repellent, particularly at dawn and dusk, and in vegetated areas such as El Yunque.
- **Zika:** Puerto Rico has historically seen Zika virus transmission. The risk level fluctuates; **couples who are pregnant or planning pregnancy should consult a physician and review current CDC guidance before travelling.** Check the Government of Canada health notices at travel.gc.ca and the CDC website for current Zika status in Puerto Rico before booking.
- **Medical facilities:** San Juan has fully US-standard medical infrastructure — hospitals, clinics, pharmacies, and emergency services operate under US federal regulations and accreditation standards. The Hospital Auxilio Mutuo, HIMA San Pablo, and other private facilities in San Juan are well-equipped. This is a significant practical advantage over many Caribbean destinations where medical infrastructure is more limited.
- **Travel medical insurance is strongly recommended.** OHIP and Canadian provincial health plans provide no coverage outside Canada, including in US territories. A policy covering emergency medical and evacuation is essential even in a destination with good medical facilities, as costs are billed at US private rates which are very high.
- **Sun and heat:** The primary health risk for most visitors is sunburn and heat exhaustion. Apply SPF 50+ liberally, stay hydrated, and seek shade during peak midday sun (11 AM–3 PM).

### Tipping Customs

Puerto Rico follows US tipping conventions — tipping is expected and an integral part of service workers' income:

| Situation | Suggested Tip |
|---|---|
| Restaurant (sit-down) | 15–20% (check bill — some tourist restaurants add automatic gratuity) |
| Resort housekeeper | USD $3–$5/night, left daily |
| Resort porter/bellhop | USD $2–$3 per bag |
| Taxi driver | 15–20% of fare |
| Uber/Lyft | Tip within app; 15–20% standard |
| Tour guide | USD $10–$20/person for a half- or full-day excursion |
| Spa therapist | 15–20% |
| Bar staff | USD $1–$2 per round |

- **Check your bill:** Some restaurants in tourist areas add an automatic 15–18% gratuity. Always confirm before adding an additional tip.
- Budget approximately **USD $150–$250 per couple** specifically for gratuities over a 5-night stay as a reasonable baseline.

### 2SLGBTQI+ Travellers

San Juan is one of the most welcoming destinations in the Caribbean for 2SLGBTQI+ couples — a genuine and meaningful differentiator from many other Caribbean islands.

**Same-sex marriage has been fully legal in Puerto Rico** since the US Supreme Court's *Obergefell v. Hodges* ruling in 2015, which applies to all US states and territories. Puerto Rico is subject to the full framework of US federal anti-discrimination protections.

- **Legal environment:** 2SLGBTQI+ identity, relationships, and expression are fully protected under US federal law. Puerto Rico has its own legislative protections for LGBTQ+ individuals as well.
- **Social environment:** San Juan — particularly the Condado and Santurce neighbourhoods — has a visible, established, and active LGBTQ+ community. Condado is widely regarded as the most LGBTQ+-welcoming neighbourhood in the city, with multiple bars, restaurants, and hotels that actively serve the community. Same-sex couples are a visible and unremarkable presence throughout the hotel, dining, and beach environments.
- **Pride events:** San Juan hosts an annual Pride parade (typically June) and a range of LGBTQ+-organized events throughout the year. Santurce's bar and arts scene includes several LGBTQ+-centred venues.
- **Overall assessment:** For 2SLGBTQI+ couples, San Juan delivers a level of legal protection, social openness, and cultural visibility comparable to a major mainland US or Canadian city. It is among the most affirming Caribbean destinations available, comfortably ahead of most Eastern and Southern Caribbean islands. Couples can expect to travel, stay, dine, and explore with complete ease.
