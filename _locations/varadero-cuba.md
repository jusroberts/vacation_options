## Flights

### Route & Schedule

Air Canada operates non-stop service between Toronto Pearson (YYZ) and Juan Gualberto Gómez Airport (VRA) in Varadero. In late April, Air Canada typically flies this route **2–3 times per week** (often Mondays, Wednesdays, and/or Saturdays — confirm exact schedule when booking as seasonal patterns shift). Flight numbers on this route include **AC1400, AC2140, AC2142, AC2220, and AC2222**.

**Flight duration:** Approximately **3 hours 35 minutes** (YYZ to VRA). Return is similar.

**Terminal at YYZ:** All Air Canada flights depart from **Terminal 1** at Toronto Pearson International Airport. Check-in counters, security, and gates are all within Terminal 1.

**Estimated round-trip cost (2 passengers, economy):**
- Economy Basic fares start from approximately **CAD $490/person** one-way in season, making a round trip roughly **CAD $1,000–$1,400 per person** all-in (taxes included).
- For 2 passengers: budget approximately **CAD $2,000–$3,000 total** for economy round-trip flights in late April.
- Prices vary significantly by booking window; booking 2–4 months in advance is recommended.
- Air Transat and WestJet also serve YYZ–VRA non-stop and are often competitively priced — worth comparing.

## Hotels

Varadero operates almost entirely on an **all-inclusive model**. Virtually all resorts include meals, drinks (domestic and many international brands), entertainment, non-motorized water sports, and beach/pool access. The peninsula runs approximately 20 km; the far northeast end (Hicacos area) tends to be quieter and more upscale, while the mid-peninsula and southwest end near town are more lively and affordable.

### 1. Royalton Hicacos Resort & Spa (Adults Only)
- **Area:** Northeast Hicacos tip of the peninsula — quieter, premium end
- **Category:** 5-star, adults-only all-inclusive
- **Price range (5 nights, 2 people):** Approximately **CAD $2,500–$4,000** depending on package (flight + hotel bundles through Sunwing/Air Canada Vacations are often cheaper)
- **Key amenities:** Direct beachfront, multiple pools, full-service spa, fitness centre, multiple specialty restaurants, swim-up bar, butler service in premium rooms
- **Best for:** Couples seeking a luxury, crowd-free experience; consistently rated TripAdvisor's #1 hotel in Varadero

### 2. Paradisus Princesa del Mar (Adults Only)
- **Area:** Northeast peninsula, Hicacos
- **Category:** 5-star, adults-only, Meliá group
- **Price range (5 nights, 2 people):** Approximately **CAD $2,800–$4,500**
- **Key amenities:** Two pristine beach sections, The Reserve (premium section with enhanced perks), spa, gourmet dining, butler service, concierge
- **Best for:** Romantic escapes; couples who want a step above standard all-inclusive with quieter ambiance

### 3. Meliá Las Américas
- **Area:** Mid-peninsula near the Varadero Golf Club
- **Category:** 5-star, adults-only all-inclusive
- **Price range (5 nights, 2 people):** Approximately **CAD $2,000–$3,200**
- **Key amenities:** Adjacent to Cuba's only 18-hole golf course (Varadero Golf Club), beachfront, pools, multiple restaurants, spa
- **Best for:** Golf-loving couples; premium product at a slightly more approachable price than the Hicacos properties

### 4. Iberostar Varadero

- **Price range (5 nights, 2 people):** Approximately **CAD $1,800–$2,800**

### 5. Barceló Solymar Beach Resort
- **Area:** Mid-peninsula, close to Varadero town
- **Category:** 4-star all-inclusive
- **Price range (5 nights, 2 people):** Approximately **CAD $1,400–$2,200**

> **Booking tip:** Canadian package operators — **Sunwing, Air Canada Vacations, Air Transat Holidays, Transat, and SellOff Vacations** — bundle flights and hotels and consistently offer lower prices than booking separately. Late April is shoulder season; deals often appear 6–8 weeks out.

## Dining

Varadero offers a mix of resort dining and independent restaurants outside the all-inclusive bubble. Eating out at a **paladar** (privately owned restaurant) is highly recommended for at least 2–3 meals — quality and authenticity tend to be better, and prices remain reasonable by Canadian standards.

### Restaurants

**1. Varadero 60**
- **Cuisine:** Cuban / International, 1960s-themed
- **Price range:** ~USD $15–$35/person
- **Known for:** Lobster dishes, sirloin steak, retro atmosphere, live music most nights. The Piquillos with Lobster Pimentier is a standout.

**2. La Barbacoa**
- **Cuisine:** Cuban BBQ and seafood
- **Price range:** ~USD $12–$30/person
- **Known for:** Whole lobster, prime cuts, very popular with resort guests venturing out for a night. Reliable quality.

**3. Salsa Suárez**
- **Cuisine:** Cuban / Italian fusion, paladar
- **Price range:** ~USD $10–$25/person
- **Known for:** Beautiful garden patio near the beach, gourmet Cuban plates with European influences. One of the most romantic settings in Varadero.

**4. Paladar Nonna Tina**
- **Cuisine:** Italian, paladar
- **Price range:** ~USD $10–$25/person
- **Known for:** Wood-fired thin-crust pizza, hand-made pasta, bruschetta. Unexpectedly authentic for Cuba; popular with long-stay guests who want a change from Cuban food.

**5. Restaurant Dante (Parque Josone)**
- **Cuisine:** Italian, silver-service restaurant in a park setting
- **Price range:** ~USD $15–$35/person
- **Known for:** Serving since 1993, lakeside setting inside Parque Josone, pasta and traditional Italian mains. Elegant for a special dinner.

**6. Bistro Bar Atlántida**
- **Cuisine:** Caribbean / Cuban tapas
- **Price range:** ~USD $8–$20/person
- **Known for:** Excellent tostones (twice-fried plantains), octopus tapas, mojitos, and a relaxed beach-adjacent vibe. Good for a light lunch.

**7. La Bodeguita del Medio (Varadero branch)**
- **Cuisine:** Cuban bar / casual food
- **Price range:** ~USD $8–$18/person
- **Known for:** Branch of the Havana institution; famous mojitos, authentic Cuban atmosphere, walls covered in graffiti signatures. More bar than restaurant, great for drinks and snacks.

**8. Don Alex**
- **Cuisine:** Italian / Cuban fusion
- **Price range:** ~USD $15–$35/person
- **Known for:** Rustic brick-wall interior away from the main tourist strip; refined setting perfect for a romantic dinner. Slightly pricier but excellent quality.

**9. El Criollo**
- **Cuisine:** Traditional Cuban
- **Price range:** ~USD $8–$18/person
- **Known for:** Classic ropa vieja, rice and beans, lechón (roast pork), yuca — the most authentic Cuban home cooking in a restaurant setting near Parque Josone.

### Street Food and Local Market Tips

- The **Mercado Municipal** (local market, western end of town) is where locals shop. Look for fresh fruit, snacks, and small cooked items. Bring CUP (Cuban pesos) in small denominations.
- **Coppelia ice cream** stalls are ubiquitous — the state-run ice cream chain is a Cuban institution and costs almost nothing in CUP.
- **Rooftop paladares** and small family-run spots along Avenida 1ra (1st Avenue) offer sandwiches, pizza, and snacks for a few dollars.
- **Scam alert:** If a stranger approaches you and enthusiastically recommends a particular restaurant, decline politely and avoid the establishment they suggest — it is a common commission scam where tourists end up at overcharging venues.

## Transportation

### VRA Airport to Hotel

Juan Gualberto Gómez Airport (VRA) is located approximately **15–20 km west of the main resort strip**. Most all-inclusive packages include airport transfers — confirm with your tour operator before arrival.

If arranging your own transfer:
- **Official metered taxis (Cubataxi):** Fixed rate of approximately **USD $25–$35** for the 20-minute ride to the resort area. Agree the price before getting in.
- **Private transfer booked in advance:** USD $30–$50 one-way, but more reliable and often includes a name card at arrivals. Book through your resort or a Cuban tour operator website.
- There is no shuttle bus service from VRA airport.

### Getting Around Varadero

**Varadero Beach Tour Bus (most popular):**
An open-top double-decker hop-on/hop-off bus serves 46 stops along the entire peninsula from 9:30 AM to 9:00 PM daily. A full-day pass costs approximately **USD $5/person**. Buses run roughly every 30 minutes. This covers every hotel, the golf club, the dolphinarium, downtown Varadero, and the marina — it is by far the most practical way to get around.

**Regular taxis (Cubataxi):**
Comfortable air-conditioned sedans. Flag one at any hotel entrance or have the concierge call one. Fares for anywhere within Varadero are typically **USD $5–$10**. Always agree on the fare before you get in; meters exist but are rarely used.

**Cocotaxis:**
Iconic yellow egg-shaped 3-wheeled vehicles that carry 2 passengers. Slightly more expensive than a regular taxi but a uniquely Cuban experience. Great for short hops along the peninsula. Negotiate price first; expect **USD $5–$8** for a short ride.

**Classic American Cars ("Maquinas"):**
Privately operated 1950s American cars serve as collective taxis along fixed routes and as private hires. For a private hire, expect to pay **USD $10–$20** depending on distance. A popular way to arrive at a restaurant for a special evening.

**Bicitaxis:**
Pedal-powered bicycle taxis ideal for very short distances downtown. A few dollars per ride, negotiable.

**Rental cars:**
Available at the airport and major hotels through Cuban state operators (Rex, Havanautos). Renting in Cuba requires paying in cash (USD/EUR/CAD) and fuel can be difficult to find due to ongoing shortages. Not recommended for first-time visitors in April 2026 given fuel availability uncertainties.

**Ride-sharing apps:**
No Uber or Lyft in Cuba. The app **La Nave** operates similarly in some Cuban cities but coverage in Varadero is limited — do not rely on it.

### Day Trips from Varadero

**Havana** (approx. 140 km, 2 hrs each way by car):
The most popular excursion. Full-day guided tours by air-conditioned bus or private classic car visit Old Havana (UNESCO World Heritage Site), Plaza de la Revolución, El Malecón, El Morro Fortress, and include lunch.
- Group tour: ~USD $75–$120/person through resort desk or tour operators
- Private tour in a classic 1950s car: ~USD $159–$220 per car (up to 4 people) through operators like VC Tours

**Matanzas** (approx. 50 km, 45 min):
Closest city; the "Athens of Cuba" for its theatres, bridges, and colonial architecture. Visit Teatro Sauto, the historic pharmacy Botica Francesa, and colourful streetscapes.
- Easily done independently by taxi (~USD $30–$40 round trip) or Viazul bus (runs 3x/day)
- Combine with the **Cueva de Bellamar** stalactite caves (~USD $8 entry) or Cueva de Saturno cenote (~USD $5, great for swimming)

**Zapata Peninsula / Bay of Pigs** (approx. 1.5 hrs):
National park (UNESCO Biosphere Reserve) with crocodile farm, museum, excellent snorkeling/diving. The Bay of Pigs Museum at Playa Girón is sobering and fascinating.
- Group tours available from resort desks; ~USD $65–$90/person

**Trinidad and Cienfuegos** (approx. 4 hrs each way — a long day):
UNESCO Heritage colonial towns. Typically a 12-hour excursion via group bus. Not for the faint-hearted as a day trip but worthwhile for travellers with cultural interest.
- ~USD $80–$110/person through group tour operators

## Attractions

### 1. Varadero Beach (Playa de Varadero)
- **Hours:** Always accessible
- **Admission:** Free
- **Description:** 22 km of fine white sand and turquoise-blue shallow water — one of the Caribbean's premier beaches. Calm, warm water (avg. 27°C in April) ideal for swimming. All resorts have direct beach access; public access points also exist.
- **Tip:** April is one of the best months — warm, relatively dry, past the peak crowds of February/March but before the summer humidity hits.

### 2. Parque Josone
- **Hours:** Daily, dawn to dusk (restaurants until 11 PM)
- **Admission:** Free (boat rides and some activities cost extra)
- **Description:** Lush tropical park built in the 1940s in the heart of Varadero. Ornamental gardens, a small lake with rowboats, walking paths, and several restaurants. A peaceful counterpoint to the beach resort strip.
- **Tip:** Best in the early morning or late afternoon; great spot for an evening dinner at Restaurant Dante.

### 3. Cueva de Ambrosio
- **Hours:** Daily ~9 AM–5 PM
- **Admission:** ~USD $5
- **Description:** A 300-metre cave system containing pre-Columbian indigenous paintings — the largest concentration of cave art in the Caribbean. The cave also served as a hideout for escaped slaves. Home to bats; natural light cascades through cave openings.
- **Tip:** Wear closed-toe shoes; guided tours (included in admission) run regularly.

### 4. Varahicacos Ecological Reserve
- **Hours:** Daily ~8 AM–5 PM
- **Admission:** ~USD $5
- **Description:** 312-hectare nature reserve at the tip of the Hicacos Peninsula. Mangroves, native cacti, iguanas, and coastal flora. Contains two caves with indigenous artefacts. Home to the "Patriarca," a 500-year-old cactus believed to be Cuba's oldest living plant.
- **Tip:** Morning is best for wildlife spotting. Bring mosquito repellent.

### 5. Cueva de Musulmanes
- **Hours:** Daily ~9 AM–5 PM
- **Admission:** Adults USD $5 | Children USD $2
- **Description:** Cave system with dramatic stalactite and stalagmite formations. Interesting guided tour covering the geological and pre-Columbian history of the cave.

### 6. Varadero Dolphinarium (Delfinario)
- **Hours:** Daily 9:30 AM–5 PM; shows at 11 AM and 3:30 PM
- **Admission:** USD $15 | Photos: +USD $5 | Swim with dolphins: ~USD $89
- **Description:** 40-minute dolphin shows with well-trained bottlenose dolphins. Swim-with-dolphins experiences available by booking in advance through the resort.
- **Tip:** The ethics of dolphin captivity are debated — consider whether this aligns with your values before visiting.

### 7. Varadero Golf Club
- **Hours:** Daily, sunrise to sunset
- **Admission:** Greens fees vary; approximately USD $50–$80/person for 18 holes (cart included)
- **Description:** Cuba's only 18-hole par-72 golf course, opened in 1999. Set between the lagoon and the ocean alongside the Meliá Las Américas resort. Well-maintained by Cuban standards; club rental available.
- **Tip:** Book tee times through your resort concierge.

### 8. Casa de la Música Varadero
- **Hours:** Shows typically start 10 PM–midnight (Tue–Sun)
- **Admission:** ~USD $10
- **Description:** Live Cuban music — salsa, son, timba — in an open-air venue. A quintessential Cuban evening out; locals and tourists mix on the dance floor.
- **Tip:** Arrive by 10:30 PM; the venue fills quickly on weekends.

### 9. Museo Municipal de Varadero
- **Hours:** Tue–Sat 10 AM–6 PM, Sun 9 AM–1 PM
- **Admission:** ~USD $3
- **Description:** A beautifully preserved 1920s wooden Cuban villa housing exhibits on Varadero's pre-revolution history, period furniture, and local heritage. The building itself is arguably the main attraction.

### 10. Marina Gaviota — Water Sports Hub
- **Hours:** Daily 8 AM–5 PM
- **Admission:** Varies by activity
- **Description:** The main hub for water-based activities: deep-sea fishing charters (half/full day), catamaran excursions to Cayo Blanco, scuba diving trips, snorkeling tours, and sunset sailing cruises.
- **Typical prices:** Catamaran day trip to Cayo Blanco (with lunch and snorkeling): USD $60–$80/person; deep-sea fishing: USD $80–$150/person

### 11. Cueva del Pirata (Nightlife)
- **Hours:** Mon–Sat, cabaret show begins ~10:30 PM
- **Admission:** ~USD $10
- **Description:** A natural cave converted into a cabaret and nightclub. Stalactites hang above the dance floor; the Afro-Cuban show is followed by dancing with a live band or DJ. One of Varadero's most unique nightlife experiences.

## Beaches

### The Main Beach: Playa de Varadero
Varadero's defining feature is a single continuous 22-km beach running the length of the Hicacos Peninsula. The sand is fine and white to pale pink, the water shallow and calm (a sandy shelf extends far offshore, keeping the surf gentle), and the colour ranges from turquoise near the shore to deep blue further out.

**Facilities:** Every all-inclusive resort has a section of the beach with sun loungers, umbrellas, bar service, and watersport rentals. Public beach access points are spaced throughout, though without the resort amenities. Water temperature in April averages **27°C** — perfect for swimming.

**Different stretches of the beach:**
- **Northeast (Hicacos):** Quieter, fronting the premium resorts (Royalton, Paradisus). Narrower in spots but pristine. Best for couples seeking tranquility.
- **Mid-peninsula:** Wider, more lively, fronting the Iberostar and Barceló properties. Good watersports infrastructure.
- **Southwest (near town):** More public beach activity, locals present, some vendors. Livelier and less manicured but more authentic.

### Cayo Blanco
A small uninhabited island accessed by catamaran from Marina Gaviota (~45 minutes). Crystal-clear water over pristine coral reefs — some of the best snorkeling near Varadero. Excursions typically include a beach lunch and open bar.
- **How to get there:** Book a catamaran day trip through your resort or Marina Gaviota (~USD $60–$80/person)
- **Facilities:** Basic facilities set up by tour operators on the day; otherwise undeveloped

### Playa Coral
Located approximately 25 km west of Varadero, toward Matanzas. Accessible by rental car or taxi. The reef here is the most developed coral system near Varadero and one of the best snorkeling/diving spots in northwest Cuba. The dive centre on-site offers guided tours.
- **How to get there:** Taxi (~USD $20 one-way) or rental car; ask your resort to arrange a dive/snorkel excursion
- **Facilities:** Small dive centre, basic amenities

### Cueva de Saturno (Cenote)
Not a beach per se, but a freshwater cenote (cave pool) with excellent visibility and fascinating rock formations. Located ~12 km from Matanzas, ~60 km from Varadero. Swimming inside the cave is surreal.
- **Admission:** ~USD $5
- **How to get there:** Taxi or as part of a Matanzas day trip

## Practical Info for Cuba Specifically

### Currency Situation (Critical — Read This)

Cuba's currency situation is genuinely complex and has changed multiple times in recent years. As of early 2026:

**CUP (Cuban Peso) — the only official currency**
- The CUP is the sole official currency since the CUC was abolished on January 1, 2021.
- Official exchange rate: approximately **USD $1 = CUP 120** (set at government exchange offices/CADECA).
- The informal street rate is significantly higher (USD 1 = CUP 300–400+ on the informal market in 2025, though a floating rate reform in December 2025 has begun narrowing this gap). Do not exchange money on the street — this is illegal and unsafe.

**MLC (Moneda Libremente Convertible)**
- A digital currency pegged 1:1 to USD, used in some government-run dollar stores. You can buy prepaid MLC debit cards (in denominations of USD $50–$1,000) to shop at these stores. Cards come with a USD $5 fee and are non-refundable.
- As a tourist staying at an all-inclusive, you will rarely need MLC.

**What currency to bring:**
- **USD cash or EUR cash are the most useful currencies** to bring. As of late 2025/early 2026, USD is widely accepted at tourist businesses, restaurants, taxis, and tips.
- **Canadian dollars (CAD) are accepted at most resorts** and exchange desks, and are easily exchanged — but not universally accepted outside the resort.
- USD incurs an 8% exchange fee at official CADECA offices; EUR and CAD incur only a 2% fee. Exchanging at least some cash to EUR before arriving will give you a better rate.
- **Credit/debit cards:** Canadian-issued Visa and Mastercard may technically work at some hotel ATMs, but reliability is very poor. Machines frequently malfunction or run out of cash. **Bring all the cash you expect to need.** Wise, Revolut, and other fintech cards do not work in Cuba.
- The general advice: bring **USD $800–$1,200 per couple** for a 5-night trip (outside all-inclusive costs) to cover tips, dining out, excursions, shopping, and incidentals. Bring small bills ($1, $5, $10).

### Internet Access — Realistic Expectations

- **Resort WiFi:** All major all-inclusive resorts provide WiFi to guests, typically free of charge. Obtain login credentials from the front desk (each device requires separate credentials). Speeds are slow by North American standards; video calls and streaming are often frustrating. Do not rely on internet access for important work.
- **Public hotspots (ETECSA NAUTA WiFi):** Available in parks, plazas, and some commercial areas. Requires purchasing a NAUTA card (~USD $1/hour or ~USD $5 for 5 hours) from an ETECSA office, some hotel desks, or authorized kiosks. Lines at ETECSA offices can be 20–30 minutes.
- **Tourist SIM card (Cubacel):** A foreign tourist SIM is available (~USD $28–$30) offering 6 GB of data, 100 national/international minutes, and 100 SMS for up to 30 days. Requires a foreign passport. More practical for those spending time outside the resort.
- **VPN:** Download and configure a VPN **before you leave Canada**. Some websites and social media platforms are blocked in Cuba. A VPN helps but does not guarantee access.
- **Bottom line:** Plan to be largely offline. Download offline maps (Maps.me or Google Maps offline), guides, and reading material before departing Toronto. Notify family/work of limited connectivity expectations.

### What to Bring from Home

Cuba faces ongoing shortages of basic goods. The following are difficult or impossible to find:
- **Prescription medications** (bring your full supply plus a few extra days' worth)
- Sunscreen (SPF 30+, essential — UV Index reaches 12 in April, classified as extreme)
- Insect repellent with DEET (dengue and chikungunya mosquitoes are active)
- Over-the-counter medications (ibuprofen, antihistamines, antidiarrheal, antacid)
- Quality tampons/menstrual products
- Condoms
- Razors, quality toiletries
- A good power bank for your phone
- A universal travel adapter (Cuba uses Type A, B, C, and L outlets — see Practical Info section)
- Cash in small denominations (see Currency section)
- Printed travel insurance documents (required for entry)
- **Optional but appreciated:** Many Canadian travellers bring small personal hygiene gifts (soap, shampoo, toothpaste) to give to hotel staff or local families. This is entirely voluntary and is not expected, but is deeply appreciated given supply shortages.

### Canadian vs. American Tourists — Key Differences

- Canadians have **no travel restrictions** to Cuba from the Canadian government side, unlike Americans who face ongoing U.S. Treasury OFAC regulations limiting the nature of travel.
- Canadian-issued credit and debit cards are not affected by the U.S. embargo — though as noted, card reliability in Cuba is generally poor regardless.
- Cuba is historically one of the top Canadian winter vacation destinations; Cuban hospitality workers are very accustomed to Canadians. English is widely spoken in resort areas.
- Canadians are generally well regarded; be courteous and respectful rather than apologetic or ostentatious.

### Travel Insurance Requirements

**Travel medical insurance is mandatory by Cuban law** for all international visitors. Cuban immigration can and does verify proof of coverage at the border.

- Your policy must cover: emergency medical treatment, hospitalization, medical evacuation, and repatriation of remains. It must be valid for the **entire duration of your stay**.
- Standard Canadian provincial health insurance (OHIP for Ontario residents) provides virtually no meaningful coverage outside Canada — do not rely on it.
- Most Canadian package tour operators include ASISTUR SA insurance in their pricing. Confirm this with your travel agent before purchasing separate coverage.
- ASISTUR SA coverage costs approximately **USD $4.50/person/day**. Top up with comprehensive trip cancellation/interruption coverage from a Canadian provider.
- If you arrive without proof of insurance, Cuba will require you to purchase local coverage at the airport. This is limited and expensive.
- Given Cuba's current advisory level (see Safety section), ensure your policy explicitly covers destinations under a "high degree of caution" advisory — some Canadian policies have exclusions.

### Health and Medical Facilities

- **Clínica Internacional Varadero:** Calle 60 esq. 1ra Ave, Varadero. Tel: +53 4566-7710. This is the main international medical clinic serving tourists in the resort area. Staffed by Cuban physicians; basic emergency care and some specialist services. Better equipped than local public hospitals but still well below North American standards.
- Most all-inclusive resorts have an on-duty doctor or nurse available 24/7.
- **Emergency number in Cuba: 104**
- Serious medical cases typically require evacuation to Havana (Clínica Central Cira García) or air evacuation out of Cuba — ensure your insurance covers this.
- Cuba has chronic shortages of pharmaceuticals. Bring everything you need.

## Safety

### Canada Travel Advisory Level

As of early 2026, the Government of Canada rates Cuba at **"Exercise a High Degree of Caution"** — the second of four advisory levels (below "Avoid Non-Essential Travel"). The primary reasons:

- Worsening shortages of electricity, fuel, food, water, and medicine that can affect resorts
- Daily scheduled power outages plus unpredictable blackouts lasting 24+ hours
- Fuel shortages disrupting transportation including taxis
- Economic instability that can disrupt flight schedules on short notice

**The Varadero Peninsula specifically** is assessed by Canada as a lower-risk sub-region — the advisory notes that resort areas including Varadero operate under normal security precautions relative to the rest of the country. However, the national conditions (power outages, fuel shortages) still affect Varadero.

**Practical implication:** The advisory does not mean Varadero is unsafe for Canadian tourists in the traditional sense — petty crime against tourists is low. The concern is primarily logistical disruption and supply/infrastructure issues. An all-inclusive resort mitigates most of these risks by providing its own food, water, and generator backup.

### Neighbourhood-Specific Safety Notes

- **The resort peninsula (Hicacos and mid-peninsula):** Generally very safe. Tourist police maintain a presence; the strip is well-lit and heavily monitored.
- **Varadero town (western end):** Safe to walk around during the day and most of the evening. Normal precautions apply: do not flash expensive jewellery or large amounts of cash.
- **Travelling to Havana or other cities:** Safe for tourists as well, but keep in mind that Havana has pockets with higher petty crime rates. Travel with a reputable guide or tour group; avoid poorly lit areas at night.

### Practical Safety Tips for Cuba

- **Petty theft and pickpocketing** are the most common crime risks. Use a money belt; do not keep your wallet in a back pocket.
- **"Jineteros"** (hustlers) are present in tourist areas and may offer cigars, rum, restaurant tips, or "friendship" that ultimately leads to a request for money. A polite "no thank you" and walking on is sufficient.
- Counterfeit cigars are extremely common. Only buy cigars from official Casa del Habano stores, not from strangers on the street — no matter how convincing the story.
- Cuba has a **significant police presence** around tourist areas; violent crime against tourists is very rare.
- Do not photograph police, military personnel, or official government buildings.
- **Power outages:** All-inclusive resorts have backup generators for common areas and most rooms. Carry a small flashlight or use your phone torch during blackouts.
- **Drink only bottled water** — even in resort areas, tap water quality is inconsistent. Use bottled water for brushing teeth when in doubt.
- Keep a photocopy of your passport, tourist card, travel insurance, and return flight information separate from the originals.

### April Weather

Late April is an excellent time to visit Varadero — it falls at the end of the dry season, just before the May–October wet season begins:

- **UV warning:** A UV Index of 12 means unprotected skin can burn in as little as 10 minutes. Apply SPF 50+ sunscreen every 90 minutes. Wear a broad-brimmed hat and UV-blocking sunglasses.
- Rain tends to come as brief afternoon showers; mornings are almost always sunny.
- Hurricane season officially begins June 1 — late April carries essentially no hurricane risk.

### Electricity and Voltage

- **Plugs:** Cuba uses **Type A** (two flat prongs), **Type B** (two flat prongs + round ground), **Type C** (two round prongs), and **Type L** (three round prongs in a line). You will encounter different outlet types even within the same hotel.
- **Voltage:** Either **110V or 220V** depending on the building — newer hotels often use 220V; older ones use 110V. Outlets are sometimes (inconsistently) labelled.
- **Frequency:** 60 Hz.
- **Canadians:** Canadian plugs (Type A/B) fit some Cuban outlets directly. However, a **universal travel adapter** is strongly recommended, as you will encounter Type C and L outlets.
- Most modern electronics (smartphones, cameras, laptops) are dual-voltage (100–240V) and just need an adapter, not a converter. Check the label. Hairdryers and electric shavers should be confirmed as dual-voltage before using.

### Tipping Customs

Tipping is deeply expected in Cuba's tourism sector and directly supports workers who earn very low state salaries:

| Situation | Suggested Tip |
|---|---|
| Restaurant (sit-down) | 10% (15% for excellent service) |
| Resort housekeeper | USD $1–$2/night, daily (not end of stay) |
| Resort porter/bellhop | USD $1–$2 per bag |
| Taxi driver | Round up or 10% |
| Tour guide | USD $5–$10/person/day |
| Resort buffet/bar staff | USD $1 at end of each visit |
| Musician/entertainer | USD $1–$2 per performance |
| Bathroom attendant (public) | USD $0.25–$0.50 |

- **Tip in paper bills** — coins cannot be exchanged and are essentially useless to Cuban recipients.
- USD $1 and USD $5 bills are ideal for day-to-day tipping. Euros and CAD are also appreciated.
- Budget an additional **USD $150–$200 per couple** specifically for tips over a 5-night stay.

### Health and Vaccination Notes

Consult a travel health clinic at least 4–6 weeks before departure. Recommended/considered vaccinations:
- **Hepatitis A:** Recommended; cases in returning Cuba travellers have been reported.
- **Typhoid:** Recommended, especially if eating outside resort.
- **Hepatitis B:** Recommended if not already immunized.
- **MMR (measles, mumps, rubella):** Ensure you are up to date; measles outbreaks are occurring globally.
- **Tetanus/diphtheria:** Booster if more than 10 years since last dose.
- Routine vaccinations (influenza, COVID-19) should be current.

**Active disease risks in Cuba (2025/2026):**
- **Chikungunya:** Active outbreak in Cuba. Spread by daytime-biting Aedes mosquitoes. Use DEET repellent, wear long sleeves at dawn/dusk.
- **Dengue fever:** Year-round risk. Same mosquito-prevention measures apply.
- **Zika virus:** Present; relevant for those who are pregnant or planning pregnancy.
- **Hepatitis A:** Transmitted via food/water; vaccination is the best protection.

Take mosquito precautions seriously — use 20–30% DEET repellent during outdoor activities, especially near standing water and in non-resort areas.

### 2SLGBTQI+ Travellers

Cuba has made significant legal strides in recent years:
- **Same-sex marriage** was legalized via a national referendum in September 2022, passed with approximately two-thirds approval.
- The 2022 Family Code also legalized same-sex adoption and recognition of common-law same-sex partnerships.
- In July 2025, Cuba passed legislation allowing gender marker changes in the civil registry without requiring surgery.
- Same-sex sexual activity is legal; gender affirmation surgery is available free of charge through Cuba's public healthcare system.
- **In practice:** Varadero is a resort destination where 2SLGBTQI+ couples travelling together are treated with the same courteous professionalism as any other guests. Same-sex couples are common and unremarkable at major all-inclusive resorts.
- **Outside resort areas:** Social attitudes are more traditional, particularly in rural areas and among older generations. Public displays of affection between same-sex couples may draw stares or comments. Most travellers report no incidents, but exercising some discretion outside the resort is practical.
- **Havana** has a visible 2SLGBTQI+ scene, particularly in the Vedado neighbourhood, along the Malecón, and at Playa Mi Cayito (popular with gay men). Havana Pride runs in May.
- The Canada travel advisory does not contain specific 2SLGBTQI+ safety advisories for Cuba.
