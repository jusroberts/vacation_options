## Dutch Side vs French Side

Sint Maarten / Saint Martin is divided by an open, unmarked border — crossing between sides requires no passport check, no stamps, and no formalities. The border is essentially a roadside sign. You can drive between the sides in 20–30 minutes.

### Key Differences

| | Dutch Side (Sint Maarten) | French Side (Saint Martin) |
|---|---|---|
| Capital | Philipsburg | Marigot |
| Currency | Caribbean Guilder (XCG, pegged to USD) | Euro (EUR) |
| Language | English (primary), Dutch | French (primary), English widely spoken |
| Voltage | 110V / 60Hz (North American Type A/B plugs) | 220V / 50Hz (European Type E plug) |
| Vibe | Lively, casino-heavy, duty-free shopping mecca | Chic, European, foodie-focused, quieter |
| Nightlife | 20+ casinos, beach bars, clubs | Low-key wine bars, bistros |
| Dining | Diverse and convenient; great casual food | World-class fine dining; Grand Case is the culinary capital |
| Beaches | Maho (plane-spotting), Mullet Bay, Cupecoy | Orient Bay, Happy Bay, Friar's Bay, Long Bay |
| Shopping | Duty-free on Front Street, Philipsburg | Boutiques in Marigot, French market |
| Cruise ships | Some dock in Philipsburg | Some dock in Marigot |

### Which Side to Stay On

**For a couple on their first visit**: The **Dutch side (Simpson Bay area)** is the most practical base. It's closest to the airport, near a wide range of restaurants and bars, has easier taxi and car access, and is duty-free for shopping. The French side is an easy 20–30 minute drive for meals and exploration.

**For foodies and romance seekers**: Consider the **French side** — particularly Grand Case or Orient Bay — for a quieter, more European atmosphere with exceptional dining right outside the door. You'll want a rental car if staying here.

**Best of both worlds**: Many experienced visitors stay on the Dutch side and spend their days driving over to the French side for meals and beaches, then return to the Dutch side for evening entertainment. The common local saying is: *"Eat in Saint Martin, party in Sint Maarten."*

### Crossing the Border

There are no border formalities whatsoever — just drive or walk through. The island functions as one destination for tourists. Look for the "Bienvenue à Saint-Martin" sign heading north and the "Welcome to Sint Maarten" sign heading south. Roads are in good condition; GPS works fine on both sides.

## Dining

Saint Martin has earned the title of "Culinary Capital of the Caribbean" — over 400 restaurants for an island of roughly 80,000 residents, representing cuisines from across the globe. The French side, especially Grand Case, is the culinary epicentre, but the Dutch side has no shortage of excellent and diverse options.

### Restaurants

**1. Le Cottage** | Grand Case, French Side
A flagship of Grand Case fine dining. Chef Laurent Cantineaux delivers elegant contemporary French cuisine in an intimate setting steps from the beach. Think seared foie gras, fresh lobster, and locally sourced fish prepared with precision. Reservations essential.
- Cuisine: French fine dining | Price: $$$$  (~$100–$150 CAD/person with wine)
- Known for: Consistently ranked among the island's top 3 restaurants

**2. La Villa Restaurant** | Grand Case, French Side
Helmed by Chef Bastian, trained at Joël Robuchon's and Anne-Sophie Pic's Michelin-starred kitchens. Creative French cuisine with Caribbean influences in a charming open-air setting in the heart of Grand Case village.
- Cuisine: French-Caribbean | Price: $$$$ (~$90–$130 CAD/person)
- Known for: Chef pedigree, impeccable tasting menus, romantic ambiance

**3. Grand Case Lobster Restaurant** | Grand Case, French Side
A Grand Case institution. Widely regarded as the island's best lobster, particularly the lobster thermidor. Also serves excellent grilled fish and French classics. Beach-adjacent with a relaxed upscale feel.
- Cuisine: Seafood/French | Price: $$$ (~$70–$100 CAD/person)
- Known for: Lobster thermidor, exceptional wine list, warm service

**4. Bistrot Caraïbes** | Grand Case, French Side
Family-run since 1994 by the Mezière brothers, this beloved bistro consistently ranks in the island's top 5. Relaxed, romantic, and casual — with expertly prepared Creole-French dishes at more approachable prices than some Grand Case neighbours.
- Cuisine: French-Creole | Price: $$$ (~$55–$85 CAD/person)
- Known for: Decades of consistency, loyal repeat clientele, warm atmosphere

**5. Les Galets** | Anse Marcel, French Side
Set around a sparkling pool in Anse Marcel bay. Chef Romuald blends French technique with island ingredients — refined cuisine in a chic tropical setting. One of the more scenic dining rooms on the island.
- Cuisine: French-Mediterranean | Price: $$$$ (~$80–$120 CAD/person)
- Known for: Pool-side setting, inventive presentations, romantic ambiance

**6. Ocean Lounge** | Philipsburg, Dutch Side
Located at the Holland House Beach Hotel on Great Bay. A lively boardwalk-facing restaurant and bar popular with locals and tourists alike. Live music on Fridays. Great for a casual evening meal or cocktails watching the sunset over Great Bay.
- Cuisine: International/Caribbean | Price: $$ (~$35–$60 CAD/person)
- Known for: Waterfront location, live music, excellent cocktails

**7. Sunset Beach Bar** | Maho Beach, Dutch Side
The legendary bar and restaurant right on Maho Beach with tables facing the runway. Solid pizza, burgers, wings, and local plates while you watch planes touch down meters overhead. Noisy, fun, and utterly unique.
- Cuisine: Casual American/International | Price: $$ (~$25–$45 CAD/person)
- Known for: The plane-spotting experience; flight schedules posted on chalkboards

**8. 978 Sanctorum Lounge** | Rambaud, French Side
An authentic Creole and Caribbean restaurant prepared with house-blended spices. A local favourite for those who want to eat away from tourist centres and experience real island flavours.
- Cuisine: Creole/Caribbean | Price: $–$$ (~$20–$40 CAD/person)
- Known for: Genuine local cuisine, unique spice blends, value for money

**9. La Chingona** | Cupecoy, Dutch Side
California-style Mexican near the Cupecoy Beach area. Excellent tacos, burritos, margaritas, and a lively bar scene. A popular spot for a more casual, fun evening on the Dutch side.
- Cuisine: Mexican-California | Price: $$ (~$30–$55 CAD/person)
- Known for: Best tacos on the island, strong cocktails, casual beach-town atmosphere

### Street Food and Local Markets

- **Lolos (Grand Case)**: Grand Case's "Restaurant Row" (Boulevard de Grand Case) includes a series of informal open-air BBQ shacks called *lolos*, which are among the best casual eating experiences on the island. Order grilled chicken, ribs, whole fish, or local sides (rice and beans, plantains, johnny cakes) for $10–$20 USD. Open evenings — arrive early as popular ones sell out.
- **Marigot Market (French Side)**: The open-air market near Marigot's waterfront is lively on Wednesday and Saturday mornings. Fresh tropical produce, Creole spices, local rums, and crafts. Great for picking up provisions.
- **Great Bay Boardwalk (Philipsburg)**: Food trucks and casual eateries along the 1.5 km boardwalk on Great Bay serve johnny cakes, fresh fish, and cold Carib beer at local prices.

| Philipsburg (Dutch side, Divi etc.) | ~$20 USD |

- Fares increase 25% after 22:00 and double after midnight until 05:00.
- Additional luggage beyond one bag per passenger may incur a $5 USD surcharge.
- Tip 10–15%.
- Contact Airport Taxi Association: +1-721-542-1681

**Car rental** is strongly recommended if staying more than 2 nights, especially if you want to explore both sides freely. Rates start around $30–$35 USD/day with major agencies (Avis, Budget, Hertz) and local operators (SAX, Leisure) at the airport. Driving is on the right-hand side on both sides of the island. Roads are generally in good condition, well-signed in English, and GPS works throughout.

### Getting Around the Island

- **Rental car**: Best option for flexibility. The entire island can be circumnavigated in about 1.5–2 hours. Parking is generally available at beaches and attractions.
- **Taxis**: Fixed-rate zones, no meters. Always confirm the fare before getting in. Book through your hotel or call the taxi associations. Uber and Lyft do not operate on the island.
- **Public minibuses**: Extremely cheap ($1–$3 USD per trip) and operated as shared route taxis. Main hubs at Backstreet in Philipsburg and Simpson Bay. Useful for getting between Simpson Bay, Maho, and Cole Bay. Less practical for the French side.
- **Water taxis**: Available between select beach areas and marinas.

### Day Trips

**Anguilla (30–45 min by ferry)**
A pristine British Overseas Territory with some of the finest beaches in the Caribbean and ultra-luxury resorts.
- Public ferry from Marigot port (French side): departs roughly 8 times daily, 20-min crossing, ~$30 USD one-way + €7 departure tax. Cash only; no advance booking.
- Private charter from the Dutch side (near airport): ~$65 USD one-way, runs ~09:00–17:00.
- Entry: Passport required. Anguilla charges an $11 USD port tax for day visitors.
- Note: Updated shuttle schedules from February 1, 2026 — check [portdemarigot.com](https://www.portdemarigot.com/en/gare) before you go.

**St. Barths (45–75 min by ferry)**
The French island of exclusivity — stunning beaches, Michelin-level dining, celebrity yachts.
- Great Bay Express (from Philipsburg/Bobby's Marina): up to 3 daily departures, 45-min crossing, ~€60–€70 one-way.
- Voyager Ferry (from Marigot): daily, 60-min crossing, AC cabins and sun deck.
- Entry: ID card sufficient (no passport needed for French territory). Book ahead at [voy12.com](https://voy12.com).

**Pinel Island (French side)**
A tiny, gorgeous uninhabited islet reachable by short water taxi from Orient Bay. No cars, no stress — just beach, snorkelling, and seafood. ~$7 USD round-trip water taxi.

## Attractions

### 1. Maho Beach Plane-Spotting
**Location**: Maho, Dutch Side (adjacent to SXM airport) | **Admission**: Free

The world's most famous beach for aircraft spotting — incoming planes from Air Canada, KLM, American Airlines, and others pass at rooftop height directly over the beach during approach. The experience is genuinely visceral: wind blast, jet noise, and aircraft within touching distance.
- **Best time**: Early-to-mid afternoon when larger widebody aircraft arrive. Check the flight schedule boards posted at Sunset Beach Bar.
- **Tips**: Avoid cruise-ship days for smaller crowds. Position yourself at the eastern end of the beach for the best approach view. The beach itself is beautiful and swimmable.
- **Nearby**: Sunset Beach Bar and Guy's Driftwood Boat Bar for food and drinks.

### 2. Orient Bay (Orient Beach)
**Location**: Orient Bay, French Side | **Admission**: Free

The island's longest and most celebrated beach — a 2 km arc of white sand and turquoise water lined with beach bars, watersports operators, and restaurants. The beach has a clothed section and a naturist (clothing-optional) section at the southern end (Club Orient area).
- **Activities**: Jet skis, parasailing, sailboat rentals, paddleboarding, snorkelling
- **Tips**: Arrive before 10:00 for the best sun chairs. Wednesday and Saturday mornings are calmer (fewer cruise ships). Excellent eats at beach bars such as Kontiki and Waikiki.

### 3. Grand Case Village — Restaurant Row
**Location**: Grand Case, French Side | **Admission**: Free

Boulevard de Grand Case is lined with over 30 restaurants — fine dining, bistros, and *lolos* (BBQ shacks) — making an evening stroll and meal here a highlight of any SXM visit. The village has a charming, low-key French Caribbean feel.
- **Best visited**: Friday and Saturday evenings when restaurants are at their liveliest.

### 4. Philipsburg Boardwalk and Front Street
**Location**: Philipsburg, Dutch Side | **Admission**: Free

The Dutch capital stretches along Great Bay between two lagoons. The 1.5 km boardwalk fronts a string of restaurants, bars, and beach vendors. Front Street is the duty-free shopping hub — jewellery, watches, electronics, liquor, and designer goods at up to 30% off.
- **Hours**: Shops roughly 09:00–18:00; some stay open for cruise ship visits.

### 5. Loterie Farm
**Location**: Pic Paradis, French Side | **Admission**: ~$15–$20 USD for grounds access; activities extra

A 135-acre nature reserve on the slopes of Pic Paradis (the island's highest point at 424 m). Offers hiking trails through secondary rainforest, a spring-fed swimming pool (The Hidden Forest Pool), zip lines, and the swanky Treehouse restaurant/bar.
- **Hours**: Typically 09:00–18:00 daily; check [loteriefarm.com](https://www.loteriefarm.com).
- **Best for**: Active couples who want a break from the beach.

### 6. Rainforest Adventures Zipline
**Location**: Near Loterie Farm, French Side | **Admission**: ~$75–$95 USD/person

Claims the steepest zipline in the world (42% gradient) plus a gondola lift to the treetop canopy. The gondola ride alone offers panoramic views over both sides of the island.
- **Hours**: Tuesday–Sunday, 09:00–15:00; last ride varies. Advance booking recommended.

### 7. Marigot Market and Fort St. Louis
**Location**: Marigot, French Side | **Admission**: Fort free / Market free

Marigot is the charming French capital of Saint Martin. The Wednesday and Saturday morning market beside the waterfront sells local produce, spices, crafts, and French cheeses. Fort St. Louis above the town offers panoramic views over Simpson Bay Lagoon and Anguilla — a 10-minute walk up from the waterfront.
- **Market hours**: Wednesday and Saturday ~07:00–13:00.

### 8. Underwater World / Snorkelling at Little Bay
**Location**: Little Bay, near Divi Resort, Dutch Side | **Admission**: Varies by operator

The waters off Little Bay contain a deliberately sunk helicopter and small aircraft, creating an artificial reef that has attracted coral and marine life. Popular with snorkellers and divers. Several operators offer guided snorkel/dive tours.
- **Tip**: Guests at Divi Little Bay can access this from the resort beach.

### 9. Rusty Parrot Rum Experience
**Location**: Dutch Side | **Admission**: ~$60–$80 USD/person

Interactive rum-making class where you learn about Caribbean rum production and blend your own bottle to take home. A fun, hands-on experience suited to couples.

### 10. Cupecoy Beach
**Location**: Cupecoy, Dutch Side | **Admission**: Free

One of the island's most dramatic beaches — framed by terracotta limestone cliffs and natural sea caves with calm, clear water in sheltered coves. Also known as the island's LGBTQ+ and clothing-optional friendly beach. No facilities on the beach itself; bring everything you need.

## Safety

### Canada Travel Advisory
The Government of Canada advises travellers to **exercise a high degree of caution** in both Sint Maarten (Dutch) and Saint Martin (French) due to crime. This is the second-tier advisory level — it does not warn against travel, but does indicate identifiable safety and security concerns.

- Sint Maarten advisory: [travel.gc.ca/destinations/sint-maarten](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/sint-maarten)
- Saint Martin advisory: [travel.gc.ca/destinations/saint-martin](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/saint-martin)

### Hurricane Irma Recovery (2017)
Hurricane Irma (Category 5, September 2017) caused an estimated $2.73 billion in damage to Sint Maarten, affecting 90% of the island's infrastructure. As of 2025–2026, recovery is essentially complete for tourists:
- Princess Juliana International Airport has been fully rebuilt to hurricane-resilient standards with $122 million in investment.
- Roads, restaurants, beach bars, and hotels are operational and fully restored.
- Vegetation has regrown. Only occasional private homes remain unrepaired — a minor background detail.
- A Sint Maarten Reconstruction Trust Fund of $519 million (World Bank-administered) continues resilience upgrades through 2028, including a new hospital and improved building standards.

### Neighbourhood Safety Notes

**Generally safe for tourists**:
- Simpson Bay and Maho (Dutch side) — busy tourist zones with high foot traffic; standard urban caution applies
- Grand Case (French side) — quiet, upscale village; very low incident rate
- Orient Bay (French side) — popular beach area; watch belongings on the beach
- Marigot waterfront (French side) — exercise caution after dark away from the main lit areas

**Exercise more caution**:
- Parts of Philipsburg away from the boardwalk and Front Street, particularly at night
- Remote or poorly-lit areas on either side after dark

### Practical Safety Tips
- Crimes of opportunity (pickpocketing, bag snatching) are the primary concern, especially at beaches and near cruise ship docks. Never leave valuables unattended on the beach.
- Do not leave bags or electronics visible in a rental car; break-ins do occur.
- Avoid ATMs after dark; use machines in hotel lobbies or shopping centres during daylight hours.
- Road safety: some local drivers are aggressive; secondary roads can be narrow. Drive defensively.
- Moped/scooter rentals: traffic can be chaotic; not recommended unless experienced with Caribbean driving conditions.
- Purchase comprehensive travel insurance including medical evacuation coverage — Sint Maarten has limited medical facilities and serious cases are evacuated to Puerto Rico or the US.
- Carry a copy of your passport; keep the original in the hotel safe.

## Practical Info

### April Weather
Late April is one of the best times to visit. Sint Maarten is just past peak dry season and conditions are excellent:

- **Temperature**: Daytime highs around 29–30°C (85–86°F); evening lows around 23–24°C (74–75°F)
- **Sea temperature**: ~26°C (80°F) — ideal for swimming
- **Sunshine**: ~9 hours per day; March and April are the sunniest months
- **Rainfall**: One of the driest months (~65 mm total, ~8–12 days with some rain). Showers are typically short downpours; the sun returns quickly
- **Humidity**: ~70–74%; feels warm but trade winds from the northeast provide relief
- **UV Index**: High. Apply SPF 30+ every 2 hours; limit midday sun exposure (10:00–15:00)
- **Crowds**: Shoulder season — peak crowds (January–March) have eased; prices are moderate; weather is still excellent. April is a sweet spot.

### Currency

| | Dutch Side (Sint Maarten) | French Side (Saint Martin) |
|---|---|---|
| Official currency | Caribbean Guilder (XCG) — replaced ANG; pegged to USD at ~1.79:1 | Euro (EUR) |
| Most practical currency | **US Dollar (USD)** accepted everywhere | USD widely accepted; change sometimes returned in EUR |
| Credit cards | Widely accepted at hotels, restaurants, shops | Widely accepted; some small vendors cash-only |
| CAD | Not accepted; convert to USD before travel |

**Practical advice**: Carry USD cash for taxis, lolos, market purchases, and smaller vendors. You do not need to carry Guilders or Euros — USD works across the entire island. Bring some small bills ($5, $10, $20). CAD is not accepted anywhere; convert at home or withdraw USD from ATMs on the island.

**ATMs**: Available throughout both sides. Use bank-affiliated ATMs (RBC, Windward Islands Bank) rather than standalone machines to minimize fees.

### Electricity / Voltage

| | Dutch Side | French Side |
|---|---|---|
| Voltage | 110V / 60Hz | 220V / 50Hz |
| Plug type | Type A/B (North American — same as Canada) | Type E (European, round 2-pin) |
| Canadian plugs | **Work without an adapter** | **Need an adapter and possibly a converter** |

If staying exclusively on the Dutch side, your Canadian electronics work natively. If staying on the French side or crossing frequently, bring a universal power adapter. Most modern electronics (phones, laptops, cameras) are dual-voltage (100–240V) and only need a plug adapter; check your device's power brick for voltage range.

### Duty-Free Shopping
The Dutch side of Sint Maarten is a major duty-free destination — savings of up to 30% on:
- Jewellery (the island is world-famous; Philipsburg has high-end jewellery stores)
- Watches and luxury goods
- Electronics
- Liquor, rum, and wine
- Perfume and cosmetics
- Cigars

**Front Street, Philipsburg** is the main duty-free strip. The Maho area also has shopping centres.

**Canadian customs note**: As a Canadian returning from the Caribbean after more than 7 days, you are entitled to a **CAD $800 personal exemption** duty-free (goods must accompany you). Up to 1.14 litres of alcohol and 200 cigarettes are also included. Declare all goods at the CBSA kiosk at YYZ upon arrival.

### 2SLGBTQI+ Notes
Sint Maarten / Saint Martin is one of the most LGBTQ+-welcoming destinations in the Caribbean, owing to its French and Dutch governance:
- **French Side**: Same-sex marriage legal since 2013 (French law applies); homosexuality decriminalized since 1791.
- **Dutch Side**: Homosexuality has never been criminalized; same-sex marriage not yet legal (unlike the Netherlands itself), but the 2012 Criminal Code prohibits discrimination on the basis of sexual orientation.
- Social atmosphere: Generally tolerant and cosmopolitan ("live and let live" mentality); more economically than socially progressive in some local circles. Overt discrimination toward tourists is very rare.
- **Cupecoy Beach** is the established LGBTQ+-friendly beach (clothing-optional, Dutch side); **Happy Bay** and **Orient Bay** are also welcoming.
- The local LGBTQ+ organization is **Sint Maarten Alliance for Equality (SAFE)**; [gaysintmaarten.com](https://gaysintmaarten.com) has tourist resources.
- PinkNews has listed St. Maarten among the top LGBTQ+-friendly Caribbean destinations.
- Same-sex couples will encounter no issues at hotels, restaurants, or beaches.

### Health and Vaccination Notes
No vaccinations are required for entry to Sint Maarten / Saint Martin. The following are **recommended** by the Government of Canada and the CDC:
- Routine vaccinations: ensure MMR (measles), tetanus-diphtheria-pertussis, chickenpox, polio, and annual flu vaccine are up to date
- Hepatitis A (food/water exposure risk)
- Hepatitis B (if relevant)
- Typhoid (if eating at informal food establishments)
- COVID-19 (per current guidelines)

**Mosquito-borne illness**: Dengue fever, chikungunya, and Zika virus are present. Use DEET-based insect repellent, especially during dusk and dawn. Zika is a concern for pregnant travellers or those planning pregnancy — consult your doctor before travel.

**Medical facilities**: Sint Maarten has basic hospital care (St. Maarten Medical Center, Cay Hill). Serious cases are evacuated to Puerto Rico or Miami. **Comprehensive travel insurance with medical evacuation coverage is strongly recommended.** Consult your travel health clinic 4–6 weeks before departure.

**Water**: Tap water is generally safe to drink on the Dutch side; the French side typically uses desalinated water, also safe. Bottled water is widely available.
