### Street Food & Local Market Tips
- **La Bandera Dominicana** — the national dish (white rice, stewed red beans, meat, and fried sweet plantains) is available for under $5 USD at local comedores (casual Dominican lunch counters). Look for them in the El Cortecito and Los Corales neighbourhoods.
- **Presidente beer** (~$2–3 USD local price) is the Dominican Republic's flagship lager — cold, crisp, and ubiquitous at beach bars.
- **Mamajuana** — a unique Dominican spirit made from tree bark and herbs soaked in rum, red wine, and honey; tastes like a potent cinnamon-spiced rum. Try it at local bars and rum shops.
- The **El Cortecito fishing village area** within Bávaro is the best zone for local restaurants, souvenir markets, and independent shops outside resort walls.
- Avoid drinking tap water anywhere in the Dominican Republic; bottled water is cheap and widely available.

## Transportation

### Getting from PUJ Airport to Hotel Areas

Punta Cana International Airport (PUJ) is conveniently located — most resort zones are within 15–60 minutes by road.

| Option | Destination | Estimated Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Official Airport Taxi | Bávaro / Barceló / Meliá resorts | ~$30 USD (~$43 CAD) | Fixed rates, no meter — ask to see rate card on entry |
| Official Airport Taxi | Cap Cana resorts | ~$30 USD (~$43 CAD) | Same fixed zone |
| Official Airport Taxi | Cabeza de Toro (Dreams, Hyatt) | ~$30 USD (~$43 CAD) | 20–25 min drive |
| Official Airport Taxi | Uvero Alto (Excellence, etc.) | ~$63 USD (~$90 CAD) | ~45–60 min drive north |
| Pre-booked Private Transfer | Any resort | from ~$38–55 USD (~$54–79 CAD) one-way | Driver meets you at Arrivals; recommended for peace of mind |
| Resort shuttle | Varies | Free–$20 USD | Many all-inclusive resorts offer paid or free transfers — confirm when booking |

**Important taxi tips:**
- Only take taxis from the **official rank outside Arrivals** — do not accept rides offered by touts inside the terminal.
- Taxis do not have meters; fares are fixed by zone. Always confirm the price before departing.
- Pay in USD or DOP (Dominican Pesos) — USD is universally accepted.
- Pre-booking a transfer online provides a fixed price and a driver waiting with a name sign; recommended for first-time visitors.

### Getting Around Punta Cana

- **Within all-inclusive resorts:** Most large resorts use golf carts or internal shuttles (e.g., Hard Rock). Walking between areas is common at smaller properties.
- **Between resort zones:** Taxis are the most practical option; negotiate fares before boarding. A cross-town trip (e.g., Bávaro to Cap Cana) costs ~$30–50 USD.
- **Local guagua (public bus):** Converted school buses that connect villages and resort zones for around $1–2 USD. Very cheap, sometimes crowded and slow — an adventure, not a convenience.
- **Rental car:** Available at the airport (Avis, Budget, Hertz, National). A compact car runs ~$50–90 USD/day. Driving is on the **right side of the road**. Useful if you plan multiple day trips. Note: roads outside resort zones can be poorly lit and unmarked.
- **Moto-concho (motorcycle taxi):** Common locally for short hops; not generally recommended for tourists due to road safety.

## Attractions

### 1. Bávaro Beach (Playa Bávaro)
- **What it is:** The #1 tourist attraction in Punta Cana — a 12-mile stretch of powdery white sand and turquoise water running from Macao to Cabeza de Toro.
- **Hours:** Beaches are accessible year-round; public access points at Bibijagua, El Cortecito, Los Corales, and Jellyfish Restaurant
- **Admission:** Free at public access points
- **Tips:** The sand famously does not heat up in the sun — you can walk barefoot in midday. Water sports (jet skis, parasailing, kitesurfing) available from beach vendors. A **Blue Flag**–certified section exists at Arena Gorda.

### 2. Saona Island (Isla Saona)
- **What it is:** A stunning uninhabited island within the East National Park (Parque Nacional del Este); the most popular full-day excursion from Punta Cana.
- **Hours:** Tours depart daily, typically 8 AM; return by 5–6 PM
- **Admission:** Included in tour price (~$50–130 USD / ~$72–186 CAD per person depending on operator and package); entrance to national park is included
- **Tips:** Speedboat trips include a stop at a natural shallow-water swimming pool in the middle of the ocean — a highlight. Catamaran trips have more music and social energy. Book in advance during late April.

### 3. Scape Park — Hoyo Azul (Blue Hole) & Cave Networks
- **What it is:** A natural theme park in Cap Cana featuring Hoyo Azul — a stunning turquoise cenote (natural limestone sinkhole) at the base of a 61-metre cliff, plus caves, zip lines, and an eco-park.
- **Hours:** Daily 9 AM – 5 PM
- **Admission:** Full park access ~$45–60 USD (~$64–86 CAD); swimming in the Blue Hole included
- **Tips:** Arrive early — the Blue Hole is more crowd-free in the morning. Bring water shoes; the path down is rocky.

### 4. Indigenous Eyes Ecological Reserve (Ojos Indígenas)
- **What it is:** A subtropical forest reserve within Puntacana Resort & Club containing 14 freshwater lagoons; swimming is allowed in 4 of them. Over 500 plant species, 36% endemic.
- **Hours:** Daily 8 AM – 5 PM
- **Admission:** ~$40 USD (~$57 CAD) for guests of Puntacana Resort & Club hotels; ~$80 USD (~$114 CAD) for external visitors. Guided tours available.
- **Tips:** Best visited on a calm, hot day when the swim in the lagoons is most rewarding.

### 5. Dune Buggy Adventure
- **What it is:** The second most popular excursion in Punta Cana — off-road buggy driving through sugar cane fields, farming villages, cocoa and coffee plantations, and a cenote cave swim.
- **Hours:** Half-day tours depart mornings (~8 AM) and afternoons (~1 PM)
- **Admission:** ~$75–120 USD (~$107–172 CAD) per person; hotel pickup usually included
- **Tips:** You'll get dusty and wet — bring a change of clothes and a waterproof phone case. Driving your own buggy (rather than riding as passenger) is more fun and usually included in the price.

### 6. Macao Beach
- **What it is:** One of the few public beaches in the region not dominated by hotels; known for powerful surf, dramatic wave breaks, and local atmosphere.
- **Hours:** Open daily
- **Admission:** Free
- **Tips:** Surfing lessons and board rentals are available from local operators. Less developed than Bávaro — bring your own snacks. A 20–30 min taxi ride north of the main resort zone.

### 7. ChocoMuseo (Chocolate Museum)
- **What it is:** A working artisanal chocolate factory and museum tracing Dominican cacao from bean to bar; the Dominican Republic is one of the world's top producers of fine-flavour cacao.
- **Hours:** Daily 9 AM – 8 PM
- **Admission:** Museum entry is low-cost (~$5–10 USD / ~$7–14 CAD); chocolate-making workshops available (~$20–30 USD)
- **Tips:** Located in the Bávaro commercial zone — easy to combine with shopping or dinner.

### 8. Los Haitises National Park
- **What it is:** A UNESCO-protected ecological reserve featuring dramatic limestone karst formations, mangrove forests, Taíno cave paintings, and diverse birdlife. The caves here were used as locations in the original Jurassic Park.
- **Hours:** Day trips depart from Punta Cana hotels daily
- **Admission:** Included in guided tour prices (~$90–150 USD / ~$129–215 CAD per person, including transport, guide, lunch, and national park fees)
- **Tips:** A long but rewarding full-day excursion (~10–12 hours total). Bring insect repellent and sun protection. Best for nature lovers and history enthusiasts.

### 9. La Cana Golf Club
- **What it is:** A 27-hole championship course within Puntacana Resort & Club designed by P.B. Dye; ranked the #1 golf course in the Caribbean by Golf Magazine.
- **Hours:** Daily from 7 AM
- **Admission:** Green fees vary ~$100–200 USD (~$143–286 CAD); carts and caddies additional. Book via the resort.
- **Tips:** 14 of the 27 holes offer ocean views. The course plays along the Caribbean coastline — spectacular for a sunrise round.

### 10. Coco Bongo Punta Cana (Entertainment)
- **What it is:** Punta Cana's most famous nightlife attraction — a Las Vegas–style dinner show and nightclub combining acrobatics, impersonators, movie tributes, live music, and a packed dance floor.
- **Hours:** Doors open ~10 PM; shows run 10:30 PM – 3:30 AM (hotel pickup from ~7 PM)
- **Admission:** General entry with 5 drinks from ~$50–80 USD (~$72–114 CAD); Gold/VIP packages with open bar and reserved seating from ~$100–150 USD (~$143–215 CAD)
- **Tips:** Book the Gold package to secure a seat — standing general admission can be exhausting for a 5-hour show. Hotel roundtrip transportation is usually included. Book in advance — it sells out regularly.

## Beaches

### Playa Bávaro
Punta Cana's centrepiece beach: 12 miles of ultra-fine white sand that stays cool underfoot even in direct sun, shallow turquoise water ideal for swimming, and a fringe of coconut palms. The beach runs through most of the main resort zone and fronts dozens of all-inclusive properties. Public access is available at four points — Bibijagua, El Cortecito, Los Corales, and Jellyfish Restaurant — though resort sections are reserved for guests. Facilities at public access points include restaurants, beach bars, and restrooms. Water sports vendors operate along the beach. Getting there: walk from most Bávaro/Cortecito hotels; taxi from Cap Cana (~$20–30 USD).

### Playa Juanillo (Cap Cana)
The most exclusive beach in Punta Cana — located inside the upscale Cap Cana development, offering pristine powdery sand, minimal crowds, and exceptional clarity of water. A $20 USD entry fee (redeemable at the Juanillo Beach Club restaurant and bar) applies for day visitors. Sun loungers, beach service, restrooms, and parking are available. Snorkelling at Buccaneers Reef offshore is available via organized boat tours. Getting there: taxi from Bávaro (~$25–30 USD).

### Macao Beach (Playa Macao)
A wild, undeveloped public beach about 20 minutes north of the main resort zone — dramatic waves, dark volcanic rock features, and strong surf make it the most exciting beach in the region for active travellers. Popular with surfers and local Dominican families on weekends. Small beach restaurant and water sports rental on site. Getting there: taxi from Bávaro (~$20–25 USD); some resort buggy tours pass through here.

### Playa Arena Gorda
A wide, Blue Flag–certified beach running in front of several major hotel complexes including Hard Rock, Iberostar, Majestic, RIU, and Bahia Principe. The beach benefits from excellent water quality and a lifeguard on duty. More waves and wind than the calmer inner Bávaro section. Mostly accessible only to guests of the adjacent resorts (no public parking). Best accessed by staying at one of the oceanfront properties or via a paid beach club day pass.

### El Cortecito / Los Corales
The most accessible public beach in the Bávaro zone — a small former fishing village where the beach merges into a busy strip of local restaurants, bars, souvenir shops, and markets. No entry restriction; vendors offer sun loungers and umbrellas for rent. Perfect for a half-day combining beach time with exploring local shops and having lunch at a non-resort restaurant. Getting there: easily walkable from most central Bávaro hotels.

### Playa Blanca (Puntacana Resort)
A sheltered, manicured beach within the private Puntacana Resort & Club. Romantic and well-maintained, with calm waters and fewer crowds than Bávaro. Day visitor access is available through a paid pass at the Visitor Centre (cost converted as a restaurant credit at Playa Blanca Beach Restaurant). Getting there: taxi from central Bávaro (~$15–20 USD).

## Nightlife & Entertainment

**Coco Bongo Punta Cana** — The crown jewel of Punta Cana nightlife. This epic entertainment venue blends a live stage show with a nightclub: acrobatics, movie tributes (The Mask, 300, Moulin Rouge, Queen), celebrity impersonators, and a non-stop dance floor. Performances run for roughly 5 hours. Gold packages include premium open bar and reserved seating. Hotel roundtrip transportation is usually bundled. Book ahead — it frequently sells out. Location: Downtown Punta Cana, between the airport and Bávaro. Cost: ~$50–150 USD (~$72–215 CAD) depending on package.

**ORO Nightclub (Hard Rock Hotel)** — The Hard Rock's in-house nightclub is one of the most polished nightlife experiences in the resort zone, with top DJs, live performances, and a sophisticated party atmosphere. Open to hotel guests and outside guests. Hard Rock hotel guests have built-in access. Cover/entry: ~$50–100 USD (~$72–143 CAD) depending on the night.

**Imagine Punta Cana** — A large nightclub built into a natural cave system with multiple rooms, each with its own musical style (reggaeton, electronic, salsa). Entry includes an open bar. An iconic and unique experience for late-night dancing. Location: near Bávaro. Entry: ~$30–60 USD (~$43–86 CAD).

**Jewel Punta Cana** — A smaller, more intimate nightclub known for a welcoming and inclusive atmosphere. Popular with younger travellers and LGBTQ+ visitors. Located in central Bávaro.

**El Cortecito & Los Corales bars** — For a more low-key evening, the El Cortecito strip has open-air bars with live merengue and bachata music, cold Presidente beers at local prices, and a relaxed neighbourhood vibe. A good option for a first evening after arrival.

**Chic Cabaret & Restaurant** — If you prefer dinner-with-a-show, Chic Cabaret combines a high-energy stage show with your meal. Formal dress code applies for men (no shorts; closed shoes required). Located within the CHIC resort.

**Beach Club Sunset Bars** — Several beachfront bars in the Bávaro zone offer fire shows, live music, and sunset cocktails. Jellyfish Restaurant's beach bar is particularly popular for early evenings.

## Safety

### Canada Travel Advisory
As of March 2026, the Government of Canada advises travellers to **exercise increased caution** in the Dominican Republic (Level 2 — the same advisory level applied to France, Italy, Spain, and Mexico). This reflects general crime levels in the country, not a specific risk to tourist areas. The full advisory is at [travel.gc.ca/destinations/dominican-republic](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/dominican-republic).

### Punta Cana Resort Zone — Generally Safe for Tourists
The Punta Cana resort zone (Bávaro, Cap Cana, Uvero Alto) has a significantly lower crime rate than the national average. Tourism represents ~80% of the local economy and the government maintains a **24/7 POLITUR (Tourist Police)** presence throughout beach areas, hotels, and nightlife zones. Less than 1% of tourists report any security incident. Violent crime against tourists in resort areas is rare.

### Area-Specific Notes
- **Resort zones (Bávaro, Cap Cana, Arena Gorda):** Considered safe. Stick to hotel beaches and licensed excursions.
- **El Cortecito / Los Corales:** Lively local area that is generally safe by day; stay aware of your surroundings and keep valuables out of sight after dark.
- **Santo Domingo (day trip):** The Zona Colonial is a safe tourist zone, but travel with your licensed tour guide and avoid venturing into unguided areas.
- **Downtown Punta Cana city centre:** Slightly less polished than resort zones — use the same urban caution you would anywhere.
- **Avoid:** Poorly lit roads and beaches after dark without a group; unmarked taxi drivers inside the airport arrivals hall; accepting rides from strangers.

### Practical Safety Tips
- Use only **licensed taxis from the official airport rank** or pre-booked transfers. No Uber in Punta Cana.
- Keep your passport in your hotel safe; carry a photocopy or digital copy.
- Be cautious with alcohol — there have been past reports of adulterated spirits at local bars. Stick to sealed bottles and resort bars if in doubt.
- Use **indoor ATMs** inside hotel lobbies or bank branches; avoid standalone street ATMs at night.
- Beware of **"pirate lawyers"** (unlicensed individuals who offer legal services near police stations at inflated rates).
- Avoid public Wi-Fi for banking transactions — cybercrime and card skimming occur.
- Book excursions through your resort concierge or well-reviewed operators (Viator, GetYourGuide) — not from unsolicited vendors approaching you on the beach.
- Travel insurance with **medical evacuation** coverage is strongly recommended. Ambulance service in Punta Cana is more reliable than in rural areas, but emergency care infrastructure is below Canadian standards.

Late April marks the transition from the dry season to the beginning of the rainy season. Rainfall typically arrives as **brief afternoon tropical showers** that pass within an hour — mornings are almost always clear and sunny. UV levels are extreme and sunburn can occur within 10 minutes of unprotected midday exposure. SPF 50+ sunscreen, a wide-brimmed hat, and UV-protective sunglasses are essential. Trade winds help make the humidity feel comfortable.

**What to pack:** Lightweight breathable clothing, multiple swimsuits, SPF 50+ reef-safe sunscreen, a hat, sunglasses, a light rain jacket or travel umbrella, insect repellent (DEET-based for evenings and jungle excursions), comfortable walking sandals, and a waterproof phone case.

### Electricity & Voltage
- **Voltage:** 110 V / 60 Hz — identical to Canada and the US
- **Plugs:** Type A (2 flat pins) and Type B (2 flat pins + 1 round grounding pin) — Canadian plugs work without any adapter
- Power outages ("apagones") can occur outside resort areas, but all major resorts operate backup generators ensuring uninterrupted power.

### Currency
- **Official currency:** Dominican Peso (DOP)
- Exchange rate (approximate, March 2026): 1 USD ≈ 58–60 DOP; 1 CAD ≈ 40–42 DOP
- **USD is widely accepted** in hotel zones, restaurants, taxis, and tour operators; DOP is preferred for local markets, comedores, and small shops.
- For the best exchange rates, use bank ATMs (Banco Popular, Scotiabank, Banco BHD) or official exchange offices (casas de cambio) — avoid exchanging at the airport or through your hotel.
- Credit cards (Visa, Mastercard) are accepted at all resorts and most restaurants and tour operators.
- Carry **small USD bills** ($1, $5, $10, $20) — useful for tipping, taxis, and beach vendors.
- Canadian dollars are generally **not directly accepted** at businesses — convert to USD or DOP before or on arrival.

### Tipping Customs
Dominican service workers rely significantly on tips; wages in hospitality are low. A 10% *Propina Legal* (legal service charge) is automatically added to most restaurant bills, but this rarely reaches the workers who serve you — an additional tip is customary and appreciated.

| Service | Recommended Tip |
|---|---|
| Restaurant (on top of Propina Legal) | 10–15% additional |
| Bar (per drink) | $1–2 USD |
| Housekeeping (per day) | $2–4 USD / day (leave daily, not just at checkout) |
| Porter (luggage) | $1–2 USD per bag |
| Taxi driver | Round up the fare; ~10% for longer trips |
| Excursion guide | 10–20% of tour cost; more if exceptional |
| All-inclusive resort staff | Check resort policy — some prohibit cash tips; many allow them discreetly |

Tip in USD (preferred) or DOP. Always mark cash as "propina" to avoid confusion.

### Health & Vaccination Notes
No vaccinations are **mandatory** for Canadians entering the Dominican Republic for tourism (exception: proof of Yellow Fever vaccination required if arriving from a Yellow Fever–endemic country such as Brazil).

**Recommended (consult your travel medicine clinic at least 4–6 weeks before departure):**
- Hepatitis A (routine for all travellers; spread through food/water)
- Typhoid (especially if eating outside resort kitchens or at street food stalls)
- Hepatitis B (if not already vaccinated)
- Routine vaccines: COVID-19, influenza, Tdap, MMR, chickenpox — ensure up to date
- **Malaria:** Risk in La Altagracia Province (where Punta Cana is located) is present but low; primarily concentrated near the Haitian border. Discuss antimalarial medication with your doctor if planning rural travel. Use DEET-based repellent in the evenings.
- **Zika:** Ongoing risk; travellers who are pregnant or trying to conceive should review CDC guidance before booking.
- Avoid swimming in fresh, unchlorinated water (lakes, rivers, streams) due to risk of schistosomiasis and leptospirosis.
- Drink only bottled or filtered water; avoid ice outside of resort kitchens.

**Travel insurance with medical evacuation coverage is strongly recommended.** Emergency care availability in Punta Cana is better than rural areas but below Canadian standards. The nearest well-equipped private hospitals are in Santo Domingo (~2 hours away).

### 2SLGBTQI+ Notes
The Dominican Republic does not criminalize same-sex relationships; however, the country is culturally conservative with a strong religious influence, and public attitudes toward LGBTQ+ people remain less tolerant than in Canada.

**Within international resort areas** — Bávaro, Cap Cana, and the major hotel zones — the atmosphere is generally open and welcoming to 2SLGBTQI+ travellers. Major resort brands (Iberostar, Hard Rock, Lopesan, Excellence) are LGBTQ-inclusive and some hold TAG Approved or IGLTA membership certifications. Nightlife venues like Coco Bongo, Imagine Punta Cana, and Jewel are described as inclusive environments.

**Outside resort zones** — in local neighbourhoods, villages, and non-tourist areas — exercise more discretion. Public displays of same-sex affection may attract unwanted attention and should be avoided outside cosmopolitan tourist settings. Use hookup and dating apps with caution in unfamiliar areas; meet people in public spaces first.

The Government of Canada specifically notes that 2SLGBTQI+ persons may face harassment in some contexts even where no legal concerns apply. Review Canada's LGBTQ+ travel guidance at [travel.gc.ca/travelling/health-safety/lgbt-travel](https://travel.gc.ca/travelling/health-safety/lgbt-travel).
