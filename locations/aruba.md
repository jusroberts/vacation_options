### Aruban Specialties to Try

- **Keshi yena** — Aruba's national dish: a savoury baked casserole of seasoned chicken (or seafood) loaded with olives, capers, raisins, and cashews, traditionally cooked inside a hollowed-out Gouda wheel; today usually served from a baking dish
- **Pastechi** — crescent-shaped deep-fried pastry pockets filled with cheese, ham, beef, chicken, tuna, or even chop suey (Asian influence is prominent); sold at bakeries, snack bars, and roadside stands island-wide
- **Pan bati** — a slightly sweet cornmeal pancake/flatbread served alongside stews and fish dishes
- **Funchi** — polenta-like cornmeal porridge, the quintessential local side dish
- **Stoba** — slow-cooked stew, typically made with goat, beef, or conch; comforting and deeply flavourful
- **Ayacas** — Aruban-style tamales wrapped in banana leaves, influenced by Venezuelan and Colombian neighbours; often prepared at Christmas but available year-round at local spots
- **Fresh fish** — wahoo, snapper, barracuda, and mahi-mahi caught daily; best at Zeerovers pier or Driftwood
- **Balashi beer** — Aruba's own lager, brewed on the island since 1999; ask for it at any bar

### Street Food and Local Markets

- **The Pastechi House (Oranjestad):** The definitive stop for a quick, cheap, authentic snack — open early for breakfast-goers
- **Huchada Bakery (Santa Cruz):** A local bakery in a residential neighbourhood for pastechi and baked goods away from tourist zones; great value
- **Bon Bini Festival (Oranjestad, every Tuesday evening at Fort Zoutman):** Weekly folkloric festival with local food stalls, craft vendors, traditional music, and dance — a perfect free introduction to Aruban culture
- **Wilhelmina Park area (Oranjestad):** Street vendors and casual eateries near the waterfront on weekend mornings

## Transportation

### Airport to Hotel Areas

Queen Beatrix International Airport (AUA) is located in the southwestern part of the island, close to the hotel strip. Getting to your hotel is straightforward:

**Taxi (recommended for arrival):**
- Official taxis queue directly outside the arrivals hall. All carry "TX" licence plates and are government-regulated with **fixed rates** (no meters — confirm rate before getting in).
- Airport to Palm Beach hotels: approximately **USD $25–$30** (~CAD $36–$43) for up to 4 passengers.
- Airport to Eagle Beach hotels: approximately **USD $22–$27** (~CAD $31–$39).
- Airport to Oranjestad: approximately **USD $12–$18** (~CAD $17–$26).
- Each additional piece of luggage: AWG 3.60 / ~USD $2.00 surcharge. Night/holiday surcharge applies after 11 PM.
- Pre-booked private transfers are available from operators like El Tours; a flat-rate private car to the Palm Beach / Eagle Beach corridor runs ~**USD $54** per vehicle for 1–4 passengers.

**Shared shuttle:**
- De Palm Tours operates a shared shuttle bus that stops at every major resort. Tickets run just over **$20 USD per person** (return fare; one-way bookings not accepted). Book through your hotel or at the airport counter. Slower than a taxi but budget-friendly for solo travellers or couples.

**Rental car:** Available directly at the airport from all major brands. See car rental section below.

### Getting Around the Island

**Taxi:**
- Fixed-fare, no meters. Available 24 hours at major hotels, at taxi stands in Oranjestad and along the Palm Beach strip, and by phone/app. No Uber or Lyft operates in Aruba.
- For trips along the resort corridor (Palm Beach to Oranjestad), taxis are convenient and affordable. A Palm Beach → Oranjestad trip takes ~15 minutes and costs ~USD $15–$20.
- For all-day excursions to the north coast or national park, costs accumulate quickly — a rental car becomes more economical for day trips.
- Always carry cash for taxis; credit cards are not accepted.

**Car Rental:**
- Strongly recommended for exploring beyond the resort corridor (national park, Natural Pool, California Lighthouse, Baby Beach).
- Rental rates start at **~USD $40–$60/day** (~CAD $57–$86/day) for a compact; 4×4/SUV rates from ~USD $70–$100/day (~CAD $100–$143/day).
- Book in advance, particularly for late April (end of peak season). All major international brands plus local operators are at the airport.
- Roads are well-maintained and traffic drives on the **right side** — identical to Canada. Road signs are in Dutch and English.
- A standard car is fine for paved routes. A **4×4 is necessary** if you plan to drive to the Conchi Natural Pool or along the rugged north coast independently.
- **Note:** As of 2024–2026, personal ATVs, UTVs, and motorcycles are **prohibited** from protected natural areas under Aruban law. Guided UTV/jeep tours are still permitted and widely available.

**Public Bus (Arubus):**
- Aruba's public bus network runs regular routes between **Oranjestad terminal** and the Palm Beach / Eagle Beach hotel strip (Route 10 and Route 10A). Fare: approximately **AWG 2.30 / USD $1.30** per ride.
- Buses run approximately every 30–60 minutes. Good for day trips to Oranjestad or moving along the hotel corridor cheaply.
- **Baby Beach** public bus service has been **suspended indefinitely** — a car or taxi is required for that destination.

### Day Trip Options

- **Jeep / UTV safari tours:** Half-day and full-day guided tours from Palm Beach cover the California Lighthouse, Alto Vista Chapel, Arikok National Park, Natural Pool (Conchi), Bushiribana Gold Mill Ruins, and Baby Beach. Prices from ~USD $80–$130/person depending on vehicle type and duration. Book through De Palm Tours, ABC Tours, or Aruba Off-Road Adventures.
- **Catamaran / snorkel cruises:** Half-day and sunset sails departing from the Pelican Pier or Hyatt Pier. Cover Antilla Shipwreck snorkelling, Arashi Beach, and the Malmok reef. ~USD $60–$90/person.
- **Oranjestad:** Only 5–10 minutes from the hotel strip by taxi or bus; easy half-day on foot.

## Attractions

### 1. Arikok National Park
- **Location:** Northeast Aruba (San Fuego and Vader Piet entrance gates)
- **Hours:** Daily; San Fuego entrance 08:00–15:30, Vader Piet entrance 08:30–15:00
- **Admission:** USD $15 per adult; children 17 and under **free**
- **What it is:** 7,900-acre protected reserve covering ~20% of the island. Features volcanic rock formations, towering cacti, rare wildlife (Aruban burrowing owl, the critically endangered Cascabel rattlesnake), ancient Caquetío Indian rock drawings (2,000+ years old), hidden coves (Boca Prins, Dos Playa), caves, and striking divi-divi trees.
- **Tips:** Bring plenty of water and sunscreen — shade is scarce. A standard car handles the main paved routes; a 4×4 is needed for off-road tracks. Guided ranger tours are highly recommended for first-time visitors and are bookable at the gate.

### 2. The Natural Pool (Conchi)
- **Location:** Inside Arikok National Park, north conservation zone
- **Admission:** Included with park entry (USD $15); guided tours cost extra (~USD $84–$126/person for a jeep or UTV excursion)
- **What it is:** A stunning natural rock formation that creates a sheltered seawater pool — perfect for swimming and snorkelling despite being on the rugged north coast. One of Aruba's most iconic sights.
- **Tips:** The 2.5-mile Daimari-to-Conchi trail is popular; the full Conchi trail is 6.6 miles. Independent access requires a 4×4. Most visitors join a guided jeep or UTV tour from Palm Beach — the easiest and most rewarding option. Go in the morning for calmer seas and better light.

### 3. California Lighthouse
- **Location:** Northern tip of Aruba, beyond Palm Beach (~20-min drive)
- **Admission:** Free to view externally; small fee to climb to the top (approximately USD $5; verify on-site)
- **What it is:** Aruba's iconic 19th-century lighthouse, named after the SS California that wrecked off the coast in 1891. At 30 metres (98 ft), it is the tallest structure on the island and offers a 360° panoramic view of the northwest coast and Palm Beach strip.
- **Tips:** Arrive around **6 PM** for spectacular sunset views and photography. The lighthouse is a popular stop on jeep tours. The nearby restaurant Renaissance Aruba's lighthouse café offers drinks with a view. Horseback riding tours to the lighthouse depart from Rancho Notorious.

### 4. Antilla Shipwreck
- **Location:** Offshore from Malmok Bay, northwest coast (~700 m from shore)
- **Admission:** Accessible by boat tour only. Snorkel tour: ~USD $60–$90/person. Scuba dive tour: ~USD $80–$130/person.
- **What it is:** The largest shipwreck in the Caribbean — a 400-ft (122 m) German cargo freighter scuttled by its captain in 1940 to prevent Allied capture. Nicknamed the "Ghost Ship," it rests at 55 ft (17 m), making it accessible to both open-water divers and snorkellers. Densely covered in coral and home to enormous schools of tropical fish.
- **Tips:** Best morning visibility. Book with established operators: Pelican Adventures, Red Sail Sports, or Aruba Snorkel Center. Most Antilla tours combine the wreck with snorkelling at Arashi Beach and Malmok reef. Bring an underwater camera.

### 5. Oranjestad (Capital City)
- **Location:** Southwest coast, 5–10 min from hotel strip
- **Admission:** Free (museums may charge a small fee)
- **What it is:** Aruba's colourful capital, blending Dutch colonial architecture with Caribbean vibrancy. Pastel-painted townhouses line the waterfront and main shopping street (Caya G.F. Betico Croes). Key landmarks include **Fort Zoutman** (1796, Aruba's oldest building), **Willem III Tower**, and the **National Archeological Museum** (free admission), which tells the story of the Caquetío people.
- **Tips:** The free trolley winds through the city centre — great for orientation. The **Bon Bini Festival** runs every Tuesday evening at Fort Zoutman: folkloric music, dance, crafts, and food stalls (~USD $3 entry). Oranjestad's main street has duty-free jewellery, fashion, and souvenir shops. The waterfront Renaissance Mall is also here.

### 6. Casibari and Ayo Rock Formations
- **Location:** Central Aruba interior
- **Admission:** Free
- **What it is:** Two remarkable clusters of enormous diorite boulders rising dramatically from the flat Aruban landscape. Casibari has a system of steps and pathways carved into the boulders, allowing you to climb to the top for panoramic views. Ayo features ancient Caquetío petroglyphs on the rock faces. Both are ~20-min drive from Palm Beach.
- **Tips:** Morning visits are best to avoid midday heat. The Casibari café at the base sells cold drinks. Easy stop on any island driving day.

### 7. Alto Vista Chapel
- **Location:** North coast, inland from California Lighthouse
- **Admission:** Free
- **What it is:** A small, peaceful yellow chapel built in 1952 on the site of Aruba's first Catholic church (1750). Surrounded by cacti and stations of the cross along the winding approach road. A pilgrimage site and one of Aruba's most photographed landmarks.
- **Tips:** A quiet, contemplative 15-minute stop. Best visited as part of a northern island loop with the California Lighthouse and rock formations. Dress respectfully if entering the chapel.

### 8. Bushiribana Gold Mill Ruins
- **Location:** North coast, near Andicuri Beach
- **Admission:** Free
- **What it is:** Dramatic ruins of a 19th-century gold smelting mill built by the Aruba Island Gold Mining Company in the 1870s during Aruba's brief gold boom. The crumbling stone structure overlooks the wild, rocky north coast with crashing waves — highly photogenic.
- **Tips:** The ruins are free to explore and are a standard stop on all jeep tours. Combine with the California Lighthouse and Natural Bridge (collapsed in 2005, but the smaller Baby Natural Bridge nearby still stands).

## Beaches

All beaches in Aruba are **public by law**. The island's west coast faces the calm Caribbean Sea; the north and east coasts face the rougher Atlantic. Most swimming and lounging happens on the west coast.

### Eagle Beach
- **Location:** Low-rise corridor, between Oranjestad and Palm Beach
- **Character:** Wide, powdery white sand; relatively uncrowded compared to Palm Beach; home to the iconic **Fofoti trees** (often photographed at sunrise). Annually rated among the top 3 Caribbean beaches.
- **Facilities:** Palapas (thatched shade huts) available free on a first-come basis; beach chair and umbrella rentals; nearby casual dining at Tulip Brasserie and others.
- **Highlight:** Several species of sea turtles nest here from March–September — observe from a distance and do not use flash photography near nests.
- **Vibe:** Relaxed, romantic, uncrowded. Best for couples seeking space and serenity. Bucuti & Tara Resort fronts this beach.

### Palm Beach
- **Location:** High-rise hotel strip, northwest coast
- **Character:** 3-km (2-mile) arc of bright white sand lined with resort hotels, beach bars, water sports concessions, and restaurants. Aruba's most active and social beach.
- **Facilities:** Full range of amenities — water sports operators (jet ski, parasailing, banana boat, paddleboard, catamaran), beach bars, beach chair/umbrella rental, casino hotels within steps.
- **Highlight:** The gentle, warm surf and constant trade winds make it ideal for swimming, sailing, and paddleboarding. Sunset catamarans depart from here nightly.
- **Vibe:** Lively and social. Best for couples who want activity, nightlife, and convenience right on the beach.

### Arashi Beach
- **Location:** Far north end of the island, near the California Lighthouse
- **Character:** Smaller, less crowded, Blue Flag-certified beach with outstanding snorkelling. Rock reefs close to shore host abundant marine life.
- **Facilities:** Free palapas; some beach chair rentals. No large food vendors nearby — bring snacks and water.
- **Snorkelling tip:** The left side of the beach (facing the water) is best for beginners with shallower, calmer water; the right side has a reef running parallel to shore for more advanced snorkellers.
- **Vibe:** Quiet and natural. A favourite among snorkellers and those seeking a break from the resort strip. Note: taxis need to be called for pickup — there is no passing traffic.

### Baby Beach
- **Location:** Southern tip of the island, near San Nicolas
- **Character:** Crescent-shaped lagoon with exceptionally shallow, calm, turquoise water enclosed by a natural breakwater. The water temperature feels like a warm bath. One of the safest swimming beaches on the island.
- **Facilities:** Palapas and beach huts along the waterline; Baby Beach Snack Shack Bar; Rum Reef Grill restaurant for a full meal.
- **Getting there:** Public bus service is suspended — a car or taxi is required (~$30–$40 USD from Palm Beach). Worth a dedicated half-day trip.
- **Vibe:** Peaceful and remote compared to the north-end beaches. Excellent for snorkelling in the designated area.

### Malmok Beach (Boca Catalina)
- **Location:** Northwest coast, between Arashi and Palm Beach
- **Character:** Smaller, quieter beach on the snorkel/dive circuit. Part of the stretch above Palm Beach with calm, clear, shallow water over a sandy bottom and scattered rocks.
- **Highlight:** The Antilla Shipwreck sits 700 m offshore — boat tours depart from near here.
- **Vibe:** Local and low-key. Good alternative if Palm Beach feels crowded.

## Safety

### Canada Travel Advisory Level
**Level 1 — Exercise Normal Precautions** (as of March 2026, per the Government of Canada). This is the lowest advisory level and applies to most safe tourist destinations. No elevated warnings are in effect for Aruba.
- Official Canada advisory: [travel.gc.ca/destinations/aruba](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/aruba)

### General Safety
Aruba is consistently one of the safest islands in the Caribbean for tourists. Violent crime against visitors is rare, and the island's economy depends heavily on tourism, so there is strong institutional investment in visitor safety. The Aruba Tourism Police Unit patrols high-traffic areas like Oranjestad and the Palm Beach strip.

- **Petty theft:** The primary risk for tourists. Do not leave valuables (phone, wallet, camera) unattended on the beach. Use in-room safes at hotels.
- **San Nicolas district:** The U.S. State Department notes elevated crime in the San Nicolas area on the southeast coast — best avoided after dark by tourists.
- **Emergency numbers:** Police: **100** | Ambulance / Fire: **911**
- **Taxis:** All official taxis display "TX" on their licence plate and are government-regulated. Use only official taxis, especially at the airport — no unlicensed touts operate landside.
- **Water safety:** Ocean currents on the **north and east (Atlantic) coasts** can be strong and unpredictable. Swim only at designated west-coast beaches. Obey any posted warning flags.
- **Driving:** Traffic drives on the **right side**. Road conditions are generally good. Some north-coast tracks are rough 4×4 terrain. Drink-driving laws are strictly enforced.
- **Sun and heat:** The Aruban sun is intense year-round. Use SPF 30+ sunscreen, wear a hat, and drink plenty of water — dehydration and sunburn are the most common tourist medical issues.
- **Mosquitoes:** Dengue fever and Zika virus are present in the region; use insect repellent (DEET-based) especially in the evening. Aruba's dry climate means mosquitoes are less prevalent than on wetter Caribbean islands.
- **Drinking water:** Tap water is safe to drink — Aruba desalinates seawater through reverse osmosis and maintains high water quality standards. No need to buy bottled water.

## Practical Info

### April Weather
- **Temperature:** Daytime highs of **29–31°C (84–88°F)**; overnight lows ~25–27°C (77–80°F). Sea water temperature ~27–28°C (81–82°F).
- **Sunshine:** Late April averages **8–9 hours** of sunshine per day. Rain, when it occurs, is brief — typically a short shower followed by sun returning within minutes.
- **Trade winds:** Aruba sits at 12° N latitude and is swept by reliable **northeast trade winds** (~25–32 km/h / 15–20 mph). These keep temperatures comfortable and bugs at bay. The winds also make Palm Beach ideal for sailing, kitesurfing, and windsurfing.
- **Hurricane belt:** Aruba lies **far outside the Atlantic hurricane belt**, roughly 27 km north of Venezuela. The island has not had a direct hurricane strike in recorded history. April falls well before hurricane season (June–November) even begins, making late April one of the most weather-reliable times to visit the entire Caribbean.
- **What to pack:** Light breathable clothing (linen, cotton), a light layer for air-conditioned restaurants, swimwear, a hat, sunglasses, reef-safe sunscreen (required at some snorkel sites), and insect repellent.

### Currency
- **Local currency:** Aruban Florin (AWG / Afl). Exchange rate is fixed: **1 USD = 1.79 AWG** (the florin has been pegged to the US dollar since 1986).
- **USD widely accepted:** US dollars are accepted almost everywhere — hotels, restaurants, shops, taxis, and attractions. Most prices are quoted in USD.
- **CAD:** Canadian dollars are **not** widely accepted. Exchange CAD to USD before departure or withdraw USD from ATMs in Aruba (50+ ATMs island-wide, dispensing USD or AWG).
- **Credit cards:** Visa and MasterCard are accepted nearly everywhere. American Express has limited acceptance. Cash is needed for taxis, small snack stands, and local markets.
- **Exchange rate reference (Mar 2026):** ~1 USD = 1.43 CAD (rates fluctuate — check before travel).

### Electricity and Voltage
- **Voltage:** 110–120V, **60 Hz** — identical to Canada. No voltage converter or adapter is needed for any Canadian device.
- **Plug type:** Type A and B (same two-flat-pin and two-flat-plus-round-ground-pin plugs as North America). Canadian plugs and devices work without modification.
- Power is generally reliable; surge protectors are a sensible precaution for sensitive electronics.

### Tipping Customs
- **Restaurants:** Check the bill for an included service charge (10–15% is common, especially at resort restaurants). If no service charge is listed, a **15–20% tip** is customary and appreciated.
- **Taxis:** **10–15% of the fare**, in cash (taxis do not accept cards).
- **Hotel housekeeping:** ~USD $2–$5/day left on the pillow or in an envelope.
- **Hotel porters:** ~USD $1–$2 per bag.
- **All-inclusive resorts:** Tipping is often included; check your package details. Cash tips to individual staff members for exceptional service are always welcome.
- Tipping is not mandatory, but it is an important part of hospitality workers' income on the island.

### Entry Requirements for Canadians
- **Passport:** Valid Canadian passport required. Passport should be valid for at least 6 months beyond your return date.
- **Visa:** Not required for Canadian citizens for stays up to 30 days (extendable).
- **ED Card (Embarkation-Disembarkation Card):** Mandatory for all visitors. Complete online at [edcardaruba.aw](https://edcardaruba.aw) before departure; you will receive a QR code to present on arrival. This is a free requirement but must be done in advance — do not skip it.
- **US Pre-clearance:** Aruba offers US customs and border pre-clearance at AUA airport on departure for travellers connecting through US gateway cities — this can save significant time on your return journey if routing through the US.

### Health and Vaccinations
- No mandatory vaccinations are required for Canadians visiting Aruba.
- Routine vaccinations (hepatitis A/B, tetanus, MMR) are recommended by most travel health clinics. Consult your physician or a travel health clinic at least 4–6 weeks before departure.
- **Travel insurance:** Strongly recommended. Ensure your policy covers emergency medical evacuation — the nearest major medical facility for complex cases may require air evacuation to Curaçao or Venezuela.
- Aruba's main hospital is **Dr. Horacio Oduber Hospital** in Oranjestad — adequate for most routine medical needs.
- Zika and dengue viruses are present in the region; use insect repellent (DEET or icaridin-based), especially at dawn and dusk.

### 2SLGBTQI+ Travel
Aruba is one of the **most 2SLGBTQI+-welcoming destinations in the Caribbean**. The island's tourism brand ("One Happy Island") is inclusive by design, and the major resorts and most restaurants are openly welcoming to all couples and travellers.

- Same-sex relationships are legal and generally respected; public displays of affection are widely accepted, especially in the resort corridor and Oranjestad.
- **Cage Nightclub** (Eagle Beach area) is Aruba's dedicated gay bar and nightclub, hosting the popular Cage Dolls drag show regularly.
- **Aruba Pride** is an annual LGBTQ+ festival, typically held in late June / early July, with events concentrated in Oranjestad and along the waterfront.
- Bucuti & Tara Beach Resort, the Manchebo Beach area, and Palm Beach hotels all actively welcome 2SLGBTQI+ couples.
- As with any destination, general situational awareness is advisable; however, Aruba presents a notably lower risk profile for LGBTQ+ travellers compared to most Caribbean nations.

### Languages
- **Papiamento / Papiamentu:** The native Creole language of Aruba and the ABC islands; a blend of Spanish, Portuguese, Dutch, English, and African languages. Most Arubans speak Papiamento as their first language.
- **Dutch:** Official language for government and legal matters.
- **English:** Spoken fluently by virtually all people working in hospitality, tourism, and retail. All tourist-facing signage is in English. Canadians will have no language barrier whatsoever.
- **Spanish:** Widely spoken due to proximity to Venezuela and Colombia; useful in local shops and neighbourhoods.
- A few useful Papiamento phrases: *Bon dia* (Good morning) | *Danki* (Thank you) | *Bon bini* (Welcome) | *Boa noce* (Good night) | *Con ta bai?* (How are you?)

### Other Quick Facts
- **Time zone:** AST (Atlantic Standard Time) — UTC−4, same as Halifax, NS. **No daylight saving time** in Aruba, so in late April (when Canada observes EDT), Aruba and Toronto are on the **same clock** (both UTC−4). No jet lag concerns.
- **Driving side:** Right-hand traffic (same as Canada).
- **Internet:** Wi-Fi is widely available and free at all major hotels; cellular roaming is available but check with your Canadian carrier for Caribbean data rates. Local SIM cards are available at the airport (SETAR is the local carrier).
- **Duty-free:** Aruba is a free-trade zone; many electronics, jewellery, and liquor items are priced competitively. Oranjestad's main shopping street and waterfront malls offer duty-free shopping.
