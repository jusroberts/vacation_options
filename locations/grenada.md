# St. George's, Grenada (GND) — Vacation Guide for Couples from Toronto

**Trip profile:** Two adults departing Toronto Pearson (YYZ), late April 2026, approximately 5 nights. Full country name: Grenada (also includes the islands of Carriacou and Petite Martinique).
**Currency note:** Flights are priced in CAD. All on-the-ground costs in Grenada are in **XCD** — the Eastern Caribbean Dollar — pegged at a fixed rate of **1 USD = 2.70 XCD**. USD is widely accepted at hotels, restaurants, and tourist-facing businesses. CAD is not commonly accepted; exchange to USD before departing Canada or use a credit card. ATMs dispense XCD.

> **Trip at a glance:**
> - 📍 **Destination:** St. George's, Grenada — "The Spice Isle"
> - ✈️ **Airport:** Maurice Bishop International Airport (GND); connecting via Barbados (BGI) or Port of Spain (POS)
> - 🕐 **Total travel time:** Approximately 9–10 hours door-to-door from Toronto (connection required)
> - 💱 **Currency:** Eastern Caribbean Dollar (XCD); 1 USD ≈ 2.70 XCD; USD widely accepted

## Flights

### Route & Schedule

> **IMPORTANT — NO DIRECT AIR CANADA SERVICE TO GRENADA:**
> **Air Canada does not operate direct flights between Toronto Pearson (YYZ) and Maurice Bishop International Airport (GND) in Grenada.** There is no non-stop or direct YYZ–GND service offered by Air Canada, WestJet, or Air Transat as of early 2026. Reaching Grenada from Toronto requires a connection, typically through another Caribbean hub.
>
> The two most practical routings for Canadians are:
> 1. **Via Barbados (BGI):** Fly Air Canada YYZ–BGI (non-stop, approximately 5 hours; seasonal winter service), then connect to Grenada on **Caribbean Airlines** or a regional carrier. The BGI–GND leg is approximately 45–50 minutes. This is the most commonly used routing for Canadians and allows a connection through a well-served hub with good onward frequency.
> 2. **Via Port of Spain, Trinidad (POS):** Fly Air Canada YYZ–BGI and continue to POS, then connect on **Caribbean Airlines** to GND. Caribbean Airlines operates POS–GND regularly. This adds a further connection and is typically longer; the BGI routing is preferred where available.
> 3. **Via a US gateway:** Fly to Miami (MIA), New York (JFK), or Charlotte (CLT) on Air Canada, then pick up a Caribbean Airlines or American Airlines connection to GND. This routing can occasionally offer competitive fares but typically results in the longest total journey time.
>
> **Verify current schedule and connection options at aircanada.com and caribbean-airlines.com before booking.** Consider working with a Caribbean-specialist travel agent who can book the full multi-carrier itinerary as a single ticket for better protection in case of misconnection.

**Recommended routing:** Air Canada YYZ–BGI (Barbados) non-stop, then Caribbean Airlines BGI–GND onward.

**Flight duration:** Air Canada YYZ–BGI is approximately **5 hours**. The onward BGI–GND hop is approximately **50 minutes**. Allow a minimum 2.5–3 hour connection in Barbados for international transit. **Total door-to-door travel time from central Toronto: approximately 9–10 hours**, including check-in, connection, and ground transfers.

**Arrival airport note:** Maurice Bishop International Airport (GND) is a small regional facility on the south coast of Grenada. It handles international arrivals smoothly but is not a large hub — clear customs and immigration, collect luggage, and you are outside and into a taxi within 20–30 minutes of landing. The airport sits approximately 15–20 minutes by road from the Grand Anse resort strip.

**Terminal at YYZ:** All Air Canada international flights depart from **Terminal 1** at Toronto Pearson International Airport.

**Estimated round-trip cost (2 passengers, economy):**
- The multi-leg routing means pricing varies significantly by booking approach and timing. Budget approximately **CAD $900–$1,500/person** round trip all-in for economy class on the combined itinerary (Air Canada YYZ–BGI + Caribbean Airlines BGI–GND–BGI + Air Canada BGI–YYZ).
- For 2 passengers: budget approximately **CAD $1,800–$3,000 total** for economy round-trip flights.
- Grenada is a lower-volume destination for Canadians than Barbados, Jamaica, or Cuba; fares for the connection legs are not heavily discounted and inventory is limited. Book 3–5 months in advance for the best availability.
- Late April is at or near the end of Caribbean winter season — some fare easing is possible compared to peak February–March, but the connection complexity means less price competition.
- **WestJet Vacations** occasionally offers Grenada as part of a Caribbean package program; check their website for bundled flight-and-hotel options that may simplify booking a multi-leg itinerary.

## Hotels

Grenada punches well above its international profile for accommodation quality. Grand Anse Beach, a sweeping 3-kilometre crescent on the southwest coast, serves as the resort hub and is the address for most of the island's best properties. The island caters to a quieter, more discerning visitor than the large-volume all-inclusive markets of Cuba or Jamaica — the emphasis is on boutique luxury, intimacy, and natural beauty rather than mass-market entertainment. This results in a smaller selection of hotels but a high quality floor throughout. Grenada also has no large-scale all-inclusive resort sector; most properties are room-only or offer meal plans separately.

Prices below are in USD per night for two adults unless otherwise stated. All prices are estimates; verify current rates directly with properties or through your travel agent.

### 1. Spice Island Beach Resort
- **Area:** Grand Anse Beach, southwest coast
- **Category:** Luxury all-inclusive; the flagship resort in Grenada
- **Price range:** Approximately **USD $900–$2,000+/night** all-inclusive, depending on suite category and season
- **What makes it special:** Spice Island Beach Resort is the most celebrated resort in Grenada and one of the finest all-inclusive properties in the Eastern Caribbean. The 64 suites are spread across beachfront and garden settings; the top-tier suites feature private plunge pools opening directly onto Grand Anse Beach. The all-inclusive package is genuinely inclusive — meals, premium bar, non-motorized watersports, and personal butler service in the pool-suite categories. The property has received numerous awards from Condé Nast Traveller and Caribbean Travel + Life, and the attentive, personalised service has become its defining characteristic.
- **Notes:** This is a genuine all-inclusive luxury offering — not a large-complex Sandals-style resort, but a small, intimate, beach-focused property with exceptional service. For a special occasion trip to Grenada, Spice Island is the definitive address. Book well in advance; the suite inventory is limited and peak dates fill early.

### 2. Calabash Boutique Hotel
- **Area:** L'Anse aux Épines (Prickly Bay area), south coast, near Grand Anse
- **Category:** Adults-only boutique luxury; room-only and meal plan options
- **Price range:** Approximately **USD $600–$1,200/night**
- **What makes it special:** Calabash is Grenada's original adults-only boutique and occupies a different market position than Spice Island — more intimate, more architecturally distinctive, and famous for a heritage of exceptional dining. The resort formerly featured the consulting involvement of Michelin-starred chef Gary Rhodes (the "Rhodes" restaurant was one of the Caribbean's most talked-about dining rooms before Rhodes' passing in 2019). The culinary legacy persists in a continued emphasis on quality dining. The 30 suites are set in tropical gardens, many with private pools, and the calm, uncrowded atmosphere is the resort's signature.
- **Notes:** Not all-inclusive. Adults-only makes this an excellent choice for couples specifically. The south-coast location is quieter than Grand Anse; a water taxi or short drive connects you to the main beach. The intimacy and culinary quality distinguish Calabash from larger properties.

### 3. Silversands Grenada
- **Area:** Grand Anse Beach
- **Category:** Ultra-luxury; contemporary design; room-only
- **Price range:** Approximately **USD $700–$2,500+/night** depending on category
- **What makes it special:** Silversands is Grenada's newest and most architecturally striking resort — a modernist property defined by a remarkable 90-metre infinity pool, one of the longest in the Caribbean, running parallel to Grand Anse Beach. The design is sleek, cosmopolitan, and deliberately different from the traditional Caribbean resort aesthetic. The suites and villas are generously proportioned and exceptionally well-appointed; the beach bar and restaurant draw both guests and island visitors. Silversands quickly established itself after opening as one of the most photogenic resorts in the region.
- **Notes:** Not all-inclusive. The contemporary design appeals to a younger luxury traveller and those who find traditional resort décor tired. The long pool is spectacular; Grand Anse Beach is directly accessible. Factor in meal and bar costs on top of the room rate — the on-property dining is high quality but priced accordingly.

### 4. Laluna
- **Area:** Morne Rouge (Portici Beach), south coast
- **Category:** Boutique luxury; Italian-owned; room-only
- **Price range:** Approximately **USD $400–$900/night**
- **What makes it special:** Laluna is one of the most distinctive boutique properties in Grenada — an Italian-owned and Italian-designed resort of 16 individual beachfront cottages built into a hillside above a private beach at Morne Rouge. The aesthetic is rustic-chic: four-poster beds, open-air showers, hammocks on private verandas, and an organic kitchen growing herbs and vegetables on-site. The Italian owner's influence on the restaurant — now The Beach House — has made it one of the most talked-about dining destinations on the island for Mediterranean-Grenadian fusion. Laluna operates on a deliberately unhurried, low-key philosophy.
- **Notes:** Not all-inclusive. The private-beach setting at Morne Rouge (a different bay from Grand Anse) gives Laluna a genuinely secluded feel. The property is small enough that staff know guests by name quickly. This is the choice for couples who want character and intimacy over resort amenity completeness. Yoga classes, spa treatments, and kayaks are available on site.

### 5. True Blue Bay Boutique Resort
- **Area:** True Blue Bay, south coast, near Maurice Bishop International Airport
- **Category:** Mid-range boutique; marina setting; room-only
- **Price range:** Approximately **USD $180–$350/night**
- **What makes it special:** True Blue Bay offers the best mid-range value proposition in Grenada's south — an independently owned boutique resort on a calm marina bay with a lively social atmosphere, an excellent on-site restaurant (Dodgy Dock), dive shop, and pool. The property has a loyal following among divers — True Blue Bay partners with local dive operators to offer packages covering Grenada's exceptional dive sites, including the Underwater Sculpture Park. The marina setting and yachting crowd give it a casual, sociable character that distinguishes it from the quieter luxury properties along Grand Anse.
- **Notes:** Not all-inclusive. The airport proximity (5 minutes by taxi) makes arrival logistics effortless. The trade-off is that Grand Anse Beach is a 10–15 minute drive away. A shuttle or water taxi to the beach is available. For couples who want a budget-conscious base with character, dive access, and a great bar scene, True Blue Bay is the top recommendation at this price tier.

### 6. Kalinago Beach Resort
- **Area:** Grand Anse Beach
- **Category:** Mid-range; beachfront; good value
- **Price range:** Approximately **USD $150–$280/night**
- **What makes it special:** Kalinago Beach Resort sits directly on Grand Anse Beach at a price point well below its luxury neighbours, making it the most accessible entry point to the island's best beach strip. The property is smaller and less amenity-rich than Spice Island or Silversands, but the beachfront location is the same, and the value proposition is clear. The on-site restaurant serves decent meals and the beach access is direct. For couples who want to be on Grand Anse Beach without Spice Island pricing, Kalinago is a practical, honest choice.
- **Notes:** Not all-inclusive. The property has been through ownership and renovation transitions; confirm current room quality and amenities at time of booking. Credit cards accepted; early booking recommended for the best room categories.

> **Booking tip:** Grenada is not a mass-market package destination for Canadians. Very few all-inclusive packages from Canadian operators are available; most bookings are made directly with properties or through Caribbean-specialist travel agents. **Working with a specialist agent who has direct relationships with Grenadian properties** can unlock preferred room categories, meal plan options, and honeymoon or anniversary amenities unavailable through direct booking. Given the complexity of the multi-leg flight itinerary, a specialist agent can also book the full journey — Air Canada plus Caribbean Airlines — on a single protected ticket. Book 3–5 months in advance for the best suite availability at the top properties.

## Dining

Grenadian cuisine is rooted in the island's extraordinary spice culture. Grenada produces approximately 20% of the world's nutmeg, along with mace, cinnamon, cloves, bay leaves, turmeric, and allspice — all of which find their way into the local kitchen in ways that give Grenadian food a warmth and complexity you will not find in most of the Caribbean. The **national dish is oil down** — a rich, slow-cooked one-pot made with breadfruit, salted meat (typically pork or chicken), callaloo leaves, and coconut milk, with nutmeg and turmeric for seasoning. It is a communal dish, traditionally prepared in a single iron pot for gatherings, and eating it at a local establishment is one of the most authentically Grenadian experiences available. **Lambi** (conch), prepared curried or stewed, is the other staple seafood dish; **crab back** (land crab stuffed back into its shell with seasoned meat) is a distinctive Grenadian appetizer; **fish broth** (a light, fragrant fish soup eaten for breakfast in some communities) reflects the island's fishing culture. **Bakes** (fried dough, served with saltfish, cheese, or butter) are the standard breakfast accompaniment. To drink: **rum punch** made with the local Jack Iron or Rivers Rum (one of the last pot-still rums in the Caribbean, extraordinarily potent), **Carib beer** (brewed in Trinidad and the regional standard), and **Grenadian cocoa tea** — a thick, dark hot chocolate made with raw cocoa paste and local spice, not to be confused with commercial hot cocoa.

### Restaurants

**The Beach House Restaurant** — Laluna's restaurant at Morne Rouge Beach, The Beach House has established itself as one of the most romantic dining destinations on the island. The menu blends Mediterranean cooking traditions with Grenadian spice and local seafood — pasta with fresh catch, wood-fired preparations, and desserts showing the Italian influence of the resort's ownership. The setting is candlelit tables on a private beach; the wine list is the best-curated of any restaurant in Grenada. Reservations recommended, and open to non-hotel guests.

**Dodgy Dock** — The casual waterfront restaurant at True Blue Bay Boutique Resort is as lively and unpretentious as its name suggests. The menu emphasises fresh local fish, grilled and simply prepared, with cold Carib beer and a sociable atmosphere that draws hotel guests and island locals alike. The Dodgy Dock is the place to eat a good, honest meal, watch the marina traffic, and spend a long, relaxed evening. Especially popular with the sailing and diving crowd.

**Umbrellas Beach Bar** — A fixture on the Grand Anse Beach strip, Umbrellas is the best option for a casual lunch on the island's most popular beach. The menu covers the usual Caribbean beach-bar range — grilled fish, burgers, lobster when in season — at prices that are fair by resort-area standards. Popular with both visitors from the surrounding resorts and day-trippers from St. George's; the atmosphere is reliably easy-going. Good cocktails and the perfect vantage point for watching the Grand Anse beach scene.

**Sails Restaurant & Bar** — Located at Secret Harbour Marina on the south coast, Sails is one of Grenada's best sunset dining options. The waterside setting above the marina makes it ideal for sundowners; the menu focuses on seafood with several well-executed local preparations alongside international standards. The clientele is a mix of yachties from the marina and island residents; the atmosphere is reliably convivial without being loud. Worth the short taxi ride from Grand Anse for an evening out.

**Belmont Estate** — Not a restaurant in the conventional sense, but a lunch experience on a working nutmeg and cocoa plantation in the north of the island. Belmont Estate serves traditional Grenadian plantation-style meals — often including oil down — in an open-air dining room on the estate grounds, as part of tours that include nutmeg harvesting, cocoa fermentation, and the chocolate-making process. The combination of cultural experience and genuinely local food makes a Belmont lunch one of the most memorable meals available in Grenada. Plan this as a full half-day excursion from the south coast.

**Carib Sushi** — A popular local restaurant in the Grand Anse Shopping Centre that has earned a loyal following among Grenadian residents — a reliable indicator of quality and value. The menu runs a creative fusion of Japanese technique with Caribbean ingredients, including fresh local tuna and flying fish. The setting is simple; the food is consistent and fairly priced. A good option for couples who want something different from the resort-area dining options.

**La Plywood** — In the fishing town of Gouyave on the west coast, La Plywood represents the authentic rum shop culture that is the social heart of Grenadian community life. Rum is poured, conversations happen, and simple food — whatever the kitchen is offering that day — is served with no ceremony. Visiting Gouyave (ideally on a Friday evening, when the weekly Fish Friday festival runs) and stopping into a rum shop is one of those travel experiences that no guidebook can fully describe. La Plywood is among the more welcoming of the local spots for visitors. Order rum, order whatever they are cooking, and settle in.

## Transportation

### GND Airport to Hotel

Maurice Bishop International Airport (GND) is located on the south coast of Grenada, approximately **15–20 minutes by road** from the Grand Anse resort strip and St. George's harbour.

**Taxis:**
- Official taxis operate from the arrivals terminal on **government-regulated fixed fares** — no meters. The approximate fare from GND airport to the Grand Anse resort area is **USD $20–$25 per vehicle**. Confirm the fare before departure.
- Taxis hold 3–4 passengers comfortably. For two travellers with luggage, a taxi is the standard and most practical arrival option.
- Many hotels — particularly the larger properties — can pre-arrange airport transfers; confirm at booking.

**Note:** There is no public bus or shuttle service from GND airport that is practical for arriving guests with luggage.

### Getting Around Grenada

> **CRITICAL: Grenada drives on the LEFT side of the road.** As a former British colony, Grenada follows the British road convention — traffic moves on the left, the driver sits on the right side of the car. This is the opposite of what Canadian drivers are accustomed to. The left-hand rule catches many North American visitors off guard, particularly at roundabouts and when making turns at intersections. If you are renting a car, allow time to adjust, drive slowly and deliberately, and remind yourself "stay left" every time you pull away. The narrow, hilly roads around St. George's and the interior require particular care. If in doubt for the first day, use taxis until you are comfortable.

**Rental Cars:**
- Car rental is available from local and international agencies at GND airport.
- An international driver's licence is required alongside your Canadian driver's licence; confirm with the specific agency at time of booking.
- Rates: approximately **USD $50–$80/day** for a compact or small SUV.
- Grenada's road network is relatively compact but often steep, narrow, and winding — especially in the interior parishes. A small SUV or 4WD is preferable to a compact if you plan to visit Grand Etang, Belmont Estate, or the northern parishes. Roads are generally passable but are not comparable to North American highway standards.
- A rental car is the most practical way to explore the island independently — the east coast, north coast, Belmont Estate, and Gouyave are all meaningful drives from the south coast hotels but well worth the effort with your own vehicle.

**Taxis:**
- Taxis are the standard mode of transport for visitors who prefer not to drive. Fixed government-rate fares apply; agree on the fare before departure.
- Approximate fares: Grand Anse to St. George's: **USD $8–$12**; Grand Anse to Gouyave: **USD $25–$35**; Grand Anse to Belmont Estate: **USD $40–$55** (or negotiate a half-day rate with a driver).
- Your hotel can arrange reliable taxi service and recommend trusted drivers for day excursions. Negotiating a fixed half-day or full-day rate with a driver (typically USD $80–$130 for a full-day island circuit) is a cost-effective approach for longer excursions.

**Minibuses:**
- Colourful, crowded, and extremely cheap, Grenada's minibus network serves set routes connecting the south coast, St. George's, and most major towns. Fares are a few XCD (under USD $2 per trip). Minibuses are a genuinely local experience and perfectly safe, but are not convenient for visitors with luggage or those heading to specific resort areas on a schedule. They run frequently during daytime hours but thin out after early evening. Worth trying at least once for the experience of Grenadian daily life.

**Water Taxis:**
- A water taxi service operates between Grand Anse Beach and the Carenage waterfront in St. George's — approximately **USD $5 per person each way**. This is one of the best-value transport options on the island: the short crossing offers a beautiful view of the horseshoe harbour and the hill-climbing capital, and arrives directly at the waterfront rather than requiring a drive through the winding roads and traffic of the capital. The crossing takes approximately 15 minutes. Strongly recommended for any visit to St. George's from the Grand Anse hotels.

**Rideshare and App-Based Transport:**
- There are no Uber, Lyft, or other app-based rideshare services operating in Grenada. Do not plan around them. Pre-arrange taxis through your hotel for airport pickups and any critical timed journeys.

## Attractions

### 1. Grand Etang National Park and Lake
- **Hours:** Park accessible daily; visitor centre approximately 8 AM–4 PM
- **Admission:** Small entry fee (approximately USD $3–$5/person)
- **Description:** Grand Etang is the ecological heart of Grenada — a national park encompassing the island's central rainforest highlands and a volcanic crater lake at approximately 530 metres elevation. The lake sits in a dormant caldera surrounded by dense tropical forest, with a colour that shifts between dark green and grey depending on the sky above. Several hiking trails radiate from the visitor centre, ranging from a 30-minute loop around the lake shoreline to more demanding half-day routes through the interior forest to peaks with views over both coasts. The park is also home to the Mona monkey — a West African species introduced to Grenada centuries ago and now thoroughly naturalised. The monkeys are accustomed to visitors and will approach closely near the visitor centre; do not feed them. The drive up from St. George's through the interior parishes is scenic throughout and passes through nutmeg and cocoa cultivation areas.

### 2. St. George's Capital
- **Hours:** Always accessible at street level; forts and museums have set hours
- **Admission:** Fort George entry approximately USD $2–$5; Grenada National Museum approximately USD $5
- **Description:** St. George's is one of the most beautiful capital cities in the Caribbean — a horseshoe harbour (the Carenage) ringed by brightly coloured Georgian-era buildings and warehouses, with steep hills behind carrying the old fortifications and the red-roofed colonial-era town. The **Carenage** waterfront is the social and commercial centre: fishing boats and inter-island ferries share the docks with café tables and the daily rhythm of a working port town. **Fort George**, on the promontory above the harbour, is a well-preserved 18th-century British fortification with panoramic views over the capital, the harbour, and the Caribbean coast. The **Grenada National Museum** nearby covers the island's history from pre-Columbian times through the colonial era to the 1983 US intervention and beyond. Allow a half-day to explore properly; the water taxi from Grand Anse is the most enjoyable way to arrive.

### 3. Belmont Estate
- **Hours:** Monday–Saturday, tours typically 9 AM–4 PM; confirm in advance
- **Admission:** Approximately USD $10–$15/person for a guided tour; lunch additional
- **Description:** A working nutmeg and cocoa plantation in St. Patrick's Parish in the north of the island, Belmont Estate is the most immersive cultural and agricultural experience available in Grenada. The guided tour walks through the entire process from harvested pods to dried nutmeg, with explanations of how Grenada came to supply a fifth of the world's nutmeg output. The cocoa section covers fermentation, drying, and the bean-to-bar process that the Grenadian chocolate industry has made its own. The estate restaurant serves traditional Grenadian meals — the oil down lunch is a highlight. This is a half-day excursion requiring transport from the south coast (approximately 45–60 minutes each way); coordinate with a hotel-recommended driver or rent a car.

### 4. Underwater Sculpture Park, Molinere Bay
- **Hours:** Daylight hours; dive and snorkel operators depart from Grand Anse and True Blue Bay
- **Admission:** Approximately USD $20–$40/person for a guided snorkel tour; USD $60–$100+ for a dive
- **Description:** The Underwater Sculpture Park at Molinere Bay is the first underwater sculpture park ever created and remains the most famous. British sculptor Jason deCaires Taylor installed a series of life-cast human figures on the seabed in the mid-2000s; over two decades, the sculptures have been colonised by coral, sponges, and sea life, becoming genuine artificial reef habitat as well as eerie and beautiful works of art. The snorkelling is accessible to all — the sculptures sit in relatively shallow water and no diving certification is required. For certified divers, the experience at depth, with the added context of the surrounding reef fish and coral growth on the figures, is unforgettable. Several operators in Grand Anse and True Blue Bay offer guided snorkel and dive trips to the park.

### 5. Annandale Falls
- **Hours:** Daily, approximately 9 AM–5 PM
- **Admission:** Approximately USD $3–$5/person
- **Description:** The most accessible waterfall excursion from St. George's — a 30-metre cascade in a lush, forested gorge approximately 8 km northeast of the capital. The falls are an easy hike from the parking area and the pool at the base is swimmable, making Annandale a good option for a short excursion between beach days. Local vendors sell spices and crafts at the entrance; the setting feels like a genuine slice of Grenada's interior landscape rather than a manicured attraction. Best combined with a drive through St. George's or Grand Etang for a full day's inland exploration.

### 6. Gouyave Nutmeg Processing Station
- **Hours:** Monday–Friday; confirm current hours locally
- **Admission:** Small entry fee (approximately USD $2–$4/person)
- **Description:** In the fishing town of Gouyave on the west coast, the nutmeg processing cooperative gives a direct view into the industry that defines Grenada economically and culturally. Workers sort, grade, and dry nutmeg and mace by hand in an open warehouse operation that operates much as it has for generations. The cooperative processes a meaningful portion of Grenada's annual nutmeg harvest; the smell of the facility — warm spice, wood, and sea air — is memorable. Combine with a visit to Gouyave for the Friday Fish Fry in the evening: the weekly outdoor fish-fry festival on the waterfront is among the most enjoyable cultural events in the Eastern Caribbean, drawing the whole community.

### 7. Grenada Chocolate Company / Tri Island Chocolate
- **Hours:** Vary; confirm directly with each producer
- **Admission:** Tours approximately USD $10–$20/person
- **Description:** Grenada has developed a bean-to-bar organic chocolate industry that has attracted international attention and awards. The **Grenada Chocolate Company**, founded in 1999, is one of the original craft bean-to-bar producers and operates on cooperative, fair-trade principles — it is owned by cacao farmers and chocolate makers working together. **Tri Island Chocolate** is a newer, similarly artisan producer. Tours of both facilities are available and explain the entire process from the fermented cacao bean through roasting, grinding, and tempering to finished chocolate bars. The chocolate is sold on-site and makes an exceptional, genuinely unique souvenir of the Spice Isle. Available in specialty food shops throughout the island as well.

## Beaches

### Grand Anse Beach
The definitive beach of Grenada — a sweeping 3-kilometre crescent of white sand on the calm Caribbean coast south of St. George's. Grand Anse is wide, clean, and well-shaded by sea grape trees at the margins; the water is calm, clear, and warm year-round. Most of Grenada's principal resorts (Spice Island Beach, Silversands, Kalinago) front directly onto Grand Anse, and beach bars, watersports operators, and vendors provide the infrastructure of an active tourist beach. Despite all of this, it is rarely crowded enough to feel overwhelming — Grenada does not receive the visitor volumes of Barbados or Antigua, and Grand Anse retains a genuinely relaxed character. Sea temperature in late April averages 27–28°C (80–82°F). The best beach on the island and among the best in the Eastern Caribbean.

### Morne Rouge Beach (BBC Beach)
A short drive south of Grand Anse, Morne Rouge occupies a smaller, more sheltered bay that offers a deliberate contrast to its famous neighbour. There are no beach vendors here, far fewer visitors, and the atmosphere is genuinely quiet. The water is calm and clear — the sheltered aspect of the bay means it is ideal for swimming and floating without distraction. Laluna resort fronts this beach; non-guests can access it from the public beach path. The lack of amenities means bringing water and shade if you plan to stay long, but the peace and beauty justify the arrangement.

### La Sagesse Beach
On the southeast coast, La Sagesse sits within a protected natural area (the La Sagesse Nature Centre) that encompasses mangroves, a salt pond, and coastal forest as well as the beach itself. The beach is remote by Grenadian standards — a significant drive from the south coast — and receives almost no casual visitors. The snorkelling off La Sagesse is considered among the best shore-entry snorkelling on the island, with reef fish, coral formations, and occasional turtles. The La Sagesse Hotel has a small restaurant at the beach entrance; otherwise, bring your own provisions. The journey is worth it for couples who want one genuinely uncrowded, nature-adjacent beach day.

### Bathway Beach
On Grenada's northeast Atlantic coast, Bathway is the most beautiful of the island's windward beaches — a long stretch of coarser sand with a dramatic coastal landscape shaped by Atlantic exposure. The water here is energetic (the Atlantic swell produces waves unsuitable for weak swimmers at the primary access point, though a natural rock barrier creates a sheltered pool safe for swimming). Bathway is a favourite weekend beach for Grenadian families and is rarely visited by resort-area tourists; the atmosphere is local, relaxed, and authentic. The drive from Grand Anse takes approximately 45–60 minutes through the interior and northeast parishes — make it a half-day excursion.

### Pink Gin Beach
A small, calm beach immediately adjacent to the True Blue Bay area on the south coast. The name refers to the faintly rosy tinge of the sand from crushed coral and shell material; the water is sheltered and exceptionally clear, making it one of the island's best shore-snorkelling options without requiring a boat. Pink Gin Beach is accessible through True Blue Bay resort and is less visited than Grand Anse. Calm conditions year-round make it suitable for less confident swimmers.

### Anse aux Epines (Prickly Bay)
Less a traditional beach than a sheltered marina bay on the south coast, Anse aux Epines (also called Prickly Bay) is lined with moored yachts and the infrastructure of Grenada's significant sailing community. The beach itself is small and secondary to the nautical atmosphere; this is the place to come for sundowners at the marina bars, for watching the sailing traffic, and for the distinctive social world of the Eastern Caribbean yachting community. Calabash Hotel and several marina-area restaurants and bars front the bay.

## Practical Info

### Weather

Grenada lies in the southern Caribbean, just outside the main hurricane belt — a meaningful practical advantage. The island has two broad seasons: a drier season from roughly December through May, and a wetter season from June through November. Late April is at the end of the dry season, with warm, sunny conditions and relatively low rainfall. May marks the beginning of the wet season; showers become more frequent but are typically brief and intense rather than all-day affairs.

| Metric | April | May |
|---|---|---|
| Daytime high | 30°C (86°F) | 30°C (86°F) |
| Overnight low | 23°C (73°F) | 24°C (75°F) |
| Average rain days | ~6 per month | ~10 per month |
| Sea temperature | 27–28°C (80–82°F) | 28°C (82°F) |
| Humidity | Moderate — dry season tail | Increasing — wet season start |
| Sunshine | ~8–9 hours/day | ~7–8 hours/day |
| UV Index | 12 — **Extreme** | 12 — **Extreme** |

- Late April is generally the best available late-season window: dry enough for reliable beach days, warm but not oppressive, and below the price peaks of January and February.
- **UV warning:** A UV Index of 12 is classified as extreme — unprotected skin can burn in under 10 minutes at midday. Apply SPF 50+ generously and reapply every 90 minutes. Wear a broad-brimmed hat and UV-blocking sunglasses, particularly during morning beach time and on water excursions.
- Hurricane season runs June through November; late April carries essentially no hurricane risk. Grenada's southerly position means it has historically been less affected by major Caribbean hurricanes than islands further north — though no location in the region is entirely immune.

### Currency

The **Eastern Caribbean Dollar (XCD)** is the official currency of Grenada, shared with seven other Eastern Caribbean nations. The XCD is **pegged at a fixed rate to the US Dollar: 1 USD = 2.70 XCD**. This rate is set by the Eastern Caribbean Central Bank and has been in place for decades; there is no exchange rate fluctuation to manage. In practice, **US Dollars are accepted everywhere** at hotels, restaurants, taxis, and tourist-facing shops at the fixed 2.70 rate (you will receive change in XCD). Canadian dollars are **not accepted**; exchange to USD before departing Canada or use credit cards.

- **Credit and debit cards** (Visa, Mastercard) are accepted at most hotels, restaurants, and larger shops. Inform your bank before travel to avoid fraud holds.
- **ATMs** are available in St. George's, Grand Anse Shopping Centre, and at the airport. They dispense XCD; the conversion to USD is straightforward at the fixed rate.
- Exchange Canadian dollars to USD **before departing Canada** for the most convenient experience; USD will serve you throughout your trip.

### Electricity

- **Voltage:** 220V, 50 Hz — significantly different from the Canadian standard of 120V, 60 Hz.
- **Plugs:** Type G (British-standard three-pin rectangular plugs), the same as used in the UK, Ireland, and many former British territories.
- **Canadian visitors require both a plug adapter and a voltage converter (or transformer) for any device not rated for 220V.** Most modern laptops, phone chargers, and camera battery chargers are dual-voltage (100–240V) and will work with only a Type G adapter — check the fine print on your charger or device power supply. Hair dryers, straighteners, and older appliances are frequently not dual-voltage and will require a converter; using them without one will destroy the device. Confirm voltage compatibility for every device before packing. Purchase a Type G adapter and a suitable voltage converter in Canada before departure.

### Language

**English** is the official and universal language of Grenada. There is no language barrier of any kind for Canadian visitors. Grenadians speak with a distinctive Eastern Caribbean cadence; communication is easy throughout the island.

### Time Zone

Grenada observes **Atlantic Standard Time (AST), UTC−4**, year-round, with no daylight saving time. In late April, Toronto is on Eastern Daylight Time (EDT, UTC−4). **There is no time difference between Grenada and Toronto in late April.** No jet lag adjustment is needed.

### Entry Requirements for Canadians

- **Passport:** A valid Canadian passport is required for entry. Ensure your passport is valid for the full duration of your trip; travel advisors generally recommend at least 6 months' validity beyond your return date.
- **Visa:** Canadian citizens do not require a visa for tourist visits to Grenada.
- **Return/onward ticket:** A confirmed return or onward airline ticket is required and will be checked by immigration on arrival.
- **Customs declaration:** Complete the customs and immigration form distributed on the aircraft before landing.
- **Length of stay:** Canadians may stay as tourists for up to 3 months without a visa; a 5-night stay is well within this limit.

### Canada Travel Advisory

As of early 2026, the Government of Canada rates Grenada at **"Exercise Normal Security Precautions"** — the lowest advisory level, equivalent to countries considered safe for routine travel. There are no specific crime, health, or political concerns noted for Grenada as of this writing. Grenada has a stable political environment and a low-key crime profile relative to many larger Caribbean destinations; petty theft and opportunistic crime in tourist areas warrant the same common-sense precautions you would apply anywhere. **Always check travel.gc.ca** for the most current advisory before departure, and register your trip through the Registration of Canadians Abroad service if you plan to be off the main tourist track for extended periods.

### Health Notes

- **No mandatory vaccinations** are required for entry to Grenada for Canadian citizens arriving from Canada.
- **Routine vaccinations** should be current: COVID-19, influenza, MMR, tetanus/diphtheria.
- **Hepatitis A** vaccination is recommended as a general precaution for any Caribbean destination.
- **Dengue fever:** Dengue-carrying Aedes mosquitoes are present in Grenada. Use a DEET-based insect repellent, particularly at dawn and dusk, and when in vegetated areas, rainforest areas like Grand Etang, or plantation estates away from the resort strip. Dengue risk is present year-round; it rises during and after rainy periods. No vaccine for dengue is currently widely available for travellers; prevention through repellent use and covered clothing in the evenings is the principal protective measure.
- **Tap water:** Generally safe at established hotels and resorts, where water comes from treated municipal sources or well-maintained hotel systems. Outside of hotels — in rural areas, local restaurants, and smaller guesthouses — **bottled water is strongly recommended**. Bottled water is widely available and inexpensive throughout the island.
- **Medical facilities:** Grenada's primary hospital is St. George's General Hospital (also known as the General Hospital) in the capital. Facilities are adequate for routine and moderate care but limited for complex trauma, cardiac events, or specialist conditions. Medical evacuation to Barbados, Trinidad, or the United States may be required for serious conditions; travel medical insurance covering emergency evacuation is essential.
- **Travel medical insurance is not optional.** OHIP and Canadian provincial health plans provide no meaningful coverage outside Canada. A comprehensive policy covering emergency medical treatment, hospitalization, and medical evacuation is strongly recommended for all travellers.
- **Sun and heat:** The primary health risk for most visitors is sunburn and heat exhaustion. Apply SPF 50+ generously, stay hydrated, and seek shade during peak midday sun (11 AM–3 PM).

### Tipping Customs

Grenada broadly follows Eastern Caribbean tipping conventions, influenced by British customs:

| Situation | Suggested Tip |
|---|---|
| Restaurant (sit-down) | 10–15% (check bill — a 10% service charge is often added automatically) |
| Resort housekeeper | USD $2–$5/night, left daily |
| Resort porter/bellhop | USD $1–$2 per bag |
| Taxi driver | 10–15% of fare, or round up to the nearest round number |
| Tour guide / driver (half or full day) | USD $10–$20/person |
| Spa therapist | 15% |
| Bar staff | USD $1–$2 per round |

- **Check your bill first:** A service charge of 10% is commonly applied at restaurants and resort dining rooms. Confirm before adding an additional tip.
- Budget approximately **USD $100–$150 per couple** specifically for gratuities over a 5-night stay as a reasonable baseline.

### 2SLGBTQI+ Travellers

Grenada retains **colonial-era laws that criminalise same-sex sexual activity** under the Criminal Code. As of early 2026, these laws remain on the books, though prosecutions are rare and enforcement is generally not directed at tourists. The legal situation is broadly similar to that of St. Kitts, St. Lucia, and several other Eastern Caribbean nations that have not yet undertaken the legal reform that Barbados completed in 2023.

In practical terms: Grenada is a socially conservative, deeply religious society, and public expression of same-sex relationships — including public displays of affection — is inadvisable anywhere on the island, including the resort strip. The island does not have a visible LGBTQ+ social scene, and unlike Barbados (which decriminalized in 2023) or the British territories (which were decriminalized by UK Privy Council order), Grenada has not yet undertaken formal legal reform. Same-sex couples visiting Grenada's international-brand resorts are unlikely to encounter overt hostility within those specific environments, but awareness of the broader legal and social context is important and the situation is meaningfully different from Canada. This is not an openly welcoming destination for 2SLGBTQI+ travellers.

**Before travelling, review the most current Government of Canada travel advisory for Grenada at travel.gc.ca**, which includes notes on local laws that may affect 2SLGBTQI+ Canadians. Assess your comfort level with the legal and social environment in full before booking.
