# St. John's, Antigua (ANU) — Vacation Guide for Couples from Toronto

**Trip profile:** Two adults departing Toronto Pearson (YYZ), late April 2026, approximately 5 nights.
**Currency note:** Flights are priced in CAD. All on-the-ground costs in Antigua are in **XCD** (Eastern Caribbean Dollar) — the official currency — though **USD is widely accepted** across the island at hotels, restaurants, shops, and taxis. CAD is not broadly accepted; convert to USD before departing or use a credit card. ATMs dispense XCD.

## Flights

Air Canada operates seasonal non-stop service between Toronto Pearson (YYZ) and V.C. Bird International Airport (ANU) in Antigua. This route runs during the winter/spring travel season — typically from approximately November through late April — and operates **2–3 times per week** at peak season. Flight frequency and exact schedule vary by season; confirm current availability at aircanada.com well before booking, as late April falls near the tail end of the seasonal window.

**Flight duration:** Approximately **4 hours** (YYZ to ANU). Return is similar.

**Terminal at YYZ:** All Air Canada flights depart from **Terminal 1** at Toronto Pearson International Airport.

**Estimated round-trip cost (2 passengers, economy):**

- **Verify the current schedule at aircanada.com before booking.** If the seasonal route has wound down for the summer by your travel dates, alternative routing through another hub (e.g., via a US gateway) may be necessary.

## Hotels

Antigua's accommodation scene is one of the most diverse and rewarding in the Eastern Caribbean. The island punches above its size: it hosts a range of genuinely world-class boutique resorts and all-inclusive properties, concentrated primarily around the sheltered southwest coast (English Harbour, Falmouth Harbour, Carlisle Bay) and the calmer northwest (Dickenson Bay, Five Islands Peninsula). Antigua famously claims 365 beaches — one for each day of the year — and the island's varied coastline means each resort area has its own distinct character.

Prices below are in USD per night for two people unless otherwise noted. Hotel taxes and service charges vary by property — confirm the full rate at booking.

### 1. Curtain Bluff Resort
- **Area:** Old Road, southwestern Antigua — on a private headland between two beaches
- **Category:** 5-star, ultra-luxury all-inclusive; one of the finest boutique resorts in the Caribbean
- **Price range:** Approximately **USD $1,200–$2,500/night** (all-inclusive, all meals, premium drinks, and most activities included)
- **Key amenities:** Perched on a dramatic headland with beaches on two sides — one calm, one for watersports — Curtain Bluff is a true Caribbean legend. All meals, all drinks (including premium wines and spirits), all watersports (sailing, windsurfing, waterskiing, scuba diving), tennis, and entertainment are included. The property operates on a genuine premium all-inclusive model with no surprise charges. A world-class wine cellar; spa; fitness centre. Fewer than 90 rooms, preserving an intimate atmosphere that larger resorts cannot replicate.
- **Best for:** Couples celebrating a milestone — honeymoon, significant anniversary, milestone birthday — who want the finest all-inclusive experience in Antigua. The combination of impeccable service, a superb natural setting, and everything genuinely included makes Curtain Bluff a bucket-list resort for many Caribbean travellers. It consistently appears on the best-resorts-in-the-world lists that actually mean something.
- **Note:** Curtain Bluff is not sold through discount channels. Book directly through the resort (curtainbluff.com) or through a luxury travel advisor with Caribbean specialisation. The resort typically closes for the summer season (roughly May–October) — confirm the April opening window at time of booking.

The scuba diving inclusion is a genuine differentiator for dive-interested couples. The Sandals model means no nickel-and-diming — everything except the spa is genuinely included.

Book directly through Sandals or a Sandals Certified Specialist for the best room availability. Air Canada Vacations packages sometimes include this property.

## Dining

Antiguan cuisine draws on the island's British colonial history, African culinary traditions brought by enslaved people, and the abundant fresh seafood of the Eastern Caribbean to create a cooking culture that is straightforward, honest, and deeply satisfying. Two dishes anchor the local culinary identity: **fungee** (a cornmeal porridge similar to polenta, stirred and shaped by hand, served as the island's staple starch) and **pepperpot** (a rich, spiced stew of meat and vegetables — the Antiguan version is distinct from the Guyanese or Trinidadian versions of the same name). Together, fungee and pepperpot constitute the national dish of Antigua. Beyond these, fresh lobster, crayfish, whelks, conch fritters, saltfish, and grilled red snapper appear across menus from beachside shacks to resort dining rooms.

The English Harbour and Falmouth Harbour area — the island's yachting and sailing hub — hosts Antigua's most concentrated and high-quality dining scene outside the resorts. St. John's, the capital, has a lively market scene and local lunch spots worth exploring. For a memorable meal with atmosphere, the harbour restaurants offer some of the best people-watching (and yacht-watching) in the Lesser Antilles.

**Budget note:** Dining in Antigua spans a wide range. Local lunch spots and market stalls: USD $10–$20/person. Mid-range restaurant dinner: USD $30–$60/person. Upscale harbour or resort dining: USD $60–$120/person. Many upscale restaurants add a 10–15% service charge automatically — always verify before adding additional gratuity.

## Transportation

### ANU Airport to Hotel

V.C. Bird International Airport (ANU) is located on the northeastern edge of the island, approximately **8 km northeast of St. John's** and anywhere from **20 minutes to 45 minutes** from the main resort areas depending on destination (Dickenson Bay is the closest major resort area; English Harbour and the southwest coast take 30–45 minutes).

**Taxis:**
- Taxis at ANU operate on **government-regulated fixed fares** by zone — there are no meters. Confirm the fare with the driver before departing.
  - ANU Airport to Dickenson Bay / St. John's: approximately **USD $15–$20 per vehicle**
  - ANU Airport to English Harbour / Falmouth Harbour: approximately **USD $35–$50 per vehicle**
  - ANU Airport to Jolly Harbour / west coast: approximately **USD $30–$45 per vehicle**
- Fares are typically quoted and settled in USD. Taxis hold 3–4 passengers comfortably; for two people, a taxi is the most convenient option.
- Fares increase after 6:00 PM and on Sundays and public holidays. Confirm this before departure.

**Resort transfers:**
- Many resorts (Sandals, Curtain Bluff, Galley Bay, Blue Waters) offer pre-arranged airport transfers — confirm at the time of booking. For a first arrival in Antigua, especially late at night, a pre-arranged transfer is worth the convenience.

**Note:** There is no public bus service to the airport. Rideshare apps do not operate meaningfully in Antigua. A taxi or pre-arranged transfer are your practical options on arrival.

### Getting Around Antigua

**CRITICAL — Drive on the LEFT:**
Antigua is a former British colony and drives on the **LEFT side of the road.** This applies everywhere on the island without exception. For Canadians accustomed to right-hand driving, this requires genuine adjustment — left-hand traffic is disorienting at first, particularly at roundabouts, right turns (which now cross traffic), and quiet rural roads where there is no other traffic to cue the correct lane. Allow a full day to acclimatize, drive slowly in the first hours, and pay heightened attention at every intersection. **This is one of the most important practical facts to understand before picking up a car in Antigua.** Do not dismiss it.

**Car Rental:**
- Car rental is available from international agencies (Avis, Budget, Hertz, Thrifty) and several well-regarded local operators at ANU airport and in St. John's.
- **A rental car is strongly recommended** for couples who want to explore the island beyond their resort — Antigua's beaches, restaurants, Nelson's Dockyard, Fig Tree Drive, and other attractions are spread across the island and there is no reliable tourist bus network connecting them.
- Rates: approximately **USD $50–$90/day** for a compact or small SUV in late April.
- A valid Canadian driver's licence is accepted, but Antigua also requires a **local temporary driving permit**, available from the rental agency at the time of pickup for approximately **USD $20**.
- Minimum age is typically 25 at most agencies; under-25 surcharges apply at some.
- **Road conditions:** Main roads are paved and generally good; secondary roads can be narrow, winding, and potholed — particularly in the interior and along the south coast near Fig Tree Drive. A small SUV handles these roads more comfortably than a compact car.
- **Keep left at all times.** Set a phone GPS to voice navigation so you can hear turn-by-turn guidance without looking at the screen — this reduces the cognitive load of adjusting to left-hand traffic.

**Taxis for day trips:**
- For couples who prefer not to rent a car, taxis can be hired by the half-day or full day. Negotiate the rate with the driver in advance — full-day island tours with a knowledgeable driver-guide run approximately **USD $100–$180** and are a practical, low-stress way to see English Harbour, Nelson's Dockyard, and the south coast without navigating left-hand traffic yourself.

**Water Taxis and Boat Charters:**
- A number of water taxis and day-charter operators run from Dickenson Bay, Jolly Harbour, and English Harbour to nearby cays, snorkelling sites, and uninhabited beaches.
- Day sails on traditional schooners or modern catamarans (lunch, snorkelling, open bar included) are one of the most popular excursions in Antigua. Operators include Wadadli Cats and others; rates approximately USD $100–$130/person for a full-day sail.

## Attractions

### 1. Nelson's Dockyard National Park (UNESCO World Heritage Site)
- **Hours:** Daily 8:00 AM – 6:00 PM (the dockyard and museum; the surrounding national park is always accessible)
- **Admission:** Approximately USD $8/person for the dockyard and museum
- **Description:** The most important historical site in Antigua and one of the most remarkable in the Eastern Caribbean. Nelson's Dockyard — named for Admiral Horatio Nelson, who was stationed here from 1784–1787 — is the only continuously operating Georgian-era dockyard in the world. The stone boathouses, sail lofts, capstan house, and officer's quarters that Nelson knew still stand around the sheltered harbour at English Harbour, many now converted into hotels, restaurants, and shops without losing their historical character. The **UNESCO World Heritage Site designation** (awarded in 2016) recognises the extraordinary integrity of the site. The surrounding national park encompasses Shirley Heights, the ruins of English Harbour's fortifications, and miles of walking trails through scrub and coastal forest. The Dockyard Museum tells the complete story of English Harbour from its establishment in the 18th century through its role in the Napoleonic Wars. The entire area is one of the finest examples of preserved colonial naval infrastructure anywhere in the world.
- **Tip:** Combine Nelson's Dockyard with a walk up to Shirley Heights (see below) in the same afternoon — the two sites are just minutes apart and together constitute a full half-day. Take your time in the dockyard itself; the architectural details reward close attention.

### 2. Shirley Heights Lookout and Sunday BBQ Sunset Party
- **Hours:** The lookout is accessible daily; the famous **Sunday BBQ runs every Sunday from approximately 4:00 PM to 10:00 PM** (steel pan music 4–7 PM; reggae band 7–10 PM)
- **Admission:** Approximately USD $15–$20/person for the Sunday event (includes the BBQ); the lookout itself is accessible for a smaller admission during the week
- **Description:** The most legendary social event in Antigua, and one of the most celebrated sunset parties in the Caribbean. Shirley Heights is a hilltop fortification above English Harbour with commanding views over Falmouth Harbour, English Harbour, and the entire southern tip of the island. Every Sunday afternoon, a barbecue party runs on the fortification's terrace as the sun descends over the anchorage below. Steel pan bands play as the light goes golden; a reggae band takes over after sunset. The crowd is a spirited mix of travellers, local families, yachties from the anchorage, and expats who have been coming for years. The BBQ (jerk chicken, ribs, pork, local sides) is hearty and well-priced; the rum punches are cold and strong; the views as the sun sets over Falmouth Harbour and the masts of a hundred anchored yachts are as good as it gets in Antigua. This is not a tourist performance — it is a genuine island institution that has been running for decades.
- **Tip:** Arrive by 5:00 PM to secure a spot with a clear view of the sunset horizon. It gets crowded after 5:30 PM. Arrange a return taxi in advance — the road down from Shirley Heights at night after several rum punches is not one to navigate on an unfamiliar left-hand road without planning ahead.

### 3. Fig Tree Drive
- **Hours:** Always accessible
- **Admission:** Free
- **Description:** A winding, scenic road through Antigua's lush interior, linking the southwest coast to the central highlands through a corridor of tropical vegetation unusual for an island as dry as Antigua is in most areas. Fig Tree Drive passes through banana plantations, mango trees (the "fig trees" of the name — "fig" is local Antiguan English for banana), enormous ficus, and thick secondary forest dripping with vines and bromeliads. The drive contrasts sharply with the dry scrub of the northern and eastern parts of the island. Several small stalls along the route sell fresh fruit, local jams, and honey — worth stopping for. The Old Road area at the western end connects to Curtain Bluff and the south coast beaches.
- **Tip:** Best as part of a half-day loop combining a southwest beach (Darkwood, Johnsons Point) with the drive back through the interior. Allow 30–45 minutes for the drive itself with stops.

### 4. Devil's Bridge
- **Hours:** Always accessible
- **Admission:** Free
- **Description:** A dramatic natural limestone arch on the northeast Atlantic coast of Antigua, carved by millennia of wave action. The sea surges through blowholes in the limestone shelf surrounding the arch, sending columns of spray into the air with each wave — a genuinely spectacular natural display, particularly on days with significant Atlantic swell. The site has a sobering historical resonance: the rocky cliff was used by enslaved Africans as a place of self-liberation — accounts record people throwing themselves into the sea here rather than endure continued bondage. A small interpretive marker acknowledges this history. The natural scenery — rugged Atlantic coastline, dramatic rock formations, rolling surf — is distinctly different from the Caribbean-side calm of most Antiguan beaches.
- **Tip:** Located near the Verandah Resort in the northeast. The surf can be dangerous — stay behind the barriers and do not approach the edge in rough conditions. Wear shoes with grip for the limestone walk.

### 5. Falmouth Harbour
- **Hours:** Always accessible
- **Admission:** Free
- **Description:** Adjacent to English Harbour and Nelson's Dockyard, Falmouth Harbour is Antigua's yachting hub and one of the great sailing anchorages of the Caribbean. During Antigua Sailing Week (late April to early May — timing overlaps with this travel window), the harbour is packed with racing yachts from around the world and the atmosphere is electric. Even outside regatta season, the harbour offers a compelling scene: waterfront restaurants, a well-equipped marina, yachts swinging at anchor from every nationality, and the social energy of a working maritime community. The restaurants and bars lining the harbour road (Abracadabra, the Antigua Yacht Club, and others) are among the island's best for both food and atmosphere.
- **Tip:** If your late April dates align with Antigua Sailing Week, this is a world-class event worth integrating into your itinerary — the racing, the parties, and the general spectacle make Falmouth and English Harbour genuinely extraordinary during that week. Check the Antigua Sailing Week website for exact dates.

### 6. Boggy Peak (Mount Obama)
- **Hours:** Accessible during daylight hours; no formal gate
- **Admission:** Free
- **Description:** The highest point in Antigua at 402 metres (1,319 feet), known historically as Boggy Peak and renamed Mount Obama in 2009 in honour of the US President's inauguration. A rough track leads from the road near the village of Urlings toward the summit, which is capped by telecommunications towers. The hike to the top is moderately strenuous (roughly 1.5–2 hours round trip on the main trail) and rewards with panoramic views of the entire island — on clear days, Montserrat, Guadeloupe, and the neighbouring islands are visible. The summit is cooler and often breezy; cloud can gather quickly. The vegetation along the trail shifts from dry scrub at the base to more lush growth near the top.
- **Tip:** Start early (before 8 AM) to beat the heat and maximise the chance of clear summit views before afternoon clouds develop. Wear proper shoes — the track is rocky and uneven. Bring water; there is none available on the trail. A local guide from the Urlings area can be arranged and adds context to the natural history.

### 7. St. John's Public Market and Heritage Quay
- **Hours:** Market — best Saturday morning from approximately 6:00 AM onwards; Heritage Quay — daily during cruise ship hours
- **Admission:** Free
- **Description:** St. John's is Antigua's capital and a genuinely lively small-city experience worth an hour or two of exploration. The **Public Market** near the waterfront is the best place to see everyday Antiguan life — vendors sell fresh produce (mangoes, soursop, coconuts, christophine, breadfruit), local spices, fresh fish, baked goods, and prepared food. Saturday morning is the liveliest session by far. **Heritage Quay**, the waterfront shopping district near the cruise pier, has duty-free shops, jewellers, and tourist outlets — it is purpose-built for cruise visitors but functional for picking up rum, hot sauce, and local crafts. The nearby **Redcliffe Quay** is a more charming, less commercial waterfront complex of restored colonial warehouse buildings housing restaurants, boutiques, and cafés — including Big Banana (see Dining). A walk through the old capital, including the **Cathedral of St. John the Divine** (with its distinctive twin towers visible from ships entering the harbour), rounds out a pleasant morning in town.
- **Tip:** Avoid St. John's on days when large cruise ships are in port — the waterfront Heritage Quay becomes very crowded and the shopping experience degrades. Check the cruise ship schedule (available at the St. John's Harbour website) and plan your capital visit for a day when ships are not expected.

## Beaches

### Dickenson Bay
Antigua's most developed and amenitised beach — a long, gently curving stretch of white sand on the northwest coast, sheltered and calm, with consistently clear, warm water. Dickenson Bay is backed by the island's largest concentration of beach bars, watersports operators, restaurants, and resort infrastructure, including Sandals Grande Antigua. Jet skis, banana boats, windsurfers, and kayaks are all available for hire directly from the beach. The water is shallow and calm enough for confident swimming; sea temperature in late April averages approximately **27°C (80°F)**. The beach is broad and wide enough that it rarely feels genuinely crowded despite its popularity. An ideal base for couples who want easy access to watersports and beachside dining without driving across the island.

### Half Moon Bay
One of the most beautiful beaches in Antigua — a wide, crescent-shaped bay on the Atlantic northeast coast with a striking combination of surf and calm zones. The bay is divided by a natural sandbar: the Atlantic side catches waves and is popular for bodysurfing; the sheltered southern end is calm and perfect for swimming. The sand is blindingly white and fine; the water an extraordinary deep turquoise against the Atlantic blue. Half Moon Bay has no permanent resort infrastructure — it is a national park beach, and the only facilities are minimal. Bring your own picnic, snorkel gear, and towels. The drive is approximately 30 minutes from Dickenson Bay and benefits from a rental car. One of Antigua's most rewarding beaches precisely because it requires a little effort to reach.

### Jolly Beach
The longest beach in Antigua at approximately 2 kilometres of gently shelving white sand on the west coast, adjacent to Jolly Harbour Marina. Jolly Beach is one of the island's more popular local beaches — visited by Antiguans as much as tourists — with a relaxed, unpretentious atmosphere, a few beach bars, and calm Caribbean-side water. The Jolly Harbour complex nearby means there is a marina, shops, and restaurants within a short walk. A good beach for a laid-back afternoon without resort crowds, with enough facilities (a beach bar, sun lounger rentals) to be comfortable without being over-managed. The sunset views across the open Caribbean from Jolly Beach are reliably excellent.

### Darkwood Beach
A quieter, more natural beach on the southwest coast with minimal development and a wild, unspoiled character unusual in a region where most beaches have resort infrastructure nearby. Darkwood is a wide bay of fine white sand with calm water and a reef not far offshore — snorkelling directly from the beach is rewarding. A single beach bar (Darkwood Beach Bar and Restaurant) provides cold drinks and simple food; otherwise the beach is refreshingly uncommercialised. Located close to Fig Tree Drive, making it a natural combination for a half-day southwest-coast excursion. One of the island's genuinely lovely beaches for couples who want to escape the resort corridor.

### Pigeon Point Beach
A small, scenic beach at the entrance to Falmouth Harbour, tucked between rocky headlands with views over the anchorage and the entrance to English Harbour National Park. The beach itself is intimate and not large, but the combination of clear, calm water, dramatic headland scenery, and proximity to the historical sites and restaurants of English Harbour make it one of the island's most satisfying beach experiences. It is the perfect place to decompress after a morning at Nelson's Dockyard — a short swim, a cold drink from the small beach bar, and the view of yachts moving in and out of the harbour. Not a beach for watersports infrastructure; purely for swimming and atmosphere.

## Practical Info

### April Weather

Late April is one of the best times of year to visit Antigua. The island sits at the southern fringe of the main Caribbean hurricane belt and has a drier climate than many of its neighbours. Late April falls in the final weeks of the dry season (*dry season runs approximately December–May*) — expect predominantly sunny days with low humidity by Caribbean standards and minimal rainfall. Trade winds from the northeast keep temperatures comfortable.

| Metric | Late April Average |
|---|---|
| Daytime high | 30°C (86°F) |
| Overnight low | 24°C (75°F) |
| Sea temperature | 27°C (80°F) |
| Humidity | ~65–70% (moderate; lower than the summer months) |
| Rainfall | ~40 mm over approximately 4 days |
| Sunshine | ~9 hours/day |
| UV Index | 11–12 — **Extreme** |

- Late April sees the end of the peak tourist season and the final stretch of the dry season. Rain when it falls is typically a brief shower rather than a prolonged downpour; the afternoon-then-clear pattern is typical.
- Trade winds (15–20 knots from the northeast) keep conditions comfortable even on the warmest days. The same winds make sailing conditions excellent — one reason Antigua Sailing Week is traditionally held in late April.
- **UV warning:** A UV Index of 11–12 is classified as extreme — unprotected skin can burn in as little as 10 minutes at midday. Apply SPF 50+ sunscreen liberally and reapply every 90 minutes. Wear a broad-brimmed hat and UV-blocking sunglasses on the beach.
- Hurricane season officially begins June 1; late April carries essentially no hurricane risk.

### Currency

Antigua's official currency is the **Eastern Caribbean Dollar (XCD)**, pegged at a fixed rate of **2.70 XCD = 1 USD**. However, the **US Dollar is widely and almost universally accepted** at hotels, resorts, restaurants, taxis, and most shops — many prices in tourist areas are quoted in USD. You do not need to acquire XCD in advance; USD cash and major credit cards (Visa, Mastercard) work throughout the island.

- Canadian dollars are **not widely accepted** in Antigua. Convert to USD before departing Canada (your bank or a currency exchange service before the airport); or simply rely on credit cards and withdraw USD from ATMs.
- **ATMs** are available at the airport, in St. John's, at Heritage Quay, and at some resorts. Machines typically dispense XCD; confirm whether your card will give you USD or XCD before selecting an amount. Canadian debit cards on the Cirrus/Plus network generally work; check with your bank regarding international withdrawal fees.
- Credit cards are accepted at essentially all resorts, restaurants in the English Harbour area, and larger shops. Smaller local restaurants and market vendors may be cash-only; carry some USD cash for these situations.
- As a rough guide, budget approximately **USD $80–$150 per couple per day** for food, drinks, activities, and transportation if not at an all-inclusive. At an all-inclusive property, most daily spending is pre-paid.

### Electricity

- **Voltage:** 110V, 60 Hz — **identical to Canada and the United States.**
- **Plugs:** Type A and B (the same two-flat-prong and three-prong plugs used throughout Canada and the US).
- **Canadian visitors need no power adapter or voltage converter whatsoever.** All Canadian electronics, phone chargers, laptop chargers, and standard appliances will work directly in Antiguan outlets without any modification. This is a genuine practical advantage over French Caribbean destinations (Martinique, Guadeloupe) where European 220V power and Type E sockets require adapters and sometimes voltage converters.

### Language

**English** is the official and universal language of Antigua. Antiguans speak English as their first language — a clear, Caribbean-accented English that Canadian visitors will have no difficulty understanding. There is no language barrier of any kind for English-speaking Canadians; menus, signage, conversations, and customer service are all conducted in English.

This is a meaningful differentiator from French Caribbean destinations: you will not need translation apps, phrase books, or translation effort of any kind. For Quebec francophone travellers, English is equally understood by all Antiguans.

### Canada Travel Advisory

As of early 2026, the Government of Canada rates Antigua and Barbuda at **"Exercise Normal Security Precautions"** — the lowest advisory level, equivalent to destinations considered safe for routine travel. This is the same level as the United States, United Kingdom, and most Western European countries. There are no specific crime, political instability, or health warnings noted for Antigua in the current advisory.

**Check travel.gc.ca** for the current advisory before departure, as advisory levels can change. In general, Antigua has a stable democracy and a longstanding reputation as a safe tourist destination.

### Health Notes

- **No mandatory vaccinations** are required for entry to Antigua for Canadian citizens.
- **Routine vaccinations** should be current: COVID-19, influenza, MMR, tetanus/diphtheria.
- **Hepatitis A** vaccination is recommended as a general travel precaution for any Caribbean destination.
- **Medical facilities:** Antigua has the Mount St. John's Medical Centre in St. John's — a full-service hospital that handles emergencies, surgery, and in-patient care. It is a reasonable facility by Caribbean standards. For complex trauma or serious illness, medical evacuation to Barbados, Trinidad, or the US (Miami, approximately 3.5 hours by air) may be necessary.
- **Travel medical insurance is essential.** Canadian provincial health plans (OHIP and equivalents) provide no meaningful coverage outside Canada. A comprehensive policy covering emergency medical treatment, hospitalization, and medical evacuation is strongly recommended. There is no legal requirement to carry proof of insurance for entry to Antigua, but not having coverage in a destination this far from Canada would be a serious financial risk.
- **Sun and heat:** The primary health risk for most visitors. Apply SPF 50+ sunscreen liberally and reapply every 90 minutes. Seek shade during peak UV hours (11 AM–2 PM). Wear a broad-brimmed hat. Stay hydrated.
- **Mosquitoes:** Present, though Antigua is drier than more heavily vegetated islands and mosquito density is relatively low outside wet areas. Standard precautions (DEET repellent at dusk and dawn) are sensible. Dengue is present at low levels — check the Public Health Agency of Canada's travel health notices before departure.
- **Water:** Tap water in Antigua is treated and meets safety standards at most resorts and in St. John's. Bottled water is widely available and preferred by most visitors. At local restaurants and rural areas, bottled water is the safest choice.
- **Sea urchins:** Present on rocky reef areas. Wear water shoes when entering the water over rocky substrate. Stepping on a black sea urchin is painful and a common tourist injury on Caribbean reef coastlines.

### Tipping Customs

Antigua follows broadly US-style tipping conventions, and tips are an important part of the income of hospitality workers:

| Situation | Suggested Tip |
|---|---|
| Restaurant (sit-down) | 10–15% (verify whether a service charge has already been added) |
| All-inclusive resort (meals included) | USD $1–$2 per drink round; USD $3–$5/day for housekeeping |
| Resort porter/bellhop | USD $2–$3 per bag |
| Taxi driver | 10–15% of fare |
| Tour guide / boat captain | USD $10–$20/person for a half or full-day excursion |
| Spa therapist | 15% |
| Bar staff (non-all-inclusive) | USD $1–$2 per round |

- **Check your bill first:** Many Antiguan restaurants and resorts automatically add a **10–15% service charge**. This is very common — always verify before adding an additional tip on top.
- At all-inclusive resorts, the tipping situation is simpler: a small daily cash tip to housekeeping and your bar/restaurant servers is appreciated but not compulsory. Gratuities are generally not required for included activities or dining venues at all-inclusive properties.
- Budget approximately **USD $100–$200 per couple** specifically for gratuities over a 5-night stay, depending on your property type.

### Entry Requirements

- **Passport:** A valid Canadian passport is required for entry to Antigua and Barbuda. No travel document other than a passport is accepted. The passport should be valid for the full duration of your stay; most travel advisors recommend at least 6 months validity beyond your return date as a general precaution.
- **Visa:** No visa is required for Canadian citizens for tourism visits to Antigua and Barbuda. Canada is on Antigua's visa-exempt list; you will be issued an entry stamp on arrival.
- **Return or onward ticket:** You must be able to demonstrate onward travel — a return ticket to Canada is standard and sufficient.
- **Length of stay:** Tourist entry is typically granted for up to 6 months, though the entry officer will determine the period at immigration. Ensure your return ticket is within this window.
- **No tourist card or advance registration** is required.
- **Customs:** The usual Canadian customs restrictions apply on return. Note that some food items and plant material cannot be brought back into Canada from the Caribbean — check the CBSA guidelines before packing any local produce or plants.
- **Time zone:** Antigua observes **Atlantic Standard Time (AST) — UTC−4** year-round, with no daylight saving time. In late April, Toronto is on Eastern Daylight Time (EDT — UTC−4). **There is no time difference between Antigua and Toronto in late April.** No jet lag adjustment required.

### 2SLGBTQI+ Travellers

Antigua's legal and social landscape for 2SLGBTQI+ travellers underwent a significant change in 2023, and it is important to understand the current situation accurately:

- **Legal status:** Antigua and Barbuda **decriminalized same-sex sexual relations in July 2023**, when the nation's High Court struck down the colonial-era buggery and serious indecency laws that had criminalized same-sex intimacy. This was a landmark ruling and a meaningful legal change. **Same-sex marriage is not legally recognized** in Antigua and Barbuda, and there are no anti-discrimination protections in law based on sexual orientation or gender identity.
- **Social context:** While the legal criminalization has been removed, **social attitudes remain conservative** across much of Antiguan society. Antigua is a predominantly Christian country with deep-rooted traditional values; public acceptance of same-sex relationships is not universal, and public displays of affection between same-sex couples outside resort environments may draw negative attention or discomfort in local communities, rural areas, markets, or religious contexts.
- **At resorts:** The major international resorts — Sandals (which updated its policy to welcome all couples in 2022), Galley Bay, Blue Waters, Curtain Bluff, and others — cater to an international clientele and are professional and welcoming to all couples. Same-sex couples are a presence in the resort and English Harbour yachting community. Within these environments, the day-to-day experience for same-sex couples is generally comfortable.
- **Outside the resort corridor:** Discretion is practically advisable in local communities, at the public market in St. John's, in churches, and in more rural areas. Most travellers report no incidents, but the gap between the legal change and the social reality is real.
- The **Canada travel advisory** as of early 2026 contains a note that sexual activity between persons of the same sex was recently decriminalized, and advises that social attitudes can be conservative. Read the current advisory at travel.gc.ca before departure.
- **Bottom line:** The July 2023 decriminalization is a genuine and meaningful step forward. For a beach-focused couples' vacation centred on resort life, English Harbour, and the water, Antigua offers a comfortable experience. The conservative social environment outside the international tourist sphere is a real consideration for travellers planning significant time in local communities.
