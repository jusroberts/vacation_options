## Hotels & Resorts

> 5-night stay (Mon Apr 20 – Fri Apr 25, 2026), 2 adults. Rates are USD × 1.43 = CAD (approx., Mar 2026). Late April is **peak season** — book well in advance. Note: **Sandals Emerald Bay is closed** (transitioning to Beaches Exuma; no reopening date confirmed as of Mar 2026). Hotel options are fewer here than in Nassau.

### Budget / Mid-Range

#### [Peace & Plenty Resort](https://www.peaceandplenty.com/) ⭐⭐⭐
- **Location:** Downtown George Town, on Elizabeth Harbour — walkable to shops, straw market, and yacht docks
- **Meal plan:** Room only (on-site restaurant Copia available; breakfast available for ~USD $5–15/person)
- **Who:** Couples and independent travelers who want a central, characterful base; historic property open since 1958
- **Price:** ~$268–$350 USD/night (~$383–$500 CAD/night) → **~$1,915–$2,500 CAD / 5 nights**
- **Amenities:** 32 rooms, outdoor pool, private Beach Club on Stocking Island accessible by free water shuttle, kayaking, paddleboarding, snorkeling, concierge, currency exchange, car rental desk
- **Notes:** Rooms are modest but well-located. Some guests note service inconsistencies; best choice if you want to be in the heart of Georgetown without renting a car. Rated 8.2/10 for couples on Booking.com.
- **URL:** [peaceandplenty.com](https://www.peaceandplenty.com/)

### Mid-Range

#### [Hideaways Exuma (Palm Bay Beach Club)](https://hideawayspalmbay.com) ⭐⭐⭐
- **Location:** Palm Bay Beach, 1 mile from George Town, 7 miles from GGT airport — on the Atlantic (windward) side
- **Meal plan:** Room only (on-site Splash Bar & Restaurant; free shuttle to George Town)
- **Who:** Couples and travelers wanting a beachfront setting with kitchenette options, a short drive from town
- **Price:** ~$230–$380 USD/night (~$329–$543 CAD/night) → **~$1,645–$2,715 CAD / 5 nights**
- **Amenities:** 70+ studios, 1BR & 2BR cottages/villas with full or partial kitchen; outdoor pool; beach bar; kayaks and paddle boats (complimentary); indoor tennis court; fitness centre; free parking; spa services; free shuttle to George Town; airport transfers available (surcharge)
- **Notes:** Windward-side beach is scenic but the water in front of the resort is very shallow (thigh-deep even far out) — not ideal for ocean swimming. Best to use the resort pool or take the shuttle to Stocking Island. April 17–20 blackout period for promotions.
- **URL:** [hideawayspalmbay.com](https://hideawayspalmbay.com)

## Beaches

### Beaches of Stocking Island
- **Access:** Water taxi from Government Dock or Augusta Bay in George Town (~15 min, ~$15–$20/person RT). Peace & Plenty Resort offers a free shuttle to their Beach Club for guests.
- A barrier island just offshore from George Town; mostly uninhabited; some of the finest beaches in the Bahamas. West-side beaches face the harbour (calmer), east-side beaches face the open Atlantic (more dramatic).
- **Chat 'N' Chill Beach:** The most visited spot; volleyball, stingrays, conch salad bar, open-air bar. Sunday pig roast is an institution. Snorkelers and divers enjoy the blue holes, coral gardens, and underwater caves nearby.
- **Starfish Beach:** Shallow, crystal-clear water with resident starfish visible in the sand. Best accessed via water taxi; Da Sand Bar on-site for snacks and drinks.
- **Hamburger Beach:** Secluded crescent bay; accessible only by rental boat or a hike + wade. Very quiet.
- No roads on Stocking Island; no stores. Bring everything you need. Three restaurants total (Chat 'N' Chill, Lumina Point, Saint Francis Resort).

### Coco Plum Beach (Cocoplum Beach)
- **Location:** North of George Town, ~10 min by car; Big D's Conch Shack is nearby.
- Stunning sandbar that extends dramatically at low tide — visit at low tide for best results. Great for shelling and finding sand dollars.
- Family-friendly; calm, shallow water. Limited facilities beyond Big D's nearby.

### Tropic of Cancer Beach (Pelican Beach)
- **Location:** Little Exuma, ~20 min south of George Town near Williams Town.
- One of the most beautiful beaches in the entire Exuma chain. Named for its position exactly on the Tropic of Cancer. Powdery white sand, vivid turquoise water.
- Excellent snorkeling right off the beach.
- Facilities: the nearby Tropic Breeze Beach Bar & Grill (run by a Cordon Bleu–trained chef). Very limited other facilities — bring water and snorkel gear.

### Jolly Hall Beach
- **Location:** Near George Town, walkable from downtown.
- Less crowded than Stocking Island; good for a quick beach fix without a water taxi. Bring your own gear — no facilities on-site.

### Forbes Hill Beach
- **Location:** South of George Town (~15 min drive).
- Secluded, calm waters ideal for swimming. Very few tourists. No facilities — bring everything you need.

## Attractions

### Swimming Pigs at Pig Beach (Big Major Cay)
- The Bahamas' single most famous attraction. A colony of free-roaming pigs (and piglets) live on Big Major Cay in the Exuma Cays and swim out to greet boats. An unforgettable experience.
- **Access:** Requires a boat tour; not reachable from the main island without chartering a boat. Most full-day boat tours from Great Exuma include Pig Beach as the headline stop.
- **Cost (from Great Exuma):**
  - Half-day open tour: ~**$160 USD/adult** (~$229 CAD) — 9am–12pm or 1pm–5pm; also visits Starfish Bay, a blue hole, iguanas
  - Full-day open tour: ~**$290–$350 USD/adult** (~$415–$500 CAD) — adds Thunderball Grotto and nurse sharks; lunch ~$30 extra
  - Private boat (up to 6): ~**$1,350–$3,800 USD** (~$1,930–$5,430 CAD), pickup and lunch included
- Book via: [Exuma Water Sports](https://exumawatersports.com/) · [Coastline Adventures Exuma](https://coastlineadventuresexuma.com/) · [Exuma Water Tours](https://www.exumawatertours.com/) · [Viator](https://www.viator.com/tours/New-Providence-Island/Exuma-Island-Hopping-and-Swimming-Pigs-Tour/d24115-16186P23)
- **Tips:** Pigs can be large and may nip; feed with open palm. Many tours add a 10% Bahamas VAT on top of listed prices + a 5% card fee with some operators. Book well in advance in April (peak season).

### Thunderball Grotto (Staniel Cay)
- An underwater cave at Staniel Cay made famous by the James Bond film *Thunderball* (1965) and *Never Say Never Again* (1983). A domed cavern lit by sunbeams from an overhead opening; spectacular snorkeling among tropical fish and coral.
- **Access:** Included in most full-day boat tours from Great Exuma (see above). Note Staniel Cay is ~80 km north of George Town — included in day tours, not a self-drive destination.
- **Cost:** Included in full-day tour pricing above (~$290–$350 USD/adult). Standalone visits require a charter.
- **Important:** Fins/flippers are no longer permitted inside the Grotto (Bahamas National Trust directive — protect the coral). Snorkel in without fins.
- Book via: [GetYourGuide – Thunderball Grotto](https://www.getyourguide.com/thunderball-grotto-l169438/) · [Exuma Water Sports](https://exumawatersports.com/tours-rates/)

### Nurse Sharks at Compass Cay
- Nurse sharks gather around the docks at Compass Cay Marina; you can swim and snorkel with them in shallow water. Completely harmless. An incredible wildlife experience.
- **Cost:** Included in many full-day tours; private island docking fee ~$15 USD/person (cash only) on top of tour price.

### Stingray Sandbar / Elizabeth Harbour Sandbar
- A shallow sandbar in Elizabeth Harbour where southern stingrays glide through ankle-to-knee-deep water. Can be visited via water taxi or short boat ride from George Town.
- Some tour operators and Chat 'N' Chill on Stocking Island offer stingray encounters.

### Exuma Cays Land and Sea Park
- A 456 sq km protected marine park — the world's first land and sea park — at the northern tip of the Exuma Cays. Some of the best snorkeling and diving in the Bahamas: untouched coral reefs, massive brain corals, sharks, rays, sea turtles.
- **Access:** By boat only, best done as part of a multi-day liveaboard or chartered boat trip. Some full-day tours from Great Exuma reach the southern edge.

### The Fish Fry (George Town)
- Technically a collection of casual waterfront shacks west of George Town, but it functions as a lively cultural attraction on Friday, Saturday, and Sunday evenings. Walk stall to stall sampling fresh conch salad, fried fish, lobster, and BBQ; live music on weekends. Free to enter; pay per dish.

## Community Tips

### Safety
- Great Exuma is considered **one of the safest destinations in the Bahamas** — effectively a small, rural community where everyone knows everyone. Crime targeting tourists is very rare.
- Minor theft (opportunistic) does occur — use common sense: don't leave valuables on the beach or in unlocked vehicles.
- The crime advisories for Nassau and Freeport do **not apply** to Exuma; the two contexts are not comparable.
- Safety risks here are overwhelmingly natural: boating mishaps, sun exposure, swimming in rough Atlantic surf on east-facing beaches.

### Money & Currency
- The **Bahamian dollar (BSD) is pegged 1:1 to the USD**; both currencies are accepted everywhere interchangeably. No need to exchange USD before or on arrival.
- **Bring cash (USD):** Taxis, small restaurants, Fish Fry stalls, and tour operators often prefer or require cash. Some remote spots are cash-only.
- Credit cards are accepted at resorts, many restaurants, and tour operators; some add a 5% card surcharge.
- ATMs: Royal Bank of Canada branch in George Town; Scotiabank ATM also available. Limited ATMs outside of George Town — carry enough cash if venturing further afield.
- Tipping: **15% standard**. Many restaurants auto-include gratuity — always check before adding extra.

### Practical Tips
- **Rent a car** — the island is approximately 50 km long with one main road. Without a car you are dependent on taxis (expensive) or constrained to your hotel. Strongly recommended for this destination.
- **Book tours well in advance** for swimming pigs, Thunderball Grotto, and boat charters. April is peak season; popular operators sell out weeks ahead.
- **Drive on the LEFT.** Streets have no stoplights and minimal signage. Navigation is mostly intuitive given the single main road.
- **April weather:** 27–29°C (80–84°F), sunny and warm, with a cooling easterly breeze. Low humidity. This is the tail end of peak season — near-perfect beach weather.
- **Medical:** There is a government clinic in George Town staffed by a resident doctor and nurse, plus a private clinic. For serious issues, patients are airlifted to Nassau (~45 min). **Travel insurance with medical evacuation coverage is strongly recommended.**
- **Electricity:** 120V / 60 Hz; Type A/B plugs (same as Canada/US — no adapter needed).
- **Water:** Drink bottled water. Tap water is generally safe for brushing teeth but bottled is standard for drinking.
- **Dining out:** Budget ~$40–80 USD/person/day if eating at local restaurants. Self-catering villas (Grand Isle, Hideaways) can reduce this significantly. Resort dining runs $50–120/person/day.
- **Stocking Island:** No shops or services on the island; bring sunscreen, water, and snorkel gear when taking the water taxi over.
- **High season ends late April:** Some seasonal restaurants and tour operators begin reducing hours after mid-April. Verify operating days before planning around a specific venue.
